Lower Bounds for Smooth Nonconvex Zeroth-Order Optimization: The Price of Determinism under Low Curvature Trace

Yuxing Peng, Zhiqing Tang, Weijia Jia

cs.LG Oct 20, 2026 · v4

TL;DR
Key deterministic lower bounds and a three-point upper bound are formalized in Lean 4 with Mathlib and checked by the kernel (Appendix J).
Abstract
Low curvature trace, an effective rank condition, is a common explanation for why randomized zeroth-order methods scale to language model fine-tuning. Can deterministic methods achieve the same savings? We study finding an $\varepsilon$-stationary point of an $L$-smooth nonconvex function $f$ on $\mathbb{R}^d$ from exact function values when, on the set where $f$ is below its initial value $f(0)$, the Hessian is dominated by a fixed matrix of trace at most $LE$. For an initial gap $Δ$, let $T=LΔ/\varepsilon^2$. We show that the Gaussian two-point method used in fine-tuning needs only $O(ET)$ calls if it accepts only the steps that decrease $f$, and we prove that every randomized method requires at least $Ω(ET)$ calls when $ET\le d$, so this cost is optimal up to constants. In that range, every deterministic method requires at least $\widetildeΩ(d\min\{T,\sqrt{ET}\})$ calls, and adaptive subspace search attains $O(d\min\{T,\sqrt{ET}\})$ calls. A deterministic method pays about $d$ calls for each direction it discovers, and the trace budget allows about $\sqrt{ET}$ directions. Determinism therefore multiplies the cost by a factor of order $d/(E+\sqrt{ET})$, up to logarithmic factors. Once $E$ exceeds $T$ by a logarithmic factor, deterministic methods require $Ω(dT)$ calls, just as they do without any trace budget. Our lower bounds hold for $C^\infty$ functions that satisfy the matrix bound on all of $\mathbb{R}^d$, even when each call also returns projections of the gradient, charged by their rank.
Problem

Low curvature trace is used to explain why randomized zeroth-order methods scale to language model fine-tuning. The question is whether deterministic methods can match this query complexity for finding ε-stationary points of L-smooth nonconvex functions whose Hessian is dominated by a matrix of trace at most LE.

Approach

The authors prove upper and lower bounds on the number of function-value queries. They analyze a descent-accepting Gaussian two-point method and adaptive subspace search. They also construct C^∞ hard instances that apply even when queries return rank-charged gradient projections. Theorem 3.2, the deterministic part of Corollary 3.3, and a three-point upper bound are formalized in Lean 4 (v4.19.0) with Mathlib and use no extra axioms.

Results

Randomized methods need Θ(ET) calls when ET ≤ d. Deterministic methods need Θ̃(d·min{T, √(ET)}) calls, so determinism costs a factor of about d/(E+√(ET)). Once E exceeds T by a logarithmic factor, deterministic methods require Ω(dT) calls.
