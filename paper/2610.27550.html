Lower Bounds for Smooth Nonconvex Zeroth-Order Optimization: The Price of Determinism under Low Curvature Trace

Yuxing Peng, Zhiqing Tang, Weijia Jia

cs.LG Oct 20, 2026 · v1

TL;DR
Deterministic lower/upper query-complexity bounds and the three-point upper bound are formalized in Lean 4 with Mathlib, provided as ancillary files.
Abstract
Low curvature trace, an effective rank condition, is a common explanation for why randomized zeroth-order methods scale to high-dimensional tasks such as language model fine-tuning. Can deterministic methods achieve the same savings? We show that they cannot. We study finding $\varepsilon$-stationary points of $L$-smooth nonconvex functions on $\mathbb{R}^d$ from exact function values, assuming that below the starting value the Hessian is dominated by a fixed matrix with trace at most $LE$; this is the effective rank condition used to explain zeroth-order fine-tuning. When $ET\le d$, the Gaussian two-point method that such fine-tuning uses needs only $O(ET)$ calls if it rejects steps that raise $f$, where $T=LΔ/\varepsilon^2$ is the iteration count of gradient descent from initial gap $Δ$, and we prove that every randomized method needs $Ω(ET)$ calls, so this cost is optimal up to constants. In contrast, every deterministic method needs $\widetildeΩ(d\min\{T,\sqrt{ET}\})$ calls, and adaptive subspace search attains $O(d\min\{T,\sqrt{ET}\})$: a deterministic method pays about $d$ calls per discovered direction, and the trace budget allows about $\sqrt{ET}$ directions. Determinism thus multiplies the cost by a factor of order $d/(E+\sqrt{ET})$ up to logarithmic factors, which grows linearly with $d$ for fixed $E$ and $T$; once $E$ exceeds $T$ by a logarithmic factor, deterministic methods need $Ω(dT)$ calls, as without any trace budget. Our lower bounds hold for $C^\infty$ functions with the matrix bound on all of $\mathbb{R}^d$, even when each call also returns gradient projections priced by rank.
Problem

Low curvature trace (an effective rank condition) explains why randomized zeroth-order methods scale to high dimensions, as in language model fine-tuning. The question is whether deterministic zeroth-order methods can achieve the same savings when finding ε-stationary points of smooth nonconvex functions.

Approach

The authors prove matching randomized bounds Θ(ET) using the Gaussian two-point method with an acceptance test, and quadratics with a hidden center for the lower bound. For deterministic methods they build a resisting-oracle adversary that hides directions behind scalar gates, combined with radial compression to control the trace budget. An adaptive restarted subspace search gives the matching deterministic upper bound. The deterministic bounds and the three-point upper bound are formalized in Lean 4 with Mathlib and checked by the kernel.

Results

Every deterministic method needs Ω̃(d·min{T,√(ET)}) calls, and subspace search attains this up to √log factors, while randomized methods need only Θ(ET). Determinism therefore costs a factor of order d/(E+√(ET)). Experiments on synthetic functions and GPT-2 curvature are consistent with this separation.
