TL;DR
Lean 4 formalization of Krachun's directed block construction giving lower bounds for k-th-power-difference-free sets, with certified exponents at k=4 and k=6.
Abstract
A Lean 4 proof of Krachun's directed (acyclic-block) lower-bound construction for sets of integers with no k-th-power difference, stated and proved for every exponent k >= 2. Let D_k(N) be the largest size of a subset of {1,...,N} no two of whose elements differ by a nonzero perfect k-th power. A pool for k is a nonempty list of blocks (m, sup, H) with pairwise coprime square-free moduli m >= 2, heights H >= 2, and sup a nonempty list of (vertex, rank) pairs with distinct vertices below m, ranks below H, and a strict rank drop along every ordered pair of vertices whose difference is a nonzero k-th-power residue mod m (so the induced digraph is acyclic). For every such pool, alpha k P <= liminf log D_k(N)/log N, where alpha k P = (sum ((k-1) log m + log t)/log H) / (1 + k sum log m / log H) and t >= 1 is the number of vertices of a block; and N^rho <= D_k(N) for all large N whenever rho < alpha k P. The theorem is instantiated at k = 4 with nine blocks, eight on the primes 5, 13, 29, 37, 53, 61, 101, 109 and one at the composite square-free modulus 51 = 3 * 17, and at k = 6 with nine blocks on the primes 7, 19, 31, 43, 67, 79, 103, 127, 139, giving the exponents 0.912145042700... and 0.950825559086..., each proved to exceed the corresponding Ruzsa transfer value ((3 + log 6/log 17)/4 = 0.908103119289... and (5 + log 6/log 13)/6 = 0.949759249243..., the latter being Ruzsa's own published Corollary at k = 6) and the decimal bounds 0.9121 and 0.9508, by exact comparisons of natural powers.

Problem

D_k(N) is the largest subset of {1,...,N} in which no two elements differ by a nonzero perfect k-th power. The goal is to machine-check lower-bound exponents for D_k(N) that improve on Ruzsa's transfer values.

Approach

Krachun's directed (acyclic-block) construction is stated and proved in Lean 4 for every k >= 2. A pool is a list of blocks with pairwise coprime square-free moduli, heights, and vertex rankings. The ranks must strictly drop along every k-th-power-residue difference, so the induced digraph is acyclic. Each such pool yields an exponent alpha with alpha <= liminf log D_k(N)/log N, and N^rho <= D_k(N) for large N whenever rho < alpha.

Results

Instantiated at k=4 with nine blocks (including the composite modulus 51) and at k=6 with nine prime blocks, the construction gives exponents 0.912145... and 0.950826.... Both are proved to exceed the Ruzsa transfer values 0.908103... and 0.949759... by exact comparisons of natural powers.
