Second-Order Least Squares as a Special Case of the Polynomial Maximization Method

Serhii Zabolotnii

stat.ME Jun 9, 2026 · v2 math.ST stat.CO
formalization probability
Read PDF arXiv abstract Code
TL;DR
Algebraic identities behind the SLS–PMM equivalence and efficiency formulas are checked with Lean 4 proofs released in the code supplement.
Abstract
For linear regression with i.i.d. homoskedastic errors, optimally weighted second-order least squares (SLS) is the degree-two polynomial maximization method (PMM) estimating equation: both select the same optimal combination of $e$ and $e^2-σ^2$, share one influence function, and attain the slope variance $c_2g_2/N$. The identity is one of estimating equations and stops where the optimal combination depends on the design. Within the polynomial moment class, degree three holds an efficiency reserve: on four right-skewed laws the cubic direction adds between $30\%$ and $50\%$ asymptotic efficiency, and under symmetric platykurtic errors it is the only informative direction. The reserve belongs to the degree-three span, which an efficient generalized method of moments estimator also attains. We give the asymptotic law of the feasible plug-in estimator, a rule for choosing the degree, Lean 4 checks of the algebra, and simulation evidence.
Problem

Second-order least squares (SLS) and the polynomial maximization method (PMM) are two semiparametric regression estimators that exploit higher-order residual moments. Their relationship had not been established, and neither had the efficiency available beyond degree-two moments.

Approach

The paper shows at the population level that optimally weighted SLS and degree-two PMM pick the same optimal combination of e and e^2−σ^2. They share one influence function and the slope variance c2·g2/N. Closed-form degree-three variance-reduction coefficients quantify an efficiency reserve. The algebra is checked in Lean 4, and Monte Carlo studies using R and the EstemPMM package compare OLS, PMM2, PMM3, SLS and GMM.

Results

On right-skewed laws the cubic direction adds 30–50% asymptotic efficiency. Under symmetric platykurtic (uniform) errors, SLS and GMM reduce to OLS while PMM3 attains relative efficiency up to 3.16. Under asymmetric errors, the PMM2–SLS per-dataset correlation rises to 0.9997 at n=1000.
