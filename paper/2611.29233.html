Unknotting number is strictly subadditive for the trefoil

Anne Dranowski, Yura Kabkov, Daniel Tubbenhauer

math.GT Nov 22, 2026 · v1
formalization verification topology
Read PDF arXiv abstract Code
TL;DR
The computational checks behind the unknotting-number bounds come with a Lean verification, available alongside the authors' data repository.
Abstract
A trefoil and a certain fourteen-crossing knot take four crossing changes to unknot separately and at most three together. We extend this example to infinitely many prime partners and show that the same partners give a saving for every nontrivial positive braid knot and, up to mirror, every nontrivial torus knot.
Problem

Unknotting number is not additive under connected sum. The question is whether the trefoil, and more generally torus knots and positive braid knots, have prime partner knots K with u(T#K) < u(T) + u(K).

Approach

The family P_m consists of two-bridge knots N([(104m+37)/104]), with signature opposite to that of the trefoil. Three explicit crossing changes unknot T#P_m; intermediate diagrams are identified by comparing triangulations of the knot exteriors. The lower bound u(P_m)=3 uses signature, plus Owens's surgery obstruction and Donaldson's theorem to obtain a lattice-embedding contradiction. Computational checks are replayable from published data, with a Lean verification also provided.

Results

For m=2, 4 and every m≥6, the P_m are pairwise distinct prime knots with u(P_m)=3 and u(T#P_m)≤3<4. This includes u(14a7385)=3. The same partners give a saving for every nontrivial positive braid knot and, up to mirror, every nontrivial torus knot.
