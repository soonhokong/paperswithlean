Problem

The step-duplicating recursor F(x,y,S(n)) → G(y,F(x,y,n)) terminates, yet no direct whole-term measure in a stated grammar can orient it, because every such measure ignores the copied argument y. The paper asks why this happens and how to characterize which observations of a term are enough to certify termination.

Approach

An observer q licenses a target P when P is constant on q's fibers. The paper uses this notion to prove an equivalence between operational inexpressibility and the existence of two indistinguishable worlds with different target values. It relates observer refinement to deterministic Blackwell comparison and licensing to statistical sufficiency. It also gives quantitative bounds: guessing-probability curves for repairs that use side symbols, and fiber chromatic numbers for set-valued certificate tasks. The general results and recursor instances are formalized in Lean 4 modules such as Meta/SchemaOperationalIncompleteness.lean and Meta/SchemaWitnessOrder.lean, building on a companion Lean artifact.

Results

Direct aggregation is proved operationally inexpressible at the step-argument dimension, and the counter observer is shown to license an orienting target outside the grammar. Further results: - The least refused weight is 1 − V_k(μ), and this curve is weakly decreasing and convex. - Natural-valued rankings in faithful realizations are shown to have infinitely many full-order classes. - Signature homomorphisms that are constant in the counter slot identify two terms that the counter projection separates.
