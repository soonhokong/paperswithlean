Short Proofs for the Minimum Number of Determined Circles

Stijn Cambie, Denis Paliy, Rafał Wrona

Abstract
We present short proofs for the main parts of the recently completed solution of Erdős Problem 506, which asks for the minimum number of distinct circles determined by a finite planar point set that is neither collinear nor concyclic. An inversion argument and a seven-point line lemma show that eight points determine at least $17$ circles, and an explicit configuration attains this bound. A single argument combining Melchior's inequality with Langer's incidence bound establishes the sharp formula for every $n\ge15$. The proofs give concise alternatives to longer arguments in the recent literature; the remaining finite cases are cited separately.
Problem

Erdős Problem 506 asks for the minimum number f(n) of distinct circles determined by n planar points that are neither all collinear nor all concyclic. The recently completed classification relies on long arguments, some of them computer-assisted.

Approach

For n=8, an inversion argument is combined with a seven-point lemma bounding three-point lines when no four points are collinear. For n≥15, Melchior's inequality and Langer's incidence bound give a single weighted-line inequality covering the whole range. The remaining finite cases are cited from other work. The full classification is formalized in Lean 4 in Wrona's credited repository, and Wang's Lean files formalize an arithmetic layer of a related proof.

Results

Short proofs are given that f(8)=17, attained by an explicit configuration, and that f(n)=1+C(n-1,2)-⌊(n-1)/2⌋ for every n≥15. The n=8 proof needs no computer-assisted classification of incidence types.
