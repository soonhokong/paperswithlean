The Golden Quadruple: Four Reals Whose Differences Form a Geometric Progression, Rigidity of the Golden Ratio, and a Bohr Almost Periodic Cosine Sum

Michał Przybylski

TL;DR
The combinatorial and algebraic core results (difference multiset, maximality, uniqueness of base, cosine-sum closed form) are formalized in Lean 4 with Mathlib, with axiom audit.
Abstract
Let $\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$. The set $\{0,\varphi,\varphi^2,\varphi^3\}$ is classical (Euclid, Elements XIII.8): by $\varphi^2=\varphi+1$ its six pairwise differences take the values $1,\varphi,\varphi^2,\varphi^3$, with multiplicities $1,2,2,1$. We write it as the golden quadruple $S=(F, F-a, F-b, F-a-b)$, $a=\varphi u$, $b=\varphi^2u$, $F>a+b$, and study its extremal and rigidity properties. No five distinct reals have all their positive differences in $\{u\varphi^n:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}$; this follows from a result of Lenstra (1977), and we give a self-contained proof. We give an elementary proof of uniqueness: every four-element set with this property is a translate of $u\varphi^m\{0,\varphi,\varphi^2,\varphi^3\}$ for some $m\in\mathbb{Z}$, and if all its differences lie in $\{u,\varphi u,\varphi^2u,\varphi^3u\}$ it is a translate of $S$. Among all $x>1$, only $x=\varphi$ admits a four-point set of reals whose set of distinct differences is $\{c,cx,cx^2,cx^3\}$. For the analogous quadruple with base $x\neq\varphi$ the failure is measured by two closure residues, which along Fibonacci quotients equal $\pm u/\mathrm{Fib}_n^2$ and $\pm \mathrm{Fib}_{n+1}u/\mathrm{Fib}_n^3$. The cosine sum $C_S(t)=\sum\cos(2πd t)$ over the six differences equals $8\cos^2(πa t)\cos^2(πb t)-2$; it is not periodic but is Bohr almost periodic, with explicit almost periods $τ_n=\mathrm{Fib}_n/u$, and we determine the two-term asymptotics $\sup_t|C_S(t+τ_n)-C_S(t)|=6\sqrt3 π\varphi^{-n}-2\sqrt3 π^3\varphi^{-3n}+O(\varphi^{-5n})$, with optimal constants; the inclusion length of the $\varepsilon$-almost periods is of order $1/\varepsilon$. For a scale $K>0$ the golden ladder $ν_n=K\varphi^{-n}$ realises the construction with differences $ν_{200},\dots,ν_{203}$; we evaluate it for $K=\sqrt{c^5/(Gh)}$. All proofs are elementary and self-contained.
Problem

The work studies four-point sets of reals whose distinct pairwise differences form a geometric progression, centered on the golden quadruple built from the golden ratio. It also analyzes the cosine sum over its six differences.

Approach

Elementary proofs show that no five reals have all differences in the golden orbit, that four-point sets with this property are unique up to translation and scaling, and that the golden ratio is the only base admitting such a set. Closure residues measure the failure for other bases. The cosine sum is shown to be Bohr almost periodic with explicit almost periods built from Fibonacci numbers. Core results are formalized in Lean 4 on Mathlib (v4.35.0), and an axiom audit confirms no sorry.

Results

The golden ratio is rigid: only x = φ admits the construction. The almost-period deviation has two-term asymptotics 6√3πφ^{-n} − 2√3π³φ^{-3n} with optimal constants. 27 audited Lean declarations use only the standard axioms.
