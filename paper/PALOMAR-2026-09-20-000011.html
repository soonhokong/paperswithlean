Abstract
The exact left-border probability is 2^(-pi(L)), and the microscopic non-vacancy probability satisfies 0 <= q_L/2^(-pi(L)) - 1 <= 2^(-pi(L)/256) for sufficiently large L. The results give conditional localization and uniqueness, stability under changes of mesoscopic cutoff, and a quantitative Poisson law for runs contained in a finite prefix. They yield the critical longest-run distribution and asymmetric almost-sure envelopes for all integer prefix lengths. The exact border identity needs no literature hypothesis. The asymptotic results use, as applicable, scalar Stein bounds, the prime number theorem, the uniform prime-divisor estimate of Laishram and Shorey, Shorey's square-product exclusion, and the divisor bound of Nicolas and Robin.

Problem

The work studies a probabilistic model whose microscopic boundary and non-vacancy probabilities depend on the prime-counting function pi(L). It targets exact border probabilities, localization, and the distribution of longest runs within finite prefixes.

Approach

The exact left-border probability 2^(-pi(L)) is proved without literature hypotheses. The asymptotic results rely, as applicable, on scalar Stein bounds, the prime number theorem, the Laishram–Shorey uniform prime-divisor estimate, Shorey's square-product exclusion, and the Nicolas–Robin divisor bound. The proofs are formalized in Lean and machine-checked by the Palomar registry.

Results

For sufficiently large L, the non-vacancy probability satisfies 0 <= q_L/2^(-pi(L)) - 1 <= 2^(-pi(L)/256). Further results give conditional localization and uniqueness, stability under changes of mesoscopic cutoff, a quantitative Poisson law for runs in a finite prefix, the critical longest-run distribution, and asymmetric almost-sure envelopes for all integer prefix lengths.
