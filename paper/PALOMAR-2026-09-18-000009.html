Abstract
A Lean formalization of a geometric strengthening/variant of Pablo Soberón's counterexample to the Grünbaum four-hyperplane mass-partition conjecture. The formalized result constructs a convex body K in R^4 with nonempty interior such that no four affine hyperplanes divide K into sixteen sign cells of equal four-dimensional volume. Equivalently, the obstructed mass can be chosen to be uniform Lebesgue volume on a convex body. The proof adapts Soberón's explicit cubic/quartic tensor obstruction and replaces the smooth positive density used in the source by a small Wulff-body perturbation of the Euclidean ball.

Problem

Grünbaum's conjecture asks whether any mass in R^4 can be split by four hyperplanes into sixteen cells of equal mass. Soberón gave a counterexample using a smooth positive density, which leaves open whether uniform volume on a convex body can also serve as a counterexample.

Approach

The work adapts Soberón's explicit cubic/quartic tensor obstruction. It replaces his smooth density with uniform Lebesgue volume on a small Wulff-body perturbation of the Euclidean ball. The argument is formalized in Lean.

Results

The formalization constructs a convex body K in R^4 with nonempty interior such that no four affine hyperplanes divide K into sixteen sign cells of equal four-dimensional volume. The Palomar registry machine-checked the proofs.
