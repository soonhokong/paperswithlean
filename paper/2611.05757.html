The most entangled states of four and seven qubits

Antony Lewis

quant-ph Nov 3, 2026 · v1

TL;DR
The four-qubit entropy maximum, its equality case, spectral corollaries, stability estimates and icosahedral-family computations are formalised in Lean 4 with Mathlib, using AI-generated proofs.
Abstract
Pure states of four and seven qubits cannot be maximally entangled across every balanced bipartition; we determine how entangled they can be on average. For four qubits we prove the conjecture of Higuchi and Sudbery: the mean von Neumann entropy of the three $2|2$ cuts is at most $1+\tfrac12\log_2 3$ bits, with equality exactly on the local-unitary orbits of their state $M_4$ and its complex conjugate. For seven qubits the mean purity of the 35 balanced $3|4$ cuts is at least $121/917$ and their mean entropy is at most $2.965356029\ldots$ bits, each with a unique optimiser up to local unitaries and qubit permutations. The two optimisers are distinct members of one icosahedral family, so minimum purity and maximum entropy select different states. The proofs bound moments of the marginal spectra by positive identities on several copies of the state, some of them spin flipped, and bound the entropy by a polynomial in those moments. The four-qubit maximum has an analytic proof; the seven-qubit proofs, and some of the four-qubit refinements below, are computer-assisted, with exact arithmetic and rigorous error bounds. We also prove the Gour–Wallach conjecture for Rényi entropies of all orders, with the maximiser changing from $M_4$ to cluster states at $q_*\approx1.588$, and obtain sharp Tsallis bounds, mixed-state bounds and quantitative stability.
Problem

Four- and seven-qubit pure states cannot be maximally entangled across every balanced bipartition. The question is how large the mean balanced-cut entropy can be, or how small the mean purity. For four qubits this is the Higuchi–Sudbery conjecture.

Approach

Moments of the marginal spectra are bounded by positive identities on several copies of the state, some of them spin flipped. The entropy is then bounded by a polynomial (cubic majorant) in those moments. The four-qubit maximum is proved analytically. The seven-qubit results use computer-assisted proofs with exact arithmetic and rigorous error bounds. Most four-qubit results, including the maximum, the equality case and the stability bounds, are formalised in Lean 4 with Mathlib in a companion repository.

Results

For four qubits, the mean 2|2 entropy is at most 1+½log₂3, with equality exactly on the local-unitary orbits of M₄ and its complex conjugate; the Gour–Wallach Rényi conjecture is proved, with the maximiser switching at q*≈1.588. For seven qubits, the mean 3|4 purity is at least 121/917 and the mean entropy is at most ≈2.965 bits. These two optima are attained by distinct states of one icosahedral family.
