Certifying total-variation contamination by betting on trimmed kernel witnesses

Hristo Inouzhe Valdes

TL;DR
The paper's numbered results are formalized in Lean 4, with a public repository mapping each result to its formal statement.
Abstract
Two laws share a common component after each discards a fraction $α$ of its mass, a trimming, exactly when their total variation is at most $α$, so the contamination level $α^\star = \mathrm{TV} (P,Q)$ is the share of each population without counterpart in the other. We bound it from below from two samples on any space with a bounded characteristic kernel. The kernel mean embeddings of all such trimmings of a law form a convex region, the regions of two laws first touch at $α^\star$, and in every direction a region reaches as far as a trimmed mean. The difference of their closest points, the witness, selects the trimming. We fit a ramp of the witness on part of the data and bet on the rest, at each level of a grid. The bound is valid at every sample size. The cut points of the tested level carry most of its finite-sample gain over an untrimmed witness, while refitting the witness at that level makes it consistent, gives its rates and returns the trimming. When a function of the kernel's Hilbert space separates the parts of the two laws without counterpart, the bound is within order $(n\wedge m)^{-1/2}$ of $α^\star$ on a grid of that mesh, which is minimax over such pairs. When these parts touch, a margin condition guarantees a polynomial rate for Sobolev kernels and a logarithmic rate for the Gaussian kernel. For the Laplace kernel on the line this guarantee is not sharp: the witness stays between its two cut points, and for densities that cross once, with a likelihood ratio bounded away from one off the crossing, the error on such a grid is again of that order up to logarithmic factors. No informative upper bound exists without assumptions. On the Right Heart Catheterization data, with confidence $0.95$, each arm must lose more than a fraction $0.35$ of its patients, if both lose the same fraction, before their covariate distributions can coincide.
Problem

The contamination level, the total-variation distance between two laws, is the smallest fraction each population must lose before the two can be trimmed into a common law. The goal is to certify lower confidence bounds on this level from two samples, with guarantees that hold at every sample size.

Approach

The kernel mean embeddings of all α-trimmings of a law form a convex region, and the regions of two laws first touch exactly at the contamination level. The difference of their closest points, the witness, selects the trimming. A ramp of the witness, with cut points, is fitted on part of the data, and a betting test is run on the held-out data at each level of a grid. The numbered results are formalized in Lean 4.

Results

The bound is valid at every sample size. It is minimax within order (n∧m)^{-1/2} of the contamination level when an RKHS function separates the contaminations, and has polynomial or logarithmic rates under a margin condition. On the Right Heart Catheterization data, with confidence 0.95, each arm must lose more than 35% of its patients before the covariate distributions can coincide.
