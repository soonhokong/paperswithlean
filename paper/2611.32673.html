Unbounded GHZ dimension from fixed-order multiphoton sources

Heng Li, Xizhi Liu

quant-ph Nov 24, 2026 · v1 math.CO
formalization combinatorics quantum
Read PDF arXiv abstract Code
TL;DR
Lean checks certificates proving the unrestricted nine-path upper bound (ruling out dimension 14), and a Lean 4 project formalizes related results.
Abstract
We study the local dimension of Greenberger–Horne–Zeilinger (GHZ) states obtained by selecting events with one photon per output path. Each coherent source emits photons with a fixed internal label into a distinct set of paths, and the sets for each label partition the outputs. For two-photon sources, the maximum local dimension is 2 for every even number of paths greater than 4. However, we show that three-photon sources can produce GHZ states of arbitrarily large local dimension. To prove this, we view the three paths fed by each source as an edge of a $3$-uniform hypergraph, so that accepted emission patterns correspond exactly to perfect matchings. We construct such hypergraphs with arbitrarily many edge-disjoint perfect matchings, one per label, and no others. The construction extends to $r$-uniform hypergraphs for every fixed $r\ge3$, yielding a GHZ dimension that grows at least polynomially as the number of paths ranges over the positive multiples of $r$.
Problem

Krenn et al. asked whether three-photon sources can produce GHZ states of arbitrarily large local dimension under postselection on one photon per output path. This is equivalent to finding r-uniform hypergraphs with many edge-disjoint perfect matchings and no other perfect matchings (d-rigid hypergraphs).

Approach

Each source is modeled as a hyperedge, so accepted emission patterns correspond to perfect matchings. Balanced r-partite rigid hypergraphs are built by encoding bipartite cycle covers and assigning third-part vertices through anchor and translation vectors. Upper bounds come from a partial-exchange argument on perfect matchings. Small cases are verified by computer: Lean checks certificates for the nine-path bound, and two independent C++ programs give a further check.

Results

For every fixed r≥3, the GHZ dimension grows at least as c_r n^{2(r-2)/7}, compared with a maximum of 2 for two-photon sources on n≥6 paths. A matching upper bound of order n^{r-1}/log n is proved. The nine-path maxima are determined exactly, with the unrestricted upper bound certified in Lean, and a balanced tripartite example attains dimension 12 on 39 paths.
