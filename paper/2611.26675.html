Uniformising random greedy maximal independent sets on graphs with at most five vertices

John Fairfax-Ball

math.PR Nov 19, 2026 · v1 math.CO

TL;DR
The full classification of EGU graphs on at most five vertices, including the real exponential-clock law and the P5 obstruction, is formalized in Lean 4.
Abstract
A random greedy algorithm scans the vertices of a finite graph in increasing order of independent exponential arrival times and accepts each vertex unless an accepted neighbour blocks it. We ask an inverse question: can strictly positive finite arrival rates be chosen so that the terminal inclusion-maximal independent sets are all equally likely? We call this exact property exponential greedy uniformisability (EGU). We establish the complete small-order classification: every finite simple graph on at most five vertices is EGU, with the unique exception of the five-vertex path $P_5$ up to graph isomorphism. A strict $2\times2$ probability minor provides an explicit obstruction for the exceptional graph; the positive cases are certified by graph-isomorphism reduction and exact constructive witnesses. We explain the independent-exponential-clock probability law and the formal Lean 4 verification, registered as Palomar PALOMAR-2026-11-19-000021 (version 1). The registration is a formal proof record, not a claim of novelty certification or journal peer review.
Problem

A random greedy algorithm accepts vertices in order of independent exponential arrival times unless an accepted neighbour blocks them. The question is whether strictly positive rates can be chosen so that every maximal independent set is output with equal probability, a property called exponential greedy uniformisability (EGU).

Approach

A first-choice recursion for output probabilities is derived from the exponential-race identity and linked formally to the genuine independent Exp clocks. Positive cases use graph-isomorphism reduction and explicit constructive rate witnesses. The exceptional graph P5 is excluded by a strict 2x2 probability minor inequality, proved through a polynomial with positive coefficients. All parts are checked in Lean 4, and the kernel replay is verified independently by NanoDa.

Results

Every simple graph on at most five vertices is EGU except the five-vertex path P5, up to isomorphism. The Lean proof uses only the standard axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound, and is registered as PALOMAR-2026-11-19-000021.
