Lean formalization, machine-checked by the Palomar registry, of a countable domain that is left PCI and a left V-ring but neither right PCI nor a right V-ring.
Abstract
A countable domain R that is a left PCI ring (every cyclic left module not isomorphic to R is injective) and a left V-ring (every simple left module is injective), but is not right Ore, not a right PCI ring and not a right V-ring. Consequently the opposite ring of R is a right PCI ring that is not left PCI. This answers in the negative the question of Faith (1973), Cozzens and Faith (Simple Noetherian Rings, 1975) and Damiano (1979) whether every right PCI ring is left PCI, and the question, going back to Cozzens and Faith (1975), whether a left V-domain must be a right V-domain. The ring is an Ore extension K[t; σ, δ] over a free field K (Cohn's universal field of fractions of a free algebra over the rationals) with a non-surjective endomorphism σ, built by iteratively adjoining free solutions of the pseudo-linear equations that govern divisibility of the cyclic modules R/Rf. The mathematics, the accompanying paper and the Lean code were produced by AI models (Anthropic's Claude) at a human's direction; no human expert has checked the mathematics.

Problem

Faith (1973), Cozzens and Faith (1975) and Damiano (1979) asked whether every right PCI ring is left PCI. Cozzens and Faith (1975) also asked whether a left V-domain must be a right V-domain.

Approach

The ring is built as an Ore extension K[t; σ, δ], where K is a free field (Cohn's universal field of fractions of a free algebra over the rationals) and σ is a non-surjective endomorphism. Free solutions of the pseudo-linear equations governing divisibility of the cyclic modules R/Rf are adjoined iteratively. The mathematics, the accompanying paper and the Lean code were produced by Claude at a human's direction.

Results

The ring R is a countable domain that is left PCI and a left V-ring but not right Ore, not right PCI and not a right V-ring, so its opposite ring is right PCI but not left PCI. This answers both questions in the negative. The Lean proofs were machine-checked, but no human expert has checked the mathematics.
