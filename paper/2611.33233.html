The smallest vertex triangle in a fixed polygon

Dawid Trela

math.MG Nov 24, 2026 · v1 math.CO

TL;DR
A Lean 4 project formalizes the unit-square upper bound 32/n^3 and its attainment at n=4k, plus finite estimates and the numerical witnesses.
Abstract
Among the triangles determined by $n$ points in convex position in the unit square, one has area at most $32/n^3$. Equality is possible exactly when $4$ divides $n$, and then the maximizing point set is unique. We prove a fixed-container theorem for a class of convex $s$-gons that includes affinely regular polygons and a one-parameter family of pairwise affinely inequivalent hexagons without a centre of symmetry. If the container has equal ears of area $a$ and the tips of its directed side vectors are in convex position, the sharp bound at $n=sk$ is $a/k^3$. Its equality conditions determine every side vector of the maximizing polygon. The proof uses the area enclosed by the normalized side directions. We compare the result with the area-normalized smallest-ear theorem of Gronchi and Longinetti and give consequences for unrestricted Heilbronn configurations with 13 and 15 points.
Problem

The paper studies the largest possible minimum triangle area among n points in convex position inside a fixed convex container, a convex-position variant of the Heilbronn triangle problem. The central case is the unit square, where the conjectured sharp bound is 32/n^3.

Approach

The bound comes from a gauge-length 'perimeter' and the area of the hull of normalized side directions, combined with an AM-GM product estimate. The method extends to convex s-gons with equal ears whose directed side vectors have tips in convex position. Equality conditions force every side vector of a maximizer, and explicit point placements show the bound is attained. A Lean 4 project, developed with ChatGPT (Codex), formalizes the square upper bound and its attainment for every 4k within a model of counterclockwise polygonal boundaries. It also checks the finite product estimate, the subdivision identities, the hexagon-family hypotheses and the grid witnesses.

Results

In the unit square, C_n(Q) ≤ 32/n^3, with equality exactly when 4 divides n and a unique maximizer. For equal-ear containers, C_{sk}(K)=a/k^3 with a unique maximizer. As consequences, optimal unrestricted Heilbronn configurations have at most 10 points in convex position for n=13 and at most 11 for n=15.
