Problem

The Kung–Traub conjecture states that a deterministic stationary root-finding method without memory, using n scalar function or derivative evaluations per step, has convergence order at most 2^(n-1). A formal, machine-checked treatment of this bound is sought.

Approach

The bound 2^(n-1) is formalized for n >= 1 real or complex scalar derivative observations. A generalized bound is proved for prescribed positive observation groups: the first group size times the product of one plus each subsequent group size. Entire-function counterexamples are constructed, including a single real witness that defeats every larger real exponent at once. A concrete inverse Hermite interpolation method is built to show the bound is attained.

Results

The Kung–Traub bound and its grouped generalization are proved, together with counterexamples showing larger exponents fail. The inverse Hermite method attains order 2^(n-1) for n >= 2 on open real intervals and has exactly that order on exp(x)-1. The registry machine-checked the proofs.
