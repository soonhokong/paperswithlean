Gradient Descent Achieves $\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-7/4})$ Convergence

Chang He, Xiaotian Jiang

TL;DR

Abstract
We propose a deterministic stepsize rule for gradient descent on a lower-bounded nonconvex function with Lipschitz-continuous gradient and Hessian. Classical analysis shows that a fixed stepsize guarantees a decrease of order $\varepsilon^2/L$ per update. In contrast, our rule uses a sequence of $\mathcal{O}(m)$ updates with a total stepsize of order $m^2/L$. We establish a weighted descent inequality for every prefix of the update sequence on a quadratic objective and control the error caused by Hessian variation on a general objective. This error determines the choice $m\asymp(L^2/(ρ\varepsilon))^{1/4}$ for small $\varepsilon$. We show that gradient descent with our stepsize rule finds an $\varepsilon$-stationary point within $\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-7/4})$ gradient evaluations, matching the optimal gradient-complexity guarantee.
Problem

Gradient descent with a fixed stepsize needs O(ε^{-2}) gradient evaluations to find an ε-stationary point of a nonconvex function with Lipschitz gradient. The question is whether choosing scalar stepsizes alone can reach the optimal O(ε^{-7/4}) complexity when the Hessian is also Lipschitz.

Approach

A recursive stepsize table is built that accelerates gradient descent on quadratics, with a weighted descent inequality holding for every prefix. Smaller copies of the table are inserted around large coefficients to limit error amplification. Stepsizes are clipped using observed gradients to bound path length per epoch, and an adaptive threshold-halving test handles failed epochs. The results are accompanied by a Lean 4 verification.

Results

Gradient descent with the proposed rule finds an ε-stationary point within O(ε^{-7/4}) gradient evaluations, matching the optimal gradient-complexity guarantee.
