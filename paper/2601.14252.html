Problem

The paper asks whether removing nominal types or inheritance from a language actually reduces maintenance burden. It models type identification as an information-theoretic problem: classifying entities using stored tags and/or attribute (interface) queries.

Approach

Entity identification is cast in the Ahlswede–Dueck sense, with a tradeoff among tag length L, witness (query) cost W, and distortion D. The paper proves an information barrier for attribute-only observation, the optimality of nominal tags, and a matroid structure on minimal sufficient query sets. The theory is then instantiated in Python projects and other domains such as biological taxonomy. The proofs are formalized in Lean 4.

Results

Nominal tags of ceil(log2 k) bits give the unique Pareto-optimal zero-error point with O(1) witness cost, while interface-only observation needs Ω(d) queries and incurs positive distortion. In 20 multiple-inheritance Python projects, only multiple parents eliminate the outside implementation pairs (12,880 without inheritance versus zero with multiple parents). The Lean development comprises 6,086 lines and 265 theorems.
