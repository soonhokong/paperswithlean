Stokes corners and the global structure of steady rotating waves in annular domains

Quan Wang

TL;DR
Polynomial identities, factorizations and inequalities in a key algebraic step are machine-checked in Lean 4 (file AnnularStokes.lean); the analytic results remain unformalized.
Abstract
We study steady rotating water waves in a planar annulus, in the model of radial gravity and constant vorticity introduced by Li and the author. The stream function is harmonic, and the free surface carries the Bernoulli condition $p_0^2|\nablaΨ|^2=Q_α(S)$ with $Q_α(R)=(R-r_-)(R-r_+)$. We first settle the crest shape. For $α\neqα_\pm$, every stagnation point lies on the circle $R=r_-$, and the surface forms a symmetric $120^\circ$ corner there. At $α=α_\pm$ the corner is a right angle. The same holds for limits of smooth solutions and, in the lower region, for overhanging waves. We then study the global bifurcation continuum. Its waves are monotone from crest to trough, and they always cross the laminar circle. Hence there is no secondary bifurcation, and the parameter stays above an explicit floor. The continuum ends in an extreme wave, overturns, or degenerates; the last case is excluded for $c_0>γ^2e^{2γ}/(1+γ)^2$. An annular Nekrasov equation and an invariant Keady–Norbury cone give a slope bound below $90^\circ$ whenever the Nekrasov weight is nonnegative on the waves. A sharpened angular flow-force estimate keeps the troughs where this weight is nonnegative, for every $γ\in(0,1)$ above the floor. Hence, whenever $c_0>γ^2e^{2γ}/(1+γ)^2$, the existence half of the Stokes conjecture holds: the continuum contains an extreme wave with a $120^\circ$ Stokes crest. In the remaining band $\sinh^2γ<c_0\leγ^2e^{2γ}/(1+γ)^2$ the result is conditional: the continuum ends in such a wave unless its parameter degenerates to the value at which the effective gravity vanishes on the stagnation circle, and we cannot exclude this degeneration. We also show that the bifurcation is supercritical for every $γ\in(0,1)$.
Problem

The Stokes conjecture concerns the existence of an extreme wave and its 120° crest. The question here is steady rotating water waves in a planar annulus with radial gravity and constant vorticity. Both the crest shape and the existence of an extreme wave along the global bifurcation continuum are addressed.

Approach

Stagnation points are localized by comparison with circular flows, and crests are classified using a perturbed Weiss monotonicity formula and blow-up analysis. The global continuum is studied through monotonicity of the waves, a laminar crossing invariant and parameter bounds. An annular Nekrasov equation with an invariant Keady–Norbury cone gives a slope bound. Some algebraic steps are independently checked in Lean 4, and the numerics are evidence only.

Results

For α≠α±, every stagnation crest forms a symmetric 120° corner; at α=α± the corner is a right angle. Whenever c0>γ²e^{2γ}/(1+γ)², the continuum contains an extreme wave with a 120° Stokes crest. In the remaining parameter band the result is conditional, and the bifurcation is supercritical for every γ∈(0,1).
