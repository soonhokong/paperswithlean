Gluons and Knots

Alfredo Guevara

TL;DR
The state-sum formula for the single-minus gluon amplitude is proved in a Lean 4 library structured as a formalized dependency graph of assumptions.
Abstract
We argue that the scattering amplitude of a single minus helicity gluon with arbitrarily many positive helicity gluons is topological. The amplitude is constant in chambers of kinematic space, its resonances are located at walls and its factorization is determined by wall-crossing. Yangian invariance dictates the amplitude to be determined by a 2d null Wilson loop that can be lifted into a positive and transverse 3d link. By reinterpreting wall-crossing and the Weinberg soft theorem as skein relations and Reidemeister moves of the subknots, we construct a state-counting description consisting of adding the `corners' of their HOMFLY-PT polynomials. Beyond the classical limit, we argue that this provides a conjectured but precise connection to topological field and string theory. The new formula is rigorously derived via a Lean-formalized dependency graph (DAG) as detailed in a companion paper, laying out the precise assumptions including Reidemeister III invariance. It is further tested exhaustively, including novel `star' kinematics where the amplitude and state count are shown to become Catalan numbers, in agreement with knot theory literature.
Problem

The tree-level scattering amplitude of one minus-helicity gluon with many plus-helicity gluons is piecewise constant in kinematic space. The goal is a topological description of this amplitude and of its wall-crossing factorization.

Approach

The amplitude is written as a 2d null Wilson loop polygon and described through chirotope coordinates. Wall-crossing laws and the Weinberg soft theorem are shown to fix the amplitude uniquely in every chamber, starting from the triangle. A state sum over decompositions of the polygon's positive 3d knot lift is built from corner coefficients of HOMFLY-PT polynomials, and the wall laws are matched to skein relations and Reidemeister moves. The derivation is formalized in a Lean 4 library as a dependency graph that makes the assumptions explicit, including Reidemeister III invariance.

Results

The knot state sum reproduces the amplitude in every chamber, with Lean-verified proofs in a companion repository. For minimal 'star' kinematics, the amplitude equals (-1)^r Cat_r, in agreement with the knot theory literature.
