Trotter Error Bounds in Lean 4 with a Cancellation-Aware Analysis of the Fourth-Order Suzuki Formula

Bingyu Xia, Jue Xu, Yue Wang

quant-ph Nov 20, 2026 · v1
formalization verification quantum mathlib
Read PDF arXiv abstract Code
TL;DR
Formalizes the Childs et al. commutator-scaling Trotter error bound and fourth-order Suzuki formula estimates in Lean 4 with Mathlib.
Abstract
We formalize the commutator-scaling bound of Childs et al. in Lean 4. For skew-adjoint generators, the local order condition gives an explicit integer step count sufficient to meet a prescribed error tolerance. We apply the theorem in Lean to the binary fourth-order Suzuki formula. For this formula we can also account for cancellation within and between steps. Using local remainder estimates of Casas and Murua, we sum the signed quintic and centered seventh-degree error terms after conjugating each to the interaction picture. For every positive integer step count, we bound the difference between this sum and the global error operator. At fixed time the explicit remainder is eighth order in the step size, and the integral of the conjugated quintic term gives the exact leading error coefficient. The Lean proofs cover the two-generator finite-sum estimates, including the spectral norm for complex matrices. On five ten-site nearest-neighbor Heisenberg instances at time $10$ and tolerance $10^{-3}$, the transported sum meets the tolerance at a mean of $129.4$ steps. Taking a separate norm at each step with the same polynomial and remainder gives a mean first crossing between $184.6$ and $185.2$ steps. The accumulated Casas–Murua local bound at $q=8$ gives a mean of $184.8$ steps.
Problem

Bounds on product-formula (Trotter/Suzuki) errors determine how many steps a Hamiltonian simulation needs. Standard commutator-scaling bounds discard sign cancellations within a step and cancellations between steps, so they overestimate the step count.

Approach

The commutator-scaling bound of Childs et al. is formalized in Lean 4 (Mathlib v4.36.0) for staged products with skew-adjoint generators. From it the authors derive an explicit sufficient integer step count. The theorem is then applied to the binary fourth-order Suzuki formula, where the exact quintic error polynomial L5 and a centered seventh-degree correction N7 are computed. A centered local-to-global propagation lemma sums these terms after conjugating them to the interaction picture, using Casas–Murua local remainder estimates. The proofs cover finite-sum estimates in the spectral norm for complex matrices.

Results

For every positive step count, the gap between the transported sum and the global error has an explicit bound of eighth order in the step size, and the quintic term gives the exact leading error coefficient. On five ten-site Heisenberg instances (t=10, tolerance 1e-3), the transported estimate needs 129.4 steps on average. This compares with 184.8 for the accumulated Casas–Murua local bound, 585.4 for Childs et al. Proposition J.1, and 123.8 for the empirical crossing.
