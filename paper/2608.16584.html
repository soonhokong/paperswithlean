Polynomial growth of complex polynomial Bohnenblust–Hille constants

Daniel M. Pellegrino, Eduardo V. Teixeira

Abstract
For complex \(m\)-homogeneous polynomials, let \(D_m\) denote the optimal dimension-free constant in the polynomial Bohnenblust–Hille inequality. We prove that, for every \(B>1/2\), there exists \(K_B>0\) such that \[ D_m\le K_B m^B,\qquad m\ge1. \] More precisely, we obtain the square-root-scale estimate \[ D_m\le \sqrt m\,\exp\!\bigl(C\sqrt{\log m}\,\log\log m\bigr). \] The proof combines coefficient-preserving degree reduction with a weighted bootstrap, separating balanced and dominant degree profiles. We also determine the critical linear-dimensional asymptotics: \[ D_{m,n_m}\longrightarrow 2 \qquad\text{whenever}\qquad \frac{n_m}{m}\longrightarrow1, \] and consequently \(\liminf_{m\to\infty}D_m\ge2\). Further applications give two-sided bounds for homogeneous Sidon constants and a quantitative logarithmic remainder in the multidimensional Bohr-radius asymptotic.
Problem

The optimal dimension-free constant D_m in the complex polynomial Bohnenblust–Hille inequality for m-homogeneous polynomials was previously known only to be subexponential, exp(O(sqrt(m log m))). It was open whether a superpolynomial loss is necessary.

Approach

The proof combines coefficient-preserving degree reduction with a weighted square-sum bootstrap across homogeneous levels. It separates balanced and dominant bidegree profiles and uses two-block mixed-norm estimates built on Weissler's hypercontractivity. Lower bounds come from Blaschke-factor and Fejér-kernel constructions and from unitary-chain random polynomials. The results and proofs are stated to have been formalized in Lean 4 via the Lea system.

Results

The authors prove D_m ≤ K_B m^B for every B > 1/2, and the sharper estimate D_m ≤ sqrt(m) exp(C sqrt(log m) log log m). They also show D_{m,n_m} → 2 when n_m/m → 1, so liminf D_m ≥ 2. Applications give two-sided bounds for homogeneous Sidon constants and a logarithmic remainder in the multidimensional Bohr-radius asymptotic.
