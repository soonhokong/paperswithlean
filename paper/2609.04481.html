Statistical Theory in the Age of Machine-Assisted Mathematics

TL;DR
Formalizes five classical statistical theory results in Lean with Mathlib, all compiling sorry-free, to expose hidden assumptions in textbook proofs.
Abstract
The computational revolution is advancing at an unprecedented pace. The combination of proof-assistant technologies and generative AI tools has recently enabled the solution of complex problems in pure mathematics at a scale that seemed unattainable only a few years ago, but they have not yet become standard tools in the development of statistical theory. In this paper we do not present new theoretical results; instead, we discuss five case studies involving classical problems in statistics and describe how a machine proof checker changes the thought process behind their proofs, in order to stimulate reflection on how these technologies may transform theoretical research and advanced statistical education. Statistical theory compresses mathematical content into expressions such as “under the usual regularity conditions”; once AI writes the proofs and machines check them, the human reader is left with the statement and its hypotheses, which is exactly what such expressions compress. Against the fear that automation erodes skills, our experience was the opposite: what changes is the job of the theorist, which moves from the steps of a proof to its statement. The new paradigm can have positive effect also on methodologists and applied statisticians, because these tools allow for quick and precise check of formal guarantees necessary to justify the application of a specific model and/or methodology. We draw consequences for refereeing, doctoral teaching, and we encourage a collective effort toward shared formal libraries for statistics. Just as high-level programming languages revolutionized empirical research, machine-assisted formalization may introduce a new paradigm for the development, verification, and communication of statistical theory.
Problem

Statistical theory often hides assumptions behind phrases like "under the usual regularity conditions". Proof assistants and AI tools are not yet standard in developing statistical theory.

Approach

Five classical statistics results are formalized retrospectively in Lean with Mathlib, with AI assistance: Wald consistency, the score/Fisher information identity, MLE asymptotic normality, the delta method, and a LASSO bound. Each textbook argument is compared with its formal counterpart. Every assumption the formalization forces into the open is traced to its source. Results not formalized enter as named explicit hypotheses rather than admitted steps.

Results

All five files compile with no sorry or extra axioms, relying only on Lean's standard axioms. The gaps found sort into topological, analytic, probabilistic, and algebraic mechanisms, with recurring patterns such as domination/envelope conditions hidden behind limit theorems. The paper draws implications for refereeing and doctoral teaching and calls for shared formal libraries for statistics.
