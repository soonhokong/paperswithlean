A counterexample to the unrestricted form of Eden's conjecture

Matthew J. Colbrook

TL;DR
Lean formalization, machine-checked by the Palomar registry, of an explicit five-dimensional polynomial ODE counterexample to the unrestricted Eden conjecture.
Abstract
For every c > 4, an explicit polynomial ODE on Euclidean five-space has a compact global attractor and uniformly negative divergence. Its finite-time local Lyapunov dimension at every positive real time and its asymptotic Kaplan–Yorke dimension have maximum 4 + 4/c, attained precisely on an invariant two-torus with irrational angular flow. The maximum over equilibria and periodic points is 3. All singular-value exponents exist, and local dimensions converge pointwise. This disproves the unrestricted form of Eden's conjecture; the attractor is not assumed transitive.

Problem

Eden's conjecture predicts that the maximal local Lyapunov dimension of a global attractor is attained at an equilibrium or periodic point. The question addressed is whether this holds without assuming the attractor is transitive.

Approach

For each c > 4, an explicit polynomial ODE on Euclidean five-space is constructed with a compact global attractor and uniformly negative divergence. Its finite-time local Lyapunov dimensions and asymptotic Kaplan–Yorke dimension are analysed, including an invariant two-torus carrying irrational angular flow. The proofs are formalized in Lean and machine-checked by the Palomar registry.

Results

The maximal local and Kaplan–Yorke dimension is 4 + 4/c, attained exactly on the invariant torus, while the maximum over equilibria and periodic points is 3. All singular-value exponents exist and local dimensions converge pointwise, which disproves the unrestricted form of Eden's conjecture.
