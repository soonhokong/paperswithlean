Formal Conjectures: An Open and Evolving Benchmark for Verified Discovery in Mathematics

Moritz Firsching, Paul Lezeau, Salvatore Mercuri, Miklós Z. Horváth, Yaël Dillies, Calle Sönne, Eric Wieser, Fred Zhang, Thomas Hubert, Blaise Agüera y Arcas, Pushmeet Kohli

TL;DR
Builds an open benchmark of 7559 research-level problem statements formalized in Lean 4 with Mathlib, including open conjectures, with baseline prover evaluations.
Abstract
As automated reasoning systems advance rapidly, there is a growing need for research-level formal mathematical problems to accurately evaluate their capabilities. To address this, we present Formal Conjectures (https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures), an evolving benchmark of currently 7559 mathematical problem statements formalized in Lean 4. Sourced from areas of active mathematical research, the dataset features 1642 open research conjectures without known solutions for mathematical proof discovery, 2923 solved problems for proof auto-formalization, and 2994 supporting statements. Notably, the repository provides a structured interface connecting mathematicians who formalize and clarify problems with the AI systems and humans attempting to solve them. Demonstrating its immediate utility, the benchmark has already been leveraged for new mathematical discoveries, including the resolution of open research conjectures, and for the formal confirmation of major newly announced results. We describe our approach to ensuring the correctness of these formalizations in a collaborative open-source project where contributions stem from an active community. In this framework, AI-generated proofs and disproofs serve as a valuable auditing mechanism to iteratively improve the fidelity of the benchmark. Finally, we provide a standardized evaluation setup and report baseline results on frozen evaluation subsets, demonstrating a climbable signal that measures the current frontier of automated reasoning on research-level mathematics.
Problem

Existing formal math benchmarks have several weaknesses. They suffer from data leakage and saturation (e.g., MiniF2F above 99%), some depend on private evaluation sets, and some rely on simple final-answer checks that do not capture full proofs. Evaluating AI on research-level mathematics needs open, uncontaminated, rigorously verifiable problems.

Approach

Formal Conjectures is an open, community-maintained repository of mathematical statements formalized in Lean 4 using Mathlib. Problems come from sources such as Erdős Problems, Wikipedia, Green's open problems, open quantum problems, OEIS and MathOverflow. Statements are split into open conjectures for proof discovery, solved problems for proof auto-formalization, and supporting test, API and textbook statements. AI-generated proofs and disproofs are used to audit formalization fidelity, and frozen evaluation subsets provide reproducible comparisons. Missing definitions are upstreamed to Mathlib.

Results

The benchmark contains 7559 statements: 1642 open conjectures, 2923 solved problems and 2994 supporting statements. It has already been used to resolve some open conjectures. In baseline results, AlphaProof proves 45–50% of an evaluation subset and a DeepMind prover agent proves 66%. A misformalization analysis attributes most of the errors found to semantic translation issues and misrepresentation of the source problem.
