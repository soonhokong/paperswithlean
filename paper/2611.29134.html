Undecidable quantum storage: no algorithm can tell from the noise rate whether a fixed circuit remembers

Seth Douglas, Nidhal Mghirbi

quant-ph Nov 22, 2026 · v1 math-ph

Abstract
Below a threshold noise rate, error correction lets larger quantum memories store a qubit for longer, without limit. Can the noise rate alone tell us whether a given noisy circuit does this? We construct fixed local circuits for which no algorithm can, already in one dimension under single-qubit noise. Each circuit is translation-invariant and time-periodic, with depolarizing noise on every wire after every layer, including all classical control. A computable list of disjoint rate intervals is indexed by the inputs $n$ of a universal Turing machine; local noise statistics select the input that the circuit simulates, and halting triggers erasure. If the machine runs forever on $n$, a qubit is stored throughout the $n$-th interval at entanglement fidelity at least $15/16$ for a time that grows without bound with size: linearly on rings, exponentially in six dimensions. The encoding and decoding maps are noiseless and global; they depend on $n$, the system size and, for decoding, the time, but not on the rate. If it halts, the fidelity of every encoding falls below $1/2+η$, for any fixed $η>0$, after a time independent of size and rate. The labels of the intervals with unbounded storage form a $Π^0_1$-complete set, and intervals of both kinds accumulate at zero, so these circuits have no noise threshold. Without rate selection, the ring construction is a translation-invariant, time-periodic memory: from an initialized classical hierarchy it stores a qubit for a time linear in the ring length at every sufficiently small rate, addressing the memory part of a one-dimensional question raised by Dünnweber, Styliaris and Trivedi. These are existence results: registers and interaction ranges are very large and the rates astronomically small.
Problem

The question is whether the noise rate alone can determine if a fixed local noisy quantum circuit stores a qubit for a time that grows without bound with system size.

Approach

The authors build translation-invariant, time-periodic local circuits with depolarizing noise on every wire. Local noise statistics select which input a universal Turing machine runs, and halting triggers erasure of the quantum data. Protected classical clocks, concatenated CSS codes or toric-code fibres store the qubit in 1D, 3D, 4D and 6D variants. Selected lemmas, conditional fidelity bounds, lifetime estimates and the certification theorem are checked in Lean 4 with Mathlib.

Results

On non-halting rate windows a qubit is stored with entanglement fidelity at least 15/16 for unbounded time: linear in size on rings, exponential in six dimensions. On halting windows every encoding forgets after a time independent of size and rate. The labels of the storing windows form a Π^0_1-complete set, and both kinds of window accumulate at zero, so these circuits have no noise threshold.
