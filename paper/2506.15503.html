Conditioned stochastic stability of equilibrium states on uniformly hyperbolic sets

Abstract
We establish the conditioned stochastic stability of equilibrium states for Hölder potentials on uniformly hyperbolic sets. While standard stochastic stability characterises measures on attractors, we analyse the statistics of transient dynamics on non-attracting sets by conditioning small random perturbations of the dynamics to not escape from our regions of interest. We prove that as the noise intensity vanishes, the quasi-ergodic measure of the $e^φ$-weighted process generated by $\e$-small random perturbations of the deterministic dynamics converges to the unique equilibrium state associated with the potential $φ- \log \left|\det \left. D T\right|_{E^u}\right|$. The results are obtained via perturbative spectral analysis of transfer operators acting on anisotropic Banach spaces and topological hyperbolic dynamics arguments. Furthermore, we extend this framework globally to Axiom A diffeomorphisms with multiple basic sets using dynamical filtrations. This work provides a rigorous characterisation of natural measures on uniformly hyperbolic repellers, which are fundamental in the context of transient chaos. Our results have been formalised in Lean 4.
Problem

Standard stochastic stability selects invariant measures on attractors, so it cannot characterise statistics of transient dynamics on non-attracting (repelling) uniformly hyperbolic sets. The goal is to identify which equilibrium states are selected when small random perturbations are conditioned not to escape a region.

Approach

Random perturbations are conditioned on survival in an isolating neighbourhood, and the resulting quasi-ergodic measures are studied. The main tools are perturbative spectral analysis of transfer operators on Baladi's anisotropic Banach spaces, Keller–Liverani perturbation theory, and topological hyperbolic dynamics arguments. The framework is extended to Axiom A diffeomorphisms with multiple basic sets via dynamical filtrations. All main theorems and the cited supporting results are formalised in Lean 4 with Mathlib, and the formalisation is attributed to AI models.
