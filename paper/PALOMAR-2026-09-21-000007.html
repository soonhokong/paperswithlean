Lean formalization proving unbiasedness, integrability, the Rao–Blackwell variance bound, and stopping-time properties for the SCOPE and REACH Monte Carlo estimators.
Abstract
SCOPE and REACH are integrable unbiased estimators of an outcome-before-stopping probability for autoregressive models on countably infinite vocabularies. REACH is the conditional expectation of a two-stage Monte Carlo estimator given the outcome-free backbones. Its variance bound against ordinary Monte Carlo is derived from that Rao–Blackwell identity and the law of total variance, for every positive sample count. The development also proves the stopping-time property, almost-sure finiteness, and the bridge between the two sampling experiments.

Problem

SCOPE and REACH are unbiased estimators of the probability that an outcome occurs before stopping in autoregressive models over countably infinite vocabularies. Their correctness and variance claims require rigorous proof, including when stopping times are only almost-surely finite.

Approach

The estimators are formalized in Lean, with the stopping-time property and almost-sure finiteness established. REACH is characterized as the conditional expectation of a two-stage Monte Carlo estimator given the outcome-free backbones. The variance bound follows from this Rao–Blackwell identity together with the law of total variance, and a bridge lemma connects the two sampling experiments.

Results

The machine-checked development proves that SCOPE and REACH are integrable and unbiased. It also proves that REACH's variance is bounded by that of ordinary Monte Carlo for every positive sample count.
