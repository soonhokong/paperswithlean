Abstract
This Lean 4 formalization of Kong and Zeng's To cover a permutohedron (arXiv:2509.13877v3, 9 April 2026) determines the minimum number of affine hyperplanes needed to cover the permutation vertices of (1,...,n), excluding the hyperplane containing every vertex. The exact answer is n for odd n at least 3, and n-1 for even n at least 4. It also proves that an arbitrary injective real tuple of length n at least 2 has the sharp hyperplane intersection bound (n-1)! when its n-1 signed critical values are distinct. Genuine polynomial-root path lifting establishes local transpositions and the cycle at infinity, with explicit corrections to the source's loop hypotheses. A symmetric polynomial norm replaces the paper's general Bezout step. One independent Challenge and one Comparator configuration cover all eighteen results, including sharp constructions, generic and four-element consequences, and the connection with convex vertices and arbitrary affine hyperplanes.

Problem

Determine the minimum number of affine hyperplanes needed to cover all permutation vertices of (1,...,n), excluding the hyperplane that contains every vertex. A related question is a sharp bound on how many permutation vertices of an injective real tuple a hyperplane can meet.

Approach

The results of Kong and Zeng (arXiv:2509.13877v3) are formalized in Lean 4. Polynomial-root path lifting establishes local transpositions and the cycle at infinity, with explicit corrections to the source's loop hypotheses. A symmetric polynomial norm argument replaces the paper's general Bezout step. One independent Challenge and one Comparator configuration check the eighteen stated results.

Results

The minimum cover size is proved to be n for odd n >= 3 and n-1 for even n >= 4. For any injective real tuple of length n >= 2 whose n-1 signed critical values are distinct, the sharp hyperplane intersection bound (n-1)! is proved. Sharp constructions, generic and four-element consequences, and links to convex vertices and arbitrary affine hyperplanes are also formalized.
