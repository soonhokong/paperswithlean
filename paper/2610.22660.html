Spectral stability for strong ideal-gas shock layers

Jeffrey Humpherys

Abstract
We give a purely analytical proof of one-dimensional spectral stability of sufficiently strong shock layers for the full ideal-gas Navier–Stokes equations with constant viscosity and heat conduction. Earlier work reduced this question to a limiting problem and gave strong numerical evidence of stability through Evans function computation, but no proof. We prove stability for every adiabatic index $1<γ<3$ and for an explicit range of ratios of heat conduction to viscosity that contains all common gases.
Problem

The question is the one-dimensional spectral stability of strong shock layers for the full ideal-gas Navier–Stokes equations with constant viscosity and heat conduction. Earlier work reduced this to a limiting strong-shock problem and gave numerical Evans-function evidence of stability, but no proof.

Approach

The spectral problem is posed in integrated coordinates. A triangular change of thermal unknown cancels the thermal mass coupling, and an energy identity with a tuned constant weight follows. Coercivity of the resulting quadratic form is shown by elementary coefficient bounds, using the explicit Becker profile and a slope enclosure on a transport-parameter region. Boundary fluxes are shown to vanish for admissible nonzero frequencies. The arguments are checked in Lean 4 with Mathlib, taking the results of Humpherys–Lyng–Zumbrun as hypotheses.

Results

Stability holds for every adiabatic index 1<γ<3 on the reference line ν=Γ+1. It also holds on an explicit region of heat-conduction-to-viscosity ratios that contains all common gases. The proof uses no numerical computation, and the Lean check contains no unproved steps.
