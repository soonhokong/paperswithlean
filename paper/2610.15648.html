Post-critically finite polynomials in characteristic $p$

Patrick Ingram

Abstract
It follows from the compactness of the connectedness locus that the locus of PCF polynomials in the moduli space of polynomials of degree d (at least 2) over the complex number is a countable union of zero-dimensional subvarieties. Over fields of characteristic p the analogous claim is certainly false, so it is natural to ask how large the components of the PCF locus can be. We show that the components of the PCF locus in the moduli space of polynomials of degree d over an algebraically closed field of characteristic p>0 have dimension at most d/p and that this bound is sharp, in the strong sense that for every p and d there are infinitely many distinct PCF components whose dimension is the greatest integer no greater than this bound (except in the case $p=d=2$).
Problem

Over the complex numbers, the post-critically finite (PCF) locus in the moduli space of degree-d polynomials is zero-dimensional. In characteristic p this fails, which raises the question of how large components of the PCF locus can be.

Approach

Transcendence over the prime field is characterized via non-archimedean absolute values, and escape-rate estimates handle the case p | d. When p does not divide d, the problem is reduced to the critical nullcone. Explicit families show the bound is sharp. The proofs were formalized in Lean with Mathlib by AI coding agents (Codex, Claude Code); the formal version uses Krull valuations in place of real-valued absolute values.

Results

Components of the PCF locus over an algebraically closed field of characteristic p have dimension at most d/p. For every p and d except p=d=2, there are infinitely many PCF components of dimension floor(d/p). Theorems 1–3 are formalized relative to the Lean kernel and Mathlib.
