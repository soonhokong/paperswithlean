The Structure of a Non-Isometric Non-Contractive Bijection of a Hereditarily Precompact Quasi-Metric Space

Thomas Duzer

TL;DR
Most theorems and the properties of the three spiral examples are machine-checked in Lean 4 with Mathlib, with files archived on Zenodo.
Abstract
Let $F$ be a non-contractive bijection of a hereditarily precompact quasi-metric space $(X, d)$. We show that $F$ is asymptotically an isometry along its backward orbits: for every $a \in X$ and $\varepsilon > 0$, $0 \le d(Fx, Fy) - d(x, y) < \varepsilon$ for all $y$ and all $x$ sufficiently far in the backward orbit of $a$. More precisely, $d = λ^{-} + D_0$, where the asymptotic distance $λ^{-}(x, y) = \lim_k d(F^{-k}x, F^{-k}y)$ is the largest quasi-pseudo-metric below $d$ for which $F$ is an isometry, and the defect $D_0(x, y)$ lies between explicit bounds given by $λ^{-}$ and by a function $c$ that is nondecreasing along orbits and tends to $0$ along backward orbits. On each orbit, $λ^{-}$ induces, after completion, an invariant quasi-pseudo-metric on a compact metrizable monothetic group on which $F$ acts as a rotation, with the compact group topology. Conversely, every infinite compact metrizable monothetic group, with any rotation by a generator and any invariant quasi-pseudo-metric whose symmetrization is a compatible metric, arises in this way from a map that is not an isometry. Every such $c$ occurs over every possible $λ^{-}$, and the defects on a single orbit are characterized. These results give a structural answer to a classification question from the author's paper on the toll spiral (arXiv:2610.13973), a non-isometric bijection of this kind answering a question of Zavarzina. Three examples on the integers show that the defect is not determined by $c$, and that the question, read as asking for a toll charged on descents only, has a negative answer, even up to bi-Lipschitz conjugacy. The results hold for quasi-pseudo-metric spaces in which every sequence has a left K-Cauchy subsequence. Except for the statements listed at the end, among them the completion to a compact group, the results are machine-checked in Lean.
Problem

The paper studies non-contractive bijections of hereditarily precompact quasi-metric spaces that are not isometries, motivated by Zavarzina's question and a classification question raised in the earlier toll-spiral paper. The goal is to describe what structure such maps are forced to have.

Approach

The author defines the asymptotic distance (register) λ⁻ as the limit of distances along backward orbits, the defect D₀ = d − λ⁻, and the fee c, the supremum of defects on backward returns. Ramsey-free chain arguments using left K-Cauchy subsequences give the 'source pays' theorem, a two-sided band bounding the defect, and vanishing of the fee in the deep past. Orbits are completed to compact monothetic groups. Three spiral examples on the integers show which features are not forced. The results, apart from listed exceptions such as the compact-group completion, are formalized in Lean 4/Mathlib on top of earlier verification files.

Results

Every such map is asymptotically an isometry along backward orbits, with d = λ⁻ + D₀ and D₀ confined to an explicit band determined by λ⁻ and c. On each orbit λ⁻ induces an invariant quasi-pseudo-metric on a compact metrizable monothetic group on which F acts as a rotation, and a converse realization holds. The examples show that the classification question, read as asking for a toll charged on descents only, has a negative answer even up to bi-Lipschitz conjugacy.
