Breaking Resonance: Square-root acceleration for Metropolis-adjusted randomized Hamiltonian Monte Carlo

Nawaf Bou-Rabee

math.PR Nov 24, 2026 · v1 math.NA stat.CO
formalization mathlib probability
Read PDF arXiv abstract Code
TL;DR
Main theorems (mixing bound and Hessian-necessity lower bound) and a Gaussian lower bound are formalized and checked in Lean 4 with Mathlib; sources released on GitHub.
Abstract
Hamiltonian Monte Carlo with a fixed integration time can fail to mix because of resonances, and randomizing the integration time removes them. We prove that Metropolis-adjusted HMC with velocity Verlet and random integration times mixes from a warm start in a number of transitions that does not depend on the condition number, for every integration-time law satisfying a Fourier condition that we call non-resonant; uniform, exponential and triangular laws are examples. For a target density proportional to $e^{-U}$ on $\mathbb R^d$, where $U$ is $m$-strongly convex with $L$-Lipschitz gradient and $L_H$-Lipschitz Hessian and $κ=L/m$, the number of gradient evaluations needed to reach a fixed total variation accuracy is, up to constants and logarithmic factors, $\sqrt{κd}+\sqrt{L_H}\,(d/m)^{3/4}$. This is the first square-root condition-number bound for this sampler beyond Gaussian targets. The proof converts relative-entropy contraction of the unadjusted proposal into a conductance bound for the adjusted chain, and shows that the integration-time law enters only through its characteristic function. Hessian regularity cannot be dropped for a fixed step size: without it there are targets in the class for which the cost is at least of order $κ^{2/3}$, up to logarithmic factors, even from a warm start.
Problem

With a fixed integration time, Hamiltonian Monte Carlo can fail to mix because of resonances. The open question was whether Metropolis-adjusted randomized HMC with velocity Verlet achieves square-root condition-number dependence beyond Gaussian targets.

Approach

The integration-time law is required to satisfy a Fourier 'non-resonant' condition; uniform, exponential and triangular laws are examples. Relative-entropy contraction of the unadjusted proposal is converted into a restricted conductance bound for the adjusted chain. The duration law enters only through its characteristic function, and a modified-energy argument controls the Verlet energy error. Theorems 1 and 2 and Proposition B.1 were formalized and checked in Lean 4 against Mathlib, using only Lean's three standard axioms.

Results

From a warm start, the gradient cost to reach fixed total variation accuracy is of order sqrt(κd) + sqrt(L_H)(d/m)^{3/4}, up to logarithmic factors. Without Hessian regularity and with a fixed step size, there are targets in the class whose cost is at least of order κ^{2/3}.
