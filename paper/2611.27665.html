On Robust Strictly Positive Real Synthesis for High-Order Interval Polynomials

Tiancheng Xu, Guowei Dou, Xiang Ji, ZhengTai Xiong, Long Wang, Wensheng Yu

eess.SY Nov 20, 2026 · v1 math.OC

TL;DR
Both main theorems, the order-ten-and-higher counterexamples and the all-order synthesis result, are formally verified in Lean 4, with a public repository.
Abstract
We establish two complementary results on common strictly positive real (SPR) numerators for Hurwitz-stable interval polynomial families. First, for every order $n \ge 10$, we construct a monic Hurwitz-stable interval polynomial family with rational endpoints and positive width in every nonleading coefficient such that every nonzero real numerator of degree at most $n$ produces a strictly negative real part at some member and some positive frequency. In particular, robust Hurwitz stability alone does not guarantee equal-degree SPR synthesis in high orders. Second, for every order $n \ge 1$, we prove that a common real SPR numerator of exact degree $n$ always exists whenever the lower endpoint of each interior coefficient interval is at most $10^{-5}$ times its upper endpoint, while the constant and leading coefficients vary over arbitrary compact positive intervals.
Problem

The question is whether every Hurwitz-stable interval polynomial family admits a common strictly positive real (SPR) numerator of equal degree. Existence was known only for orders n ≤ 4 and for polynomial segments.

Approach

A finite obstruction criterion uses vertex–frequency evaluation rows with full column rank and a strictly positive linear dependence. An explicit rational order-ten Hurwitz box satisfies this criterion. The obstruction is lifted to all higher orders via the map a(s) ↦ s·a(s) + η. Separately, a scale-separation argument based on four-coefficient stability inequalities constructs a common numerator when the interior endpoint ratios are small.

Results

For every n ≥ 10, there are full-width rational Hurwitz boxes that admit no nonzero numerator of degree at most n. For every n ≥ 1, a common SPR numerator exists whenever l_j/u_j ≤ 10^-5 for all interior coefficients. Both theorems are machine-checked in Lean 4.
