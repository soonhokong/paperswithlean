Abstract
Formalizes Arrow's impossibility theorem for finitely many voters and finitely many alternatives (at least three): every social welfare function satisfying unanimity and independence of irrelevant alternatives is dictatorial. The proof follows the classical decisive-coalition argument: field expansion (a coalition decisive for one pair is decisive for every pair) and group contraction (a decisive coalition with at least two voters contains a smaller decisive coalition), iterated down to a singleton dictator. This is a reusable formalization artifact, not a claim of new mathematics.

Problem

Arrow's impossibility theorem states that every social welfare function satisfying unanimity and independence of irrelevant alternatives, over at least three alternatives, is dictatorial. The goal is a machine-checked version of this result for finitely many voters and alternatives.

Approach

The proof follows the classical decisive-coalition argument. Field expansion shows that a coalition decisive for one pair of alternatives is decisive for every pair. Group contraction shows that a decisive coalition with at least two voters contains a smaller decisive coalition. Iterating contraction reduces the coalition to a single voter, the dictator.

Results

A complete Lean formalization of the general finite version of Arrow's theorem, with proofs machine-checked by the registry. It is presented as a reusable formalization artifact rather than new mathematics.
