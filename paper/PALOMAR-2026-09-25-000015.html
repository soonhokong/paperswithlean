TL;DR

Abstract
A Lean 4 / mathlib formalization of Dana Scott's 1964 paper *Measurement Structures and Linear Inequalities* (Journal of Mathematical Psychology 1 (1964), 233–247). The paper derives necessary and sufficient conditions for three finite representation problems — intransitive indifference, ordered differences, and subjective probability — from the general solvability criterion for finite systems of linear inequalities. The development also covers Scott's ordered-group and signed-charge remarks, literal vector cancellation, and the KPS obstruction to de Finetti's five axioms. Every compared equivalence is described predicate-by-predicate in Challenge.lean and the README, including its numerical-representation side and its qualitative cancellation, permutation, reversal, probability, or Kelley-cover side. The additional infinite theorem is a new, stronger modern characterization first stated in this project; it adapts Kelley's 1959 countable-cover/separation method and is not attributed to Scott. The Lean development was drafted with AI-agent assistance under Lars Warren Ericson's direction and review. Dana S. Scott, Vijay D'Silva, and Brian Milnes are co-authors of the formalization report. There is no sorry outside the deliberate holes in Challenge.lean, no project-defined axiom, and Mathlib is the only Lake dependency.

Problem

Dana Scott's 1964 paper gives necessary and sufficient conditions for finite representation problems in measurement theory: intransitive indifference, ordered differences, and subjective probability. It derives them from the solvability criterion for finite systems of linear inequalities. These results had not been machine-checked.

Approach

The results are formalized in Lean 4 on top of Mathlib, which is the only Lake dependency. The development also covers Scott's ordered-group and signed-charge remarks, literal vector cancellation, and the KPS obstruction to de Finetti's five axioms. Each equivalence is stated predicate-by-predicate in Challenge.lean, linking numerical representations to qualitative cancellation, permutation, reversal, probability, or Kelley-cover conditions. The development was drafted with AI-agent assistance under human direction and review.

The formalization contains no sorry outside the deliberate holes in Challenge.lean and no project-defined axioms. It adds a new, stronger infinite characterization theorem that adapts Kelley's 1959 countable-cover/separation method.
