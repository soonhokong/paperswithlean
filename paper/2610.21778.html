Same Spectrum, Harder First-Order Training: Phase-Locking in Single- versus Multi-Qubit Re-Uploading Circuits

Michael Poppel, David Bucher, Maximilian Zorn, Markus Baumann, Sebastian Wölckert, Claudia Linnhoff-Popien, Philipp Altmann, Jonas Stein

quant-ph Oct 15, 2026 · v1 cs.LG

TL;DR
All lemmas, propositions and corollaries on the tangent structure of re-uploading circuits are formalised in Lean 4 with Mathlib, developed with Aristotle; cited results enter as hypotheses.
Abstract
Angle-encoded variational quantum circuits implement truncated Fourier series. For one-dimensional inputs, a single-qubit re-uploading (serial) circuit represents every function that a multi-qubit circuit with the same encoding budget $E$ can; we find that this exchange of width for depth is not free in first-order training. Because its state lives on the Bloch sphere, all parameters of the serial circuit move the state, at each input, along one complex direction, which we call phase-locking. We prove that this costs no Fourier direction but leaves no direction to spare: however many parameters it has, the serial circuit has at most $2E+2$ tangent directions, one more than its $2E+1$ Fourier coefficients need, while a two-qubit control with the same encoding budget and model class generically has at least $6E+6$. The cost in training is measured against this control, as the number of runs out of 100 that reach the optimum within a fixed step budget. On an exact loss with flat-spectrum targets the gap grows with $E$; at $E=32$ the serial circuit converges in 3, 53 and 83 runs under gradient descent, Adam and Levenberg–Marquardt, the control in 99, 100 and 100. On three real-world series, at matched parameter counts, the serial circuit reaches the least-squares optimum in at most 9 runs under Adam and in 32 to 93 under Levenberg–Marquardt, the control in at least 98 and in all 100; its held-out error is up to about $1.5$ times that of the control under Adam, but no larger in the typical run under Levenberg–Marquardt.
Problem

A single-qubit re-uploading (serial) circuit can represent every one-dimensional function that a multi-qubit circuit with the same encoding budget E can. The question is whether trading width for depth costs anything in first-order training.

Approach

The authors prove that the serial circuit's state on the Bloch sphere makes all parameters move it along one complex direction at each input, which they call phase-locking. This caps the serial circuit at 2E+2 tangent directions, against at least 6E+6 for a two-qubit ancilla control with the same model class. The structural results are formalised in Lean 4/Mathlib. Training is then compared on exact losses and on real-world series under gradient descent, Adam and Levenberg–Marquardt.

Results

At E=32 on flat-spectrum targets, the serial circuit converges in 3, 53 and 83 of 100 runs under gradient descent, Adam and Levenberg–Marquardt; the control converges in 99, 100 and 100. On three real-world series the serial circuit reaches the least-squares optimum in at most 9 runs under Adam, against at least 98 for the control. Under Adam its held-out error is up to about 1.5 times the control's.
