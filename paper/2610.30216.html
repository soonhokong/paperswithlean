Lean-QuantumAlg: A Framework for AI Agents to Produce Quantum Algorithms that Humans Can Trust

Lei Zhang, Mingrui Jing, Yusheng Zhao, Jiahong Liu, Xin Wang

quant-ph Oct 21, 2026 · v1

TL;DR
Lean 4 framework built on Mathlib for certifying AI-generated quantum algorithms against correctness and resource-bound specifications, with nine formalized algorithms.
Abstract
AI agents are increasingly capable of constructing quantum algorithms and proofs, yet machine-checked proofs alone cannot guarantee that the intended problem is solved or that resource claims match the verified computation.Here we introduce Lean-QuantumAlg, a Lean 4 framework for trustworthy AI-assisted development of quantum algorithms.Its Algorithm-Solves-Problem contract binds correctness, success probability, borrowed-state restoration and quantum and classical resource bounds to a single execution, while separating the guarantees that Lean checks from the problem specifications and execution models that humans review. We demonstrate the framework on nine AI-formalized quantum algorithms, from Grover search to quantum attacks on RSA-2048 and P-256, with less than 1% of each algorithm's imported source code requiring statement-level human review once the shared framework has been audited. By integrating reusable mathematical foundations, certified algorithmic primitives and execution-grounded verification, Lean-QuantumAlg provides a basis for AI agents to construct and certify quantum algorithms, opening a path toward scalable AI-driven discovery of quantum algorithms.
Problem

AI agents can produce quantum algorithms with machine-checked proofs. A checked proof alone does not guarantee that the intended problem is solved or that resource claims match the verified computation. Human review of the formal statements remains necessary but is costly.

Approach

Lean-QuantumAlg is a Lean 4 library built on Mathlib that defines an Algorithm–Solves–Problem contract. The Solves relation binds correctness, success probability, borrowed-state restoration, and quantum and classical resource bounds to a single execution in an explicit execution model. In that model, gate, oracle and classical-operation tokens are enforced by types. AI agents construct the algorithms and proofs, and humans review only the problem specification, the execution model and the final theorem main.

Results

Nine quantum algorithms were formalized by AI within the framework, ranging from Grover search to quantum attacks on RSA-2048 and P-256. Once the shared framework has been audited, less than 1% of each algorithm's imported source code requires statement-level human review.
