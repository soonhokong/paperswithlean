The Cost of an Unknown Horizon for the Last Iterate of Clipped SGD under Heavy-Tailed Noise

Yuxing Peng, Zhiqing Tang, Weijia Jia

Abstract
When training stops at a data-dependent time, a guarantee for the returned model must hold at all times simultaneously. We study the last iterate of clipped SGD on smooth convex objectives under heavy-tailed stochastic gradients with a bounded central $p$th moment, $1<p\le2$. With a known horizon $T$, clipped SGD reaches the optimal last-iterate rate $O(T^{-α_p})$, where $α_p=(p-1)/p$ (Liu, 2026; Jiang et al., 2026). Without a known horizon, Chezhegov et al. (ICLR 2026) obtained the time-uniform rate $\tilde O(t^{-2(p-1)/(3p)})$ and conjectured that a better schedule reaches the optimal rate. We show that for clipped SGD, and more generally for every rule whose iterate does not advance on rounds without new gradient information, the optimal time-uniform rate is $\tilde O(t^{-β_p})$, where $β_p=2(p-1)/(3p-1)$. Our deterministic schedule for clipped SGD uses neither a horizon nor a confidence input and attains $\tilde O(t^{-β_p})$ at all times simultaneously, and hence almost surely. Conversely, no such rule attains $\tilde O(t^{-r})$ at all times for any $r>β_p$; for clipped SGD, this holds even with adaptive, randomized step sizes and clipping thresholds. Under finite variance, the known-horizon, optimal time-uniform, and previous time-uniform exponents are $1/2$, $2/5$, and $1/3$. Since $β_p<α_p$, the conjecture, which holds with a known horizon, fails without one. By contrast, an update that queries gradients at the running average can advance the iterate on rounds without new gradient information and attains $\tilde O(t^{-α_p})$ at all times. Memory alone does not suffice: heavy-ball momentum with geometric forgetting and polynomially bounded step sizes cannot improve on the exponent $β_p$.
Problem

When training stops at a data-dependent time, last-iterate guarantees must hold at all times simultaneously. The paper asks what last-iterate rate clipped SGD can attain on smooth convex objectives under heavy-tailed noise with a bounded central pth moment when the horizon is unknown.

Approach

The authors give a deterministic horizon-free step-size and clipping schedule and analyze it with a tail-sum potential and martingale tail bounds. They prove matching lower bounds for rules whose iterate does not advance without new gradient information, and they study fixed-time tradeoffs, running-average queries, and heavy-ball momentum. Theorems 1, 4 and 8(i) are formalized in Lean 4 with Mathlib, including their proof dependencies.

Results

The optimal time-uniform exponent is beta_p = 2(p-1)/(3p-1), which is 2/5 under finite variance, compared with 1/2 at a known horizon. This disproves the conjecture of Chezhegov et al. that the known-horizon rate is reachable without a horizon. Querying gradients at the running average recovers the known-horizon rate, while geometric-forgetting momentum cannot beat beta_p.
