Algebraic Spectral Curves of Two-Channel Operator Pencils with Power-Law Response

Kejun Liu

Abstract
A finite matrix can acquire a nontrivial algebraic spectral curve when its entries contain a multivalued response. We study this mechanism for a two-channel pencil with scalar response $z^β$ and $J$-self-adjoint matrices. If $β=r/n$ is in lowest terms and the coefficients are generic, the normalized determinant curve in $(z,λ)$ has genus $n-1$ and is a double cover. It is rational for $n=1$, elliptic for $n=2$, and hyperelliptic for $n\geq3$. The generic genus depends only on the denominator $n$. The proof reduces birationally to a classical hyperelliptic normal form. The numerator appears as the cyclic character of the distinguished coupling and controls its divisor and boundary singularity. Here $λ$ is a complexified coupling coordinate: the curve is the joint determinant locus, not a fixed-coupling spectrum. For irrational exponents the local monodromy has infinite order, while rational approximants converge uniformly on compact sets and preserve isolated zeros on a fixed branch. Negative exponents in $(-1,0)$ arise from causal Volterra kernels and cutoff continuum-bath Schur complements, separating finite channels from nonlocal memory. As an application, the exact leading infrared monomial model of a zero-temperature conformal response of dimension $h_{\rm probe}$ has, for rational $2h_{\rm probe}$, genus $\operatorname{den}(2h_{\rm probe})-1$. For the fundamental fermion in the even-$q>2$ Sachdev–Ye–Kitaev saddle, this becomes $q/2-1$. The core statements, including determinant algebra, branch counts, and causal-response identities, are machine-checked from raw matrix definitions.
Problem

A finite two-channel matrix pencil whose entries contain a multivalued power-law response z^β can have a nontrivial algebraic spectral curve. The question is how the genus of this curve depends on the exponent.

Approach

The determinant of the J-self-adjoint two-channel pencil is computed on the cyclic cover z=u^n, and a coordinate change maps it birationally to a hyperelliptic normal form y^2=Q_n(u). The discriminant and Riemann–Hurwitz arguments give the genus, irrational exponents are treated through monodromy and rational approximation, and the result is applied to near-AdS2 conformal responses. The determinant algebra, branch counts, and causal-response identities are formalized in Lean 4 on Mathlib. Classical inputs such as the hyperelliptic genus formula and Rouché's theorem are packaged as Lean structures with proposition fields.

Results

For reduced β=r/n and generic coefficients, the normalized determinant curve has genus n-1 and is a double cover. For n=2 an explicit j-invariant formula in the matrix invariants is given. For the fundamental fermion in the even-q SYK saddle, the genus is q/2-1.
