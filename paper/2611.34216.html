Ramification divisors and twisted Nambu brackets of Keller maps

Geoffrey Mboya, Stephen Mboya, Carringtone Kinyanjui

Abstract
For a polynomial map $φ\colon\A^n\to\A^n$ we study the Yau twist of the Jacobian Nambu bracket by $φ^*$. The Hamiltonian fields of pulled back functions span the image of the adjugate of $dφ$, so the twist detects the ramification divisor of $φ$. For a Keller map $F$ the twist is removable exactly when $F$ is an automorphism; otherwise $F$ is not proper, and every projective extension of $F$ has a non-zero effective ramification divisor supported on the boundary, whose image is the non-properness set. These results apply to every counterexample to the Jacobian conjecture and to families of Keller maps. For the explicit three-dimensional counterexample we show that $\A^3$ is the variety of binary cubic forms of a special shape with a marked simple root, and that $F$ forgets the root. This gives the degree, the fibres, the non-properness set, the monodromy and the structure of the twisted ternary algebra. Three examples modelled on weighted scrolls illustrate equivariance, degeneration to an automorphism, and an $A_1$ singularity of the fibre.
Problem

The work asks what the Yau twist of the Jacobian Nambu bracket by the pullback of a polynomial map says about the map's geometry. The case of interest is Keller maps and counterexamples to the Jacobian conjecture.

Approach

Hamiltonian fields of pulled-back functions are identified with the image of the adjugate of dφ, so the twist detects the ramification divisor. For Keller maps, projective extensions and canonical divisors are used to study ramification at infinity. An explicit three-dimensional counterexample is analysed by realising A^3 as a variety of binary cubic forms with a marked root. The algebraic core is certified in Lean 4 with Mathlib through AI-generated proof files. The geometric parts were checked by hand.

Results

For a Keller map, the twist is removable exactly when the map is an automorphism. Otherwise every projective extension has a nonzero effective ramification divisor on the boundary, whose image is the non-properness set. For the explicit counterexample, the paper determines the degree, fibres, non-properness set and monodromy.
