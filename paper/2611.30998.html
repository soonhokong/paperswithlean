Spurious Information in Large Markets: Random Matrix Laws for Entropy, Mutual Information and the Value of Information

James Rice

econ.EM Nov 23, 2026 · v1 stat.CO

TL;DR
Selected algebraic and combinatorial steps (telescoping digamma identities, Narayana/Catalan recursions, BBP threshold identities) are machine-checked in Lean 4, with a public repository.
Abstract
Entropy, Kullback-Leibler divergence and mutual information have become standard currencies for measuring how much one set of financial variables reveals about another. In large cross-sections they are almost always computed from sample covariance matrices, and the noise in those matrices is the object of random matrix theory. This paper develops the random matrix theory of Gaussian information functionals and applies it to markets. I show that the plug-in mutual information between two blocks of p and r variables estimated from $n$ observations has an exact expected excess, the spurious information, that is a digamma telescope independent of the true dependence. Under independence its law is that of a product of Beta variables. In the proportional regime it grows linearly with an order-one variance. The same quantity is a linear spectral statistic of the Jacobi ensemble, which yields a closed form for the logarithmic moment of Wachter's law. Spurious information increases strictly whenever uninformative coordinates are added. In a CARA-Gaussian economy the certainty-equivalent value of a signal equals mutual information divided by risk aversion, so these results translate one for one into a spurious value of information. The canonical-correlation phase transition then defines a frontier of minimum detectable information. On daily returns of 49 US industry portfolios from 1990 to 2026, I document the market mode, the reliability of sector eigenvectors implied by the BBP transition, the out-of-sample cost of noise dressing, and an information linkage between carbon-intensive and financial industries that survives exact bias correction and conditioning on the market.
Problem

Entropy, KL divergence and mutual information in finance are usually computed from sample covariance matrices. In large cross-sections, random matrix noise biases these plug-in estimates.

Approach

Develops a random matrix theory of Gaussian information functionals. It derives the exact expected excess ('spurious information') of plug-in mutual information as a digamma telescope, its Beta-product null law, proportional-regime limits via Wachter's law, and a CARA-Gaussian value-of-information identity. Formulas are checked by Monte Carlo, and supporting algebraic and combinatorial steps are verified in Lean 4.

Results

The spurious information is independent of the true dependence, and subtracting it gives an exactly unbiased estimator. It grows strictly when uninformative coordinates are added and translates one-for-one into a spurious value of information. An application to 49 US industry portfolios (1990–2026) documents the market mode, sector eigenvector reliability and a carbon–finance information linkage.
