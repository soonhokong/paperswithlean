$δ$-derivations of simple finite-dimensional Jordan superalgebras

Ivan Kaygorodov

math.RA Oct 12, 2010 · v2 math.AC
formalization algebra
Read PDF arXiv abstract
TL;DR
Lean checks the Jordan identity for the corrected K_10 multiplication table over Q, and checks that its 1/2-derivations are scalar in characteristic 0.
Abstract
We describe non-trivial $δ$-derivations of semisimple finite-dimensional Jordan algebras over an algebraically closed field of characteristic not $2$, and of simple finite-dimensional Jordan superalgebras over an algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$. For these classes of algebras and superalgebras, non-zero non-trivial $δ$-derivations are shown to be missing for $δ\notin\{ 0,\frac12,1\}$, and we give a complete account of $\frac12$-derivations.
Problem

The paper classifies non-trivial δ-derivations of semisimple finite-dimensional Jordan algebras in characteristic not 2. It does the same for simple finite-dimensional Jordan superalgebras over an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0.

Approach

Case analysis runs over the classification of simple Jordan algebras: F·1, J(V,f) and H(D_n,J). It then covers the superalgebras M_{m,n}^{(+)}, Q(n)^{(+)}, osp, P(n), J(V,f), J(Γ_n), K_3, D_t and K_10. A unity argument rules out δ ≠ 1/2 for unital algebras, and basis computations handle the 1/2-derivations. A corrected version fixes an ambiguous K_10 multiplication table, and Lean is used to verify the Jordan identity and the scalar form of its 1/2-derivations.

Results

Non-zero non-trivial δ-derivations do not exist for δ ∉ {0, 1/2, 1}. A complete account of 1/2-derivations is given; in most cases they are scalar multiplications φ(x) = αx. The corrected K_10 results are machine-checked in Lean.
