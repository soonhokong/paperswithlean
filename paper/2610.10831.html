Tweaking the constant in the Linear Hadwiger Theorem

David R. Wood

TL;DR
The main theorem bounding chromatic number by 19,885,160 times Hadwiger number is formally verified in Lean 4 with Mathlib, extending Norin–Steiner's Lean formalisation; files are ancillary.
Abstract
Norin and Steiner recently proved the Linear Hadwiger Conjecture. That is, there is an absolute constant $C$ such that every graph $G$ satisfies $χ(G)\leqslant C\,\text{had}(G)$, where $χ(G)$ is the chromatic number and $\text{had}(G)$ is the Hadwiger number of $G$. Their proof gives a non-optimised constant $C$ of order $10^{100}$. This paper uses extensive AI-based optimisation to show that every graph $G$ satisfies $χ(G) \leqslant 19{,}885{,}160\,\text{had}(G)$.
Problem

Norin and Steiner proved the Linear Hadwiger Conjecture, χ(G) ≤ C·had(G), but with a non-optimised constant C of order 10^100. The goal is to make the constant explicit and much smaller.

Approach

Each part of the Norin–Steiner argument is reworked with explicit constants, with extensive AI-based optimisation. The changes include a refined version of Lin's theorem that yields dense, highly connected small subgraphs, an explicit chromatic-separable reduction, and an additive bootstrap in place of the multiplicative one. The final theorem is formally verified in Lean 4 with Mathlib, building on the existing Lean formalisation of the Norin–Steiner proof and its external inputs.

Results

Every graph G satisfies χ(G) ≤ 19,885,160·had(G). The term (472/3)·126,115.1, the constant for colouring graphs of order t(log t)^{O(1)}, accounts for 99.8% of the constant. The Lean statement matches Theorem 1.3 exactly, and the Lean files are provided as arXiv ancillary files.
