Tweaking the constant in the Linear Hadwiger Theorem

David R. Wood

math.CO Oct 7, 2026 · v2 cs.DM
formalization mathlib combinatorics
Read PDF arXiv abstract Code
TL;DR
The main theorem bounding chromatic number by 19,885,160 times Hadwiger number is formally verified in Lean 4 with Mathlib, building on Norin–Steiner's Lean formalisation.
Abstract
Norin and Steiner recently proved the Linear Hadwiger Conjecture. That is, there is an absolute constant $C$ such that every graph $G$ satisfies $χ(G)\leqslant C\,\text{had}(G)$, where $χ(G)$ is the chromatic number and $\text{had}(G)$ is the Hadwiger number of $G$. They estimate that their proof gives a non-optimised constant $C$ of order $10^{100}$. This paper uses AI assistance to optimise their argument, and shows that every graph $G$ satisfies $χ(G)\,\leqslant\,1{,}118{,}430\,\text{had}(G)$.
Problem

Norin and Steiner proved the Linear Hadwiger Conjecture, χ(G) ≤ C·had(G), with a non-optimised constant of order 10^100. The goal is to obtain an explicit, much smaller constant.

Approach

The Norin–Steiner argument is optimised with AI assistance. The main changes are a refined form of Lin's theorem that extracts dense, highly connected small subgraphs, an explicit chromatic-separable reduction, rooted-minor results via Wollan's massed pairs, and an additive rather than multiplicative bootstrap. The final theorem is formally verified in Lean 4 with Mathlib, extending the Norin–Steiner Lean formalisation; the Lean files are provided as arXiv ancillary files.

Results

Every graph G satisfies χ(G) ≤ 1,118,430·had(G) according to the abstract. The body text and Table 1 state 19,885,160·had(G), likely an earlier version of the bound. The Lean statement matches the main theorem exactly.
