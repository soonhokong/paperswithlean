Abstract
A formalization in Lean 4 and Mathlib of the Erdős discrepancy theorem, proved by Terence Tao in 2015: for every sequence f(1), f(2), … of signs ±1 and every C there are d ≥ 1 and n with |f(d) + f(2d) + ⋯ + f(nd)| > C, so every ±1 sequence has unbounded discrepancy along homogeneous arithmetic progressions. This is the original ±1 formulation, Corollary 1.2 of Tao's paper; its Hilbert-space-valued Theorem 1.1 is not formalized. The proof follows the architecture of Tao's argument: a Fourier-analytic reduction from a sequence of bounded discrepancy to a stochastic completely multiplicative function with bounded mean-square partial sums; a van der Corput argument, driven by the logarithmically averaged two-point Elliott estimate, forcing such a function to pretend to be a twisted Dirichlet character; and a generalized Borwein–Choi–Coons argument excluding that case. The analytic number theory these steps use is proved in the same development rather than assumed: the entropy-decrement proof of the two-point Elliott estimate from Tao's companion paper, the Matomäki–Radziwiłł short-interval estimate on the major arcs via the Dirichlet-polynomial route of Matomäki, Radziwiłł and Tao, the Halász–Montgomery large-values inequality, a large-values bound for Dirichlet polynomials over primes from a zero-free region of the Riemann zeta function, that zero-free region from Weyl-sum bounds on zeta, and a form of Vinogradov's mean value theorem. Where a published route did not fit, the development adapts it; the adaptations are documented. The theorem depends only on Lean's three standard axioms. This is a formalization of a published result, not a new proof; it was produced by AI agents working through pull requests checked by continuous integration in the ProofFleet repository (formerly MoltResearch).

Problem

The Erdős discrepancy theorem, proved by Tao in 2015, states that every ±1 sequence has unbounded discrepancy along homogeneous arithmetic progressions. The work gives a machine-checked proof of this ±1 formulation (Corollary 1.2 of Tao's paper); the Hilbert-space-valued Theorem 1.1 is not formalized.

Approach

The development follows Tao's argument in Lean 4 with Mathlib. A Fourier-analytic reduction passes to a stochastic completely multiplicative function. A van der Corput argument driven by the two-point logarithmic Elliott estimate forces that function to pretend to be a twisted Dirichlet character, and a generalized Borwein–Choi–Coons argument excludes that case. The needed analytic number theory is proved within the development, including entropy decrement, the Matomäki–Radziwiłł short-interval estimates, Halász–Montgomery, a zeta zero-free region from Weyl sums, and Vinogradov's mean value theorem. The proofs were written by AI agents through CI-checked pull requests in the ProofFleet repository.
