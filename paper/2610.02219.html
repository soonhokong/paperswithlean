Existence of an infinite family of substrates satisfying all the postulates of integrated information theory, exclusion included, with arbitrarily large integrated information

A. Mayeux

q-bio.NC Sep 9, 2026 · v1 cs.IT
formalization mathlib combinatorics
Read PDF arXiv abstract Code
TL;DR
Proves exclusion and a super-exponential lower bound on integrated information for an XOR cycle in Lean 4 with Mathlib, building on a formalized IIT 4.0 library.
Abstract
For infinitely many sizes we exhibit a cycle of binary units, each taking the exclusive-or of itself and its two clockwise neighbours, that is a complex in every state, no proper subsystem being more integrated, and whose integrated information grows as two raised to a power quadratic in the number of units.
Problem

Integrated information theory (IIT 4.0) has known upper bounds on integrated information, but no family of growing systems had been proved to have growing integrated information. No substrate had been proved to satisfy the exclusion postulate.

Approach

The paper studies a cycle of N binary units, each computing the XOR of itself and its two clockwise neighbours. A damage model reduces system integrated information under partitions to a count of damaged units, using bijectivity when 3 does not divide N. Partitions are classified by damage budget to show the whole cycle beats every proper subsystem, and arcs of length at least 3 are shown to be distinctions that feed a relation-counting lower bound. All statements except one classification proposition are formalized in Lean 4 against Mathlib and a separate IIT 4.0 library. Language-model assistants wrote the proofs against human-fixed statements.

Results

For infinitely many N, the cycle is a complex in every state, with system integrated information at least 4 against strictly less than 4 for every proper subsystem. Its integrated information satisfies Φ ≥ 2^{⌊N²/8⌋}·2^{-N}/(2N) − 1. The development is kernel-checked with only standard axioms; the arc classification (Proposition 6.2) is verified only numerically.
