Abstract
A Lean 4 formalization, against Mathlib, of the qualitative statement of J. H. Bae, "A resolution of Erdős Problem #190: the canonical van der Waerden number satisfies H(k)^{1/k}/k → ∞" (arXiv:2604.20588, v2), Corollary 1.2, via the elementary argument of its Section 4.3. Let H(k) be the least N such that every finite colouring of {1,…,N} contains a monochromatic or a rainbow k-term arithmetic progression; Erdős and Graham (1979) asked whether H(k)^{1/k}/k → ∞ (Problem 190 in Bloom's database). The compared theorems state that for every C there is K such that for all k ≥ K every N for which [N] is canonical for k satisfies (Ck)^k < N (divergence, and its Filter.Eventually form); this is a statement about canonical N only and by itself says nothing about H(k) (it holds vacuously for a k with no canonical N, where H(k) = sInf ∅ = 0). Combined with the existence of a canonical N for every k — the Erdős–Graham theorem, via Szemerédi's theorem, which is not formalized — it gives H(k) > (Ck)^k for all large k, i.e. H(k)^{1/k}/k → ∞; that conditional conclusion is stated as H_divergence, with the existence as an explicit hypothesis on H(k) = sInf {N | canonical}. The fourth compared theorem is the explicit bound behind the argument: for k ≥ 12 there is a prime p with (k−1)/2 < p ≤ k−1 such that every canonical N exceeds p^(p − ⌊k/3⌋)·⌊⌊k/3⌋^(k−1)/(16k²)⌋ (integer divisions, as in the Lean statement). The proof composes the Erdős–Lovász lower bound W(r,k) − 1 ≥ r^(k−1)/(16k²) for van der Waerden numbers (obtained from the symmetric Lovász local lemma, applied with a number of colours r₀ = ⌊k/3⌋ growing with k), the restricted Blankenship–Cummings–Taranchuk recurrence W(r,k) − 1 ≥ p·(W(r−1,k) − 1) for primes r ≤ p ≤ k, Bertrand's postulate, and elementary asymptotics. The paper's main theorem, the explicit rate H(k)^{1/k}/k ≥ (1/e − ε(k))·k/log k obtained with the Baker–Harman–Pintz prime-gap theorem, is not formalized (that theorem is not available in Mathlib); the formalized argument gives the weaker rate k^{1/6−o(1)}, which suffices for the qualitative statement. The compared theorems depend only on propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound.

Problem

Erdős Problem 190 asks whether the canonical van der Waerden number H(k) satisfies H(k)^{1/k}/k → ∞. Here H(k) is the least N such that every finite colouring of {1,…,N} contains a monochromatic or rainbow k-term arithmetic progression. Bae's arXiv:2604.20588 resolves the problem, and the goal is to machine-check its qualitative Corollary 1.2.

Approach

The formalization follows the elementary argument of Section 4.3 of the paper. It combines three ingredients: the Erdős–Lovász lower bound W(r,k) − 1 ≥ r^(k−1)/(16k²), proved via the symmetric Lovász local lemma with r₀ = ⌊k/3⌋ colours; a restricted Blankenship–Cummings–Taranchuk recurrence for primes r ≤ p ≤ k; and Bertrand's postulate. Elementary asymptotics then yield that every canonical N eventually exceeds (Ck)^k. The existence of canonical N, which relies on Szemerédi's theorem, is not formalized and is taken as an explicit hypothesis.

Results

Four compared theorems are proved: - a divergence statement for canonical N, together with its Filter.Eventually form; - the conditional H_divergence; - an explicit prime-based bound for k ≥ 12. These depend only on the standard axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. The paper's sharper rate (1/e − ε)·k/log k, which needs the Baker–Harman–Pintz theorem, is not formalized; the formalized argument gives the weaker rate k^{1/6−o(1)}.
