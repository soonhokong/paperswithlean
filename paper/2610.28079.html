Irrationality exponents of logarithmic numbers: Values, quotients, and graded approximation

Abstract
We establish degree-dependent irrationality-exponent bounds for logarithms at algebraic arguments and sharper bounds when conjugate logarithms match. A two-torus construction gives an upper bound of three for ratios of logarithms of multiplicatively independent positive rational numbers. We also treat normalized quadratic logarithms and circular logarithms. The rational-logarithm and rational-power exponent-two cases were treated earlier by Liu, Jiang and Zhang; we retain them within the broader framework and its Lean verification. Starting from the interpolation and determinant method in OpenAI's September 24, 2026 preprint on the irrationality exponent of $π$, we track the arithmetic cost of algebraic embeddings and the effects of matching conjugate logarithms. A second, graded Pade construction gives near-optimal measures for powers and bounded-degree polynomial values at sufficiently small logarithmic arguments, with estimates uniform in both coefficient height and rational denominator. We state the branch, independence, parameter-order, and approximation-field hypotheses explicitly. The Lean supplement has reviewed endpoint correspondences for all 62 numbered results: 53 direct checked endpoints or compositions, and nine applications with named classical theorem inputs. Native proofs of those inputs and separate formalizations of every unnumbered assertion are not supplied. Appendix A gives the exact declarations, hypotheses, and verification evidence.
Problem

The paper bounds irrationality exponents of logarithms at algebraic arguments, of ratios of logarithms, and of powers and polynomial values of small logarithmic arguments. These cases extend earlier exponent-two results for rational logarithms.

Approach

It adapts the separated-weight interpolation and determinant method from an earlier proof that mu(pi)=2. The argument tracks the arithmetic cost of algebraic embeddings and the effect of matching conjugate logarithms. A two-torus construction handles quotients of logarithms. A graded Padé construction gives uniform bounds for powers. The main results are machine-checked in a Lean supplement whose declarations are listed in an appendix.

Results

For algebraic a, mu(log a) is at most 2d, and it equals 2 when a positive power of a is rational. For multiplicatively independent positive rationals a and b, 2 <= mu(log a/log b) <= 3. Further results give mu = 2 for normalized quadratic logarithms and for arctan of nonzero rationals.
