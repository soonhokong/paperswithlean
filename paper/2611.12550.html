A Strict Upper Bound for Three-Point Weighted Selection in Planar Vector-Valued Regression

Sergey Volkov

TL;DR

Abstract
We prove $F_{\mathrm{weighted}}(2,2,3)<11/6$ for minimum-Frobenius-norm least-squares regression with two input and two output coordinates, improving the upper bound $15/8$. More precisely, a universal $\varepsilon_0>0$ satisfies $\mathcal T_3\le(5/6-\varepsilon_0)E$ for every finite residual frame. The proof combines an exact scalar certificate, rigidity of equality in planar selection, and a compactness argument that controls disappearing weights. Together with the known lower bound, this gives $13/8\le F_{\mathrm{weighted}}(2,2,3)<11/6$. No numerical value of $\varepsilon_0$ is claimed.
Problem

The constant F_weighted(2,2,3) for three-point weighted data selection in minimum-Frobenius-norm least-squares regression, with two input and two output coordinates, was known to lie between 13/8 and 15/8. The goal is a strict improvement of the upper bound.

Approach

The proof uses Zhang's residual-frame formulation, interpolation hulls, star localization and a seven-atom support reduction. It combines an exact scalar certificate from a planar selection lemma, rigidity showing equality cannot occur, and a compactness argument that controls disappearing weights. The scalar moment selection, its anisotropy bound, scalar sharpness and an algebraic obstruction are formalized in Lean 4 with Mathlib, using only standard axioms. The paper states the proof was produced by an AI model.

Results

A universal ε0>0 gives T_3 ≤ (5/6−ε0)E for every finite residual frame. Hence 13/8 ≤ F_weighted(2,2,3) < 11/6; no numerical value of ε0 is claimed.
