Singularities in global pluripotential theory

Mingchen Xia

TL;DR
All statements of the book on plurisubharmonic singularities are formalized in Lean 4, with acknowledged formalization help from collaborators.
Abstract
This book is an expanded version of the author's lecture notes at the Institute for Advanced Study in Mathematics (IASM) at Zhejiang University. We systematically develop the theory of plurisubharmonic singularities on compact Kähler manifolds and relate it to the non-Archimedean pluripotential theory and toric pluripotential theory.
Problem

Plurisubharmonic singularities on compact Kähler manifolds are hard to classify locally. Known results are spread across three related settings: Kähler, non-Archimedean (Berkovich), and toric pluripotential theory.

Approach

The book builds a unified theory of global singularities using three equivalence relations: singularity type, P-equivalence, and I-equivalence. It centres on I-good potentials and uses tools such as an analytic Bertini theorem, trace operators, b-divisors and partial Okounkov bodies. The author states that all statements in the book have been formalized in Lean 4.

Results

An equivalence theorem gives three characterizations of I-good potentials: limits of analytic singularities, a mass–volume identity, and agreement of envelopes. Further results include toric theory for big line bundles, non-Archimedean applications, and convergence of partial Bergman kernels with arbitrary singular weights.
