The Cranston–Li–Su–Wang–Xu (2026) conjecture is true

Dave Goel

math.CO Oct 29, 2026 · v2
formalization combinatorics
Read PDF arXiv abstract Code
TL;DR
All theorems on reconfiguration of nowhere-zero flows in even graphs are formalized in Lean 4, with a public repository of the proofs.
Abstract
Let $A$ be a finite abelian group. A nowhere-zero $A$-flow of a graph can be changed along a cycle: going around the cycle, add a fixed non-zero element of $A$ to the value of every edge that points forward and subtract it from the value of every edge that points backward. The Cranston–Li–Su–Wang–Xu (2026) conjecture on the reconfiguration of nowhere-zero flows states that if $A$ has at least four elements, then in every graph in which all degrees are even, any two nowhere-zero $A$-flows are joined by a chain of such changes through nowhere-zero $A$-flows. It also states that, for every integer $k\ge 4$, the same holds for integer flows whose values are between $1$ and $k-1$ in absolute value, where each change adds a non-zero integer. We prove this conjecture. The proof is by induction on the number of edges. If a flow enters a vertex along one edge and leaves it along another edge with the same value, the two edges can be replaced by a single edge; this gives a smaller graph. The main step is to change every flow into a flow with such a pair of edges. For $A$-flows we do this when all degrees are at most $4$; repeatedly replacing a vertex of degree at least $6$ by a triangle, following Cranston et al., reduces the general case to this one. For integer flows, we show that, in a graph in which all degrees are even, any two nowhere-zero flows with values in the cyclic group $\mathbb{Z}_k$, $k\ge 4$, are joined by changes that add only $1$, $-1$, $2$ or $-2$, and that such changes lift to integer flows. All theorems of this paper are also proved in the Lean 4 proof assistant.
Problem

The Cranston–Li–Su–Wang–Xu (2026) conjecture states that in every graph with all degrees even, any two nowhere-zero A-flows (|A|≥4) are connected by cycle steps through nowhere-zero flows. It makes the same claim for integer k-flows with k≥4. Previously this was known only for groups of even order, for very large groups, for even k, or for planar graphs.

Approach

The proof is by induction on the number of edges. A pair of edges carrying the same value through a vertex (a balanced pair) can be split off, which gives a smaller graph. The main step reaches a balanced pair for graphs with degrees at most 4, and vertices of higher degree are reduced by replacing them with triangles. For integer flows, Z_k-flows are connected using steps of ±1 and ±2, and these steps are then lifted to integer flows.

Results

Both parts of the conjecture are proved for all finite abelian groups with |A|≥4 and all integers k≥4. All results are machine-checked in Lean 4 using only the standard axioms.
