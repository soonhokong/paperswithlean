Abstract
A machine-checked Lean 4 formalization of the principal population-gap perturbation results of Yi Yu, Tengyao Wang and Richard J. Samworth, "A useful variant of the Davis-Kahan theorem for statisticians" (Biometrika 102 (2015) 315-323). This single Palomar entry compares both conclusions of Theorem 2 and both displays of Corollary 1 for eigenspaces of real symmetric matrices, together with the right- and left-singular-subspace conclusions of Theorem 3 for general real matrices and two supporting equivalences between its Gram-eigenvector formulation and the paper's paired singular-vector equations. The common idea is that every separation hypothesis in Theorems 2 and 3 is stated entirely in terms of the population object. Theorem 2 uses the population eigenvalue gap min(lambda_(r-1)-lambda_r, lambda_s-lambda_(s+1)); Theorem 3 applies the same idea to the squared singular spectrum, with the additional factor (2 sigma_1 + ||A-hat-A||_op). No sample eigenvalue or singular-value gap is assumed, so the sample frame is arbitrary among admissible orthonormal frames at the selected block, including under multiplicity. The selected results have deliberately different source dispositions. Theorem 2 is source-exact. Corollary 1 writes out the unit normalization inherited from its introduction as the d=1 case of Theorem 2; the standalone printed display omits that normalization and its second conclusion is false without it. Theorem 3 is source-corrected: its printed convention sigma^2_(rank(A)+1) := -infinity makes the denominator infinite at a rank boundary and yields a false assertion. The compared statement instead uses the ambient Gram-spectrum convention used by the paper's own proof, with singular values zero past rank, while retaining the printed restriction 1 <= r <= s <= rank(A). Because this one entry contains that correction, its source relationship is conservatively recorded as `adapts`.

Problem

Yu, Wang and Samworth (2015) proved a Davis-Kahan variant for statisticians. It bounds eigenspace and singular-subspace perturbations using only population gaps. The goal is a machine-checked account of Theorem 2, Corollary 1 and Theorem 3, identifying where the printed statements need adjustment.

Approach

A Lean 4 formalization states both conclusions of Theorem 2 and both displays of Corollary 1 for real symmetric matrices. It also states the right- and left-singular-subspace conclusions of Theorem 3 for general real matrices, plus two equivalences between Gram-eigenvector and paired singular-vector formulations. All separation hypotheses involve only the population object. Sample frames are therefore arbitrary admissible orthonormal frames, including under multiplicity.

Results

Theorem 2 is formalized exactly as printed. Corollary 1 needs the unit normalization from its introduction, since its second conclusion is false without it. Theorem 3 is corrected: the printed -infinity convention at the rank boundary gives a false assertion, so the formal statement uses zero singular values past rank, as in the paper's own proof.
