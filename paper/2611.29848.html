Circle-Pattern Linear Systems for the Twenty Trivalent Delaunay Types of a Genus-Two Surface: Rank, Feasibility and Polytope Dimension

TL;DR

Abstract
The hyperelliptic quotient of a closed genus-two Riemann surface is a hyperbolic sphere with six cone points of angle pi. Its Delaunay triangulation T is dual to a trivalent Voronoi graph G of one of twenty combinatorial types. For each type we study the angle system Ax = 1, x >= 0, where A is the vertex-edge incidence matrix of T and pi x_e is the angle subtended at a cone point by the Voronoi edge dual to e, equivalently the intersection angle of the two circumcircles adjacent across e. We prove that rank A = 6 for all twenty types, including the six whose triangulation has a loop, so the affine solution space always has the dimension of Teichmuller space. The polytope P = {Ax = 1, x >= 0} behaves differently. It is six-dimensional for exactly ten types, the ten Delaunay-realizable ones. For nine types it has dimension one, two or three, because non-negativity forces some wedge angles to vanish, and for the type of signature (2,2,2,2,8,8) it is empty. We show that five of the nine and the empty type never occur as Delaunay triangulations, and that the other four occur only as triangulations of non-trivalent Delaunay decompositions. Every vertex of every P is half-integral, as for fractional perfect matching polytopes. For the ten full-dimensional types every facet x_e = 0 lies at an edge where the Whitehead move is admissible, though not conversely, and the move identifies the facets of the two polytopes; these identifications pair the facet orbits of the ten cells under their automorphism groups. The free unfolding of the gluing grows at rate about 7.093. All finite statements are checked by an independent program, and the rank, infeasibility, forced coordinates, dimensions, vertices and facets are verified in Lean 4.
Problem

The hyperelliptic quotient of a genus-two surface is a hyperbolic sphere with six cone points, and its Delaunay triangulation falls into one of twenty trivalent combinatorial types. For each type the paper studies the rank of the angle system Ax=1 and the dimension and feasibility of the polytope Ax=1, x>=0.

Approach

Rank is computed through an incidence-matrix lemma extended to graphs with loops. Forced coordinates are found by linear programming and exact rational arithmetic, and vertices are characterized via a Balinski-type half-integrality argument. Polytope facets are compared with admissible Whitehead moves. The finite statements are checked by a Python script and formalized in Lean 4: omega proves the forced values and infeasibility, and native_decide handles the remaining finite computations.

Results

The rank is 6 for all twenty types. The polytope is 6-dimensional for exactly the ten Delaunay-realizable types, has dimension 1 to 3 for nine types, and is empty for signature (2,2,2,2,8,8). All vertices are half-integral, and every facet lies at an admissible Whitehead-move edge.
