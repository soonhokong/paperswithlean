The bias interval of the elephant random walk

Abstract
Guérin, Laulin, Raschel, and Simon conjectured that the initial biases giving the superdiffusive elephant random walk a bimodal limit law form an interval centered at one half, and they described the problem as challenging. We prove this. For every superdiffusive memory the limit density has two local maxima exactly when the bias lies in a proper open interval, and outside it, including its endpoints, the density has a unique local maximum. The interval is nonempty exactly above a single memory threshold, numerically about $0.6688$. Near this threshold $a_*$ the distance between the two modes is of order $(a-a_*)^{1/2}$, and the width of the interval is of order $(a-a_*)^{3/2}$. The bias boundary is continuous and tends to zero at least quadratically fast as memory tends to one. The proof bounds the sign changes of the first and second differences of the finite walk, including those created at the boundary, and passes them to the limit.
Problem

Guérin, Laulin, Raschel and Simon conjectured that the initial biases giving the superdiffusive elephant random walk a bimodal limit density form an open interval centered at 1/2. The paper also asks for which memory parameters this interval is nonempty.

Approach

The proof bounds the sign changes of the first and second differences of the finite walk's count distribution, including changes created at the boundary, using a recurrence with positive interior coefficients. These bounds are passed to the limit density. Interval-arithmetic certificates computed with Arb give the numerical constants. Lean files verify the finite sign arguments and the analytic threshold proof, taking the classical weak limit as a named hypothesis.

Results

For every superdiffusive memory, the limit density has exactly two local maxima precisely when the bias lies in an open interval (b_a, 1-b_a), and is unimodal otherwise. The interval is nonempty exactly for memory above a threshold a_* ≈ 0.6688. Near a_*, the mode separation scales as (a-a_*)^{1/2} and the interval width as (a-a_*)^{3/2}.
