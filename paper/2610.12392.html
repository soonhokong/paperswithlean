Tiling 3D Space by (Translates of) a Single Polycube is Undecidable

Erik D. Demaine, Stefan Langerman

cs.CG Oct 8, 2026 · v2 math.CO math.MG
formalization mathlib combinatorics logic
Read PDF arXiv abstract Code
TL;DR
The co-RE-completeness proof for single-polycube tiling is fully formalized in Lean 4, building on Mathlib and a Lean formalization of Wang tiling undecidability.
Abstract
We prove co-RE-completeness, and thus undecidability, of the following problem: given a single (connected) polycube, decide whether it tiles 3D Euclidean space by translations. The same result holds if we also allow rotations and/or reflections. We reduce from Wang tiling using the decorated two-prime Sudoku construction of Greenfeld and Tao and a cyclic encoding adapted from OpenAI's 3D aperiodic tile, and apply a reduction of Kim to make the prototile connected (via faces). Dimension three is optimal: translational monotiling is known to be decidable in $\mathbb{Z}^2$ and for a single (possibly disconnected) polyomino in $\mathbb{R}^2$.
Problem

The question is whether deciding if a single connected polycube tiles 3D space by translations, possibly also allowing rotations or reflections, is decidable. Translational monotiling is already known to be decidable in 2D.

Approach

The proof reduces from Wang tiling through a chain of five reductions. It uses the decorated two-prime Sudoku construction of Greenfeld and Tao, a cyclic encoding adapted from OpenAI's 3D aperiodic tile, and Kim's reduction to make the prototile face-connected. The entire proof is formalized in Lean 4 on top of Mathlib and lean-wang, with a short trusted file Abstract.lean stating the main theorems. AI tools were used heavily to find, write, and formalize the proof.

Results

The problem is co-RE-complete, and therefore undecidable, in both Z^3 and R^3. Dimension three is optimal for a single prototile. The Lean proof is checked to rely only on Lean's three standard axioms.
