A Solution to the Birkhoff Problem

Kevin Schlegel

math.RT Nov 15, 2026 · v1

Read PDF arXiv abstract Code
TL;DR
Matrix-equation computations reducing the classification were carried out by an AI model and formally verified in Lean 4 using Mathlib.
Abstract
For a commutative uniserial ring $R$ of length $n$ we describe the indecomposable objects in the monomorphism category of $R$ independent of the specific ring for $n\leq 5$ and only dependent on the residue field for $n = 6$. Thus, the classification problem for the monomorphism category of $\mathbb{Z}/p^6 \mathbb{Z}$ reduces to the known case of $\mathbb{F}_p[x]/(x^6)$, resolving the Birkhoff Problem.
Problem

The Birkhoff Problem asks for a classification of indecomposable objects in the monomorphism category of Z/p^6 Z, which describes subgroups of finite abelian p-groups. It was the only unresolved case: lengths up to 5 were already classified, and lengths of 7 or more are wild.

Approach

Monomorphism categories are realized as torsion-free classes. Ideal-quotient results for torsion-free classes over Artin algebras show that S(R)/<S(R')> is equivalent to the torsionless modules over a finite-dimensional algebra B. This reduces computations C1–C3 to matrix equations over R. An AI model (GPT-6 Astra) solved those equations, including Hom-space computations, and verified them in Lean 4 with Mathlib.

Results

For a commutative uniserial ring of length up to 5, the indecomposable objects of S(R) are independent of the specific ring; for length 6 they depend only on the residue field. Hence S(Z/p^6 Z) reduces to the known classification for F_p[x]/(x^6), resolving the Birkhoff Problem.
