Poisson statistics for one-dimensional Anderson–Bernoulli Schrödinger operators

Yi Han

TL;DR
The main Poisson limit theorem has an end-to-end Lean 4 formalization using Mathlib, checked by Lean's kernel, with a public repository.
Abstract
For a one-dimensional discrete Schrödinger operator with bounded nonconstant iid potential, we prove a unit-intensity Poisson limit for local eigenvalues unfolded by the integrated density of states. The limit holds at every energy in the interior of the almost-sure spectrum. In particular, it holds for Bernoulli potentials at every fixed nonzero coupling. The proof uses auxiliary phase functions built from inverse stationary measures to obtain an exact IDS drift. Projective contraction then yields exponential crossing times, and a spatial subdivision argument gives independent Poisson counts in disjoint energy windows. At energies where the IDS has a finite positive derivative, the usual linear rescaling gives the same limit.
Problem

The goal is Poisson statistics for local eigenvalues of one-dimensional discrete Schrödinger operators with bounded nonconstant iid potentials, including Bernoulli potentials at every nonzero coupling. For Bernoulli potentials, the atomic law blocks the classical Minami-estimate approach.

Approach

Auxiliary phase functions built from inverse stationary measures give an exact drift for the integrated density of states (IDS). Projective contraction yields exponential laws for crossing times of coupled phase gaps. Renewal arguments then give scalar Poisson counts, and spatial subdivision gives asymptotic independence across disjoint energy windows. The proof of the main theorem is formalized end-to-end in Lean 4 with Mathlib.

Results

Eigenvalues unfolded by the IDS converge to a unit-intensity Poisson point process at every energy in the interior of the almost-sure spectrum. Where the IDS has a finite positive derivative, the usual linear rescaling gives the same limit. The Lean formalization covers Theorem 1.1 and Proposition 2.2.
