Bounded Relative Boundary Implies Narrow DNF Approximation

Chenghua Liu, Boning Meng

cs.CC Aug 31, 2026 · v2 cs.DM
formalization combinatorics probability
Read PDF arXiv abstract Code
TL;DR
The authors, with AI assistance, completed a Lean formalization of their proofs of the main DNF approximation theorem and Friedgut's conjecture.
Abstract
We prove Friedgut's conjecture (J. Amer. Math. Soc. 12 (1999)) that every increasing family in the $p$-biased discrete cube with bounded relative boundary can be approximated arbitrarily well by one whose minimal elements have size bounded independently of the dimension and the bias. For $0<p\le 1/2$, every increasing Boolean function with total resampling influence at most $K$ is $\varepsilon$-close under $μ_p^n$ to a monotone DNF of width $\exp(O((K+1)^2/\varepsilon^2))$, from which the full conjecture follows for all $p\in(0,1)$. We build on Hatami's pseudo-junta theorem (Ann. of Math. 176 (2012)), whose approximator need not be increasing. Our randomized shifts produce an increasing approximator that is measurable with respect to a controlled refinement of Hatami's adaptive partition. Retaining only short positive certificates yields the desired narrow DNF.
Problem

Friedgut conjectured in 1999 that every increasing family in the p-biased discrete cube with bounded relative boundary can be approximated by a family whose minimal elements have size bounded independently of the dimension and the bias. Hatami's pseudo-junta theorem gives an adaptive approximation, but its approximator need not be increasing.

Approach

Starting from Hatami's construction, made explicit with arity and activation-load bounds, the authors apply randomized bias-matched coordinate shifts. These choose an upper or lower shift with probabilities 1-p and p, which makes the approximator increasing without increasing the expected error. The result stays measurable with respect to a controlled refinement of Hatami's adaptive partition. Active 1-coordinates then form positive certificates, and keeping only the short certificates gives a monotone DNF. The proofs were formalized in Lean.

Results

For 0<p≤1/2, every increasing Boolean function with total resampling influence at most K is ε-close under μ_p^n to a monotone DNF of width exp(O((K+1)^2/ε^2)). The full Friedgut conjecture follows for all p in (0,1).
