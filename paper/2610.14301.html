Compact Quasi-Metric Spaces Are Expand-Contract Plastic

Abstract
On a totally bounded metric space, every non-contractive self-map is an isometry. For quasi-metric spaces this fails. Here quasi-metrics are $T_1$: $d(x,y)=0$ implies $x=y$. Zavarzina gave a hereditarily precompact counterexample and asked whether rigidity holds at least for bijections. The author's toll spiral (arXiv:2610.13973) shows that it does not. We prove that compactness restores it: every compact quasi-metric space is expand-contract plastic, that is, each of its bijective non-contractive self-maps is an isometry. Countable compactness suffices, as does compactness of the conjugate topology. Bijectivity is needed, even on a compact, hereditarily precompact space. For arbitrary non-contractive self-maps, we prove rigidity when the space is hereditarily precompact and its conjugate topology is sequentially compact, in particular when it is also Yoneda complete; neither hypothesis can be dropped. Such maps are then surjective. In both theorems the $T_1$ axiom can be weakened to $R_0$, $d(x,y)=0$ implies $d(y,x)=0$, if hereditary precompactness is taken to mean that every sequence has a left K-Cauchy subsequence; surjectivity then holds up to points at distance 0 both ways. The weaker $T_0$ axiom is not enough: there is a $T_0$ space, compact in both topologies and satisfying all the other hypotheses of both theorems, with a bijective non-contractive non-isometry. Finally, for an asymmetric norm in finite dimension, the classical theorem makes the symmetric unit ball and sphere rigid for all non-contractive self-maps, and the forward ones if the norm is $T_1$. In infinite dimension, the symmetric ball and sphere need not be plastic, and for the forward ones we pose the quasi-metric form of the unit-ball question of Cascales, Kadets, Orihuela and Wingler. The two theorems and the counterexamples of Section 6 are machine-checked in Lean, except the steps listed at the end.
Problem

Zavarzina asked whether every bijective non-contractive self-map of a precompact quasi-metric space is an isometry. The toll spiral answers this negatively, which leaves open which hypotheses do restore rigidity for quasi-metric spaces.

Approach

The paper proves that compactness, countable compactness, or compactness of the conjugate topology forces bijective non-contractive maps to be isometries, using recurrence (return) lemmas for orbits. A second theorem covers arbitrary non-contractive maps under hereditary precompactness plus sequential compactness of the conjugate topology. Counterexamples show the hypotheses cannot be dropped, including a T0 space with a non-isometric bijection. The main theorems, the counterexamples and the finite-dimensional asymmetric-ball mechanism are formalized in Lean 4/Mathlib, apart from some listed steps.

Results

Every compact quasi-metric space is expand-contract plastic, and the separation axiom can be weakened from T1 to R0 but not to T0. Finite-dimensional asymmetric unit balls and spheres are rigid. In infinite dimension the symmetric ball and sphere need not be plastic, and the quasi-metric form of the unit-ball question for the forward ones is posed.
