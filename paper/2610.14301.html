Compact Quasi-Metric Spaces Are Expand-Contract Plastic

Thomas Duzer

math.FA Oct 9, 2026 · v2
formalization mathlib topology analysis
Read PDF arXiv abstract Code
TL;DR
The two main rigidity theorems for quasi-metric spaces and most counterexamples are machine-checked in Lean 4 over Mathlib, archived on Zenodo.
Abstract
On a totally bounded metric space every non-contractive self-map is an isometry. For quasi-metric spaces, taken T_1 throughout, this fails: Zavarzina gave a hereditarily precompact counterexample and asked whether bijectivity restores rigidity; the toll spiral shows that it does not. We prove that compactness does: every compact quasi-metric space is expand-contract plastic, that is, each of its bijective non-contractive self-maps is an isometry. Countable compactness suffices, as does compactness of the conjugate topology. Bijectivity cannot be dropped: a compact, hereditarily precompact quasi-metric on the natural numbers makes the shift non-contractive and not an isometry. For arbitrary non-contractive self-maps we prove rigidity when the space is hereditarily precompact and its conjugate topology is sequentially compact, in particular when it is hereditarily precompact and Yoneda complete; neither hypothesis can be dropped. In both theorems the T_1 axiom can be weakened to R_0, with hereditary precompactness read sequentially, but not to T_0: we construct a T_0 space with all the remaining hypotheses of both theorems, carrying a bijective non-contractive self-map that is not an isometry. Under R_0 the two-sided case reduces to the metric theorem; the one-sided theorems do not, and plasticity does not pass to the symmetrization even for compact spaces. In finite dimension the unit ball of a T_1 asymmetric normed space, forward or symmetric, and its sphere are rigid for every non-contractive self-map; without T_1 the forward ball need not be; in infinite dimension the symmetric ball and sphere are not plastic in general, and the forward versions carry the quasi-metric form of the unit-ball question of Cascales, Kadets, Orihuela and Wingler, which is posed. The two theorems are machine-checked in Lean, and so are the counterexamples, apart from a few statements listed at the end.
Problem

Zavarzina asked whether bijective non-contractive self-maps of precompact quasi-metric spaces must be isometries, generalizing the Freudenthal–Hurewicz rigidity theorem for totally bounded metric spaces. The toll spiral shows they need not be, which leaves open which hypotheses do restore rigidity.

Approach

The authors prove recurrence lemmas for the orbits of non-contractive maps using sequential compactness and the R_0 axiom, and derive two rigidity theorems from them. They construct counterexamples, including toll-spiral variants, a T_0 three-layer space, and a shift on the natural numbers, to show that each hypothesis is needed. The theorems and the main counterexample are formalized in Lean 4 over Mathlib (LowerLimits.lean, UpperLimits.lean, ShiftExample.lean). A few cited or auxiliary statements are listed as not mechanized.

Results

Every compact (or countably compact, or conjugate-compact) quasi-metric space is expand-contract plastic. Every non-contractive self-map is an isometry when the space is hereditarily precompact and its conjugate topology is sequentially compact. Neither hypothesis can be dropped, and T_1 can be weakened to R_0 but not to T_0. The Lean proofs depend only on the standard axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound.
