Explicit Absolutely Maximally Entangled states in dimensions twice a prime power

Palash Pandya

quant-ph Nov 12, 2026 · v2
formalization quantum
Read PDF arXiv abstract Code
TL;DR
Cites third-party Lean formal proofs of AME(9,10), AME(11,10) and AME(9,6) states, including an input essential to the nine-party classification.
Abstract
Absolutely maximally entangled (AME) states are pure multipartite states with maximally mixed reductions on every set of at most half the parties. We construct explicit families in local dimension $2q$, twice an odd prime power $q$, using an established coupling of qubits and $q$-level systems. The $q$-level states remove interference between branches, so the qubit states need maximally mixed reductions only on average. This gives AME$(9,2q)$ and AME$(11,2q)$ for every odd prime power $q\geq5$, AME$(13,2q)$ for $q\geq17$, AME$(15,2q)$ for $q\geq9$, and AME$(17,2q)$ for $q\geq67$. For every odd $N\geq3$, the same coupling gives AME$(N,2q)$ under the sufficient condition $q\geq\max\{N,2^{N-2}\}$ on the odd prime power $q$. Within this coupling, the smallest such $q$ must grow exponentially with $N$. Combined with known inputs and products, these families complete the nine-party classification: for integer $d\geq2$, an AME$(9,d)$ state exists if and only if $d\geq3$. Choosing the branch pairing also gives AME$(15,2p)$ states with a cyclic shift of the parties compensated by local unitaries, for every prime $p\equiv1\pmod{15}$.
Problem

Absolutely maximally entangled (AME) states in local dimension 2q cannot come from tensor products, because qubit AME states exist only for N=2,3,5,6. Explicit constructions were missing for many party counts, and the nine-party existence classification was open.

Approach

A coupling identifies a qubit and a q-level system at each site with one 2q-level system. Branches pair N-qubit states with q-level states whose cross blocks vanish, so the qubit mixture only needs maximally mixed reductions on average. Stabilizer-code qubit mixtures and extended-Cauchy parent states over finite fields supply the ingredients. Third-party Lean proofs (Atlas library, Kitamura) formally verify specific instances such as AME(9,10), AME(11,10) and AME(9,6).

Results

The construction gives AME(9,2q) and AME(11,2q) for q≥5, AME(13,2q) for q≥17, AME(15,2q) for q≥9 and AME(17,2q) for q≥67. It also gives AME(N,2q) for every odd N whenever q≥max{N,2^{N-2}}. Combined with known inputs, an AME(9,d) state exists if and only if d≥3, and cyclically symmetric AME(15,2p) states exist for primes p≡1 mod 15.
