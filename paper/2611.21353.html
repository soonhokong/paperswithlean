An Adaptive Reduced Speed of Light Approximation

Chong-Chong He, Mark R. Krumholz, Benjamin D. Wibking

astro-ph.IM Nov 16, 2026 · v1 astro-ph.GA

TL;DR
Stability-bound proofs from the von Neumann analysis, including the unequal-opacity extension and appendix lemmas, are machine-checked in Lean 4 with Mathlib.
Abstract
The reduced speed of light approximation (RSLA) is a common approach in radiation-hydrodynamic (RHD) simulations of non-relativistic systems where the gas speed $v_{\rm gas} \ll c$. In the RSLA one eases the severe time step constraint imposed by the speed of light $c$ by replacing it with a reduced value $\hat{c}$ chosen so that $v_{\rm gas} \ll \hat{c} \ll c$. This approach works well in flows with low or moderate optical depth, but fails in optically thick media, where it makes thermal relaxation too slow and gives incorrect RHD wave modes. Here we propose an Adaptive Reduced Speed of Light Approximation (ARSA) that removes these problems by replacing the global speed reduction factor $φ_0 \equiv \hat{c}/c$ with a local, cell-by-cell reduction factor $φ$ derived from a von Neumann stability analysis of the implicit-explicit update used by modern two-moment RHD codes. The resulting limiter has no tunable parameters, can be computed using only local operations, and improves on the classic RSLA by allowing $φ\to 1$ in cells where a reduced speed of light is not required because the exchange with the gas damps unstable modes, allowing recovery of the correct thermal relaxation and wave-damping rates in highly-opaque regions. We compare ARSA to the classic RSLA in a range of standard RHD test problems, and show that in opaque regions where gas holds most of the heat capacity the ARSA is much more accurate than the RSLA at the same time step, or that it can achieve the same accuracy as the RSLA with up to 100 times fewer time steps. However, gains are limited in regions where radiation holds a large share of the heat capacity, as it does in some astrophysical systems.
Problem

The reduced speed of light approximation (RSLA) eases time-step limits in radiation-hydrodynamics simulations. It fails in optically thick media, where it slows thermal relaxation and gives incorrect wave modes.

Approach

The authors replace the global reduction factor with a local, cell-by-cell factor. This factor comes from a von Neumann stability analysis of the IMEX PD-ARS update with HLL transport used by two-moment codes. The resulting closed-form limiter has no tunable parameters and uses only local operations. The key stability results are machine-checked in Lean 4 with Mathlib, and the scheme is implemented and tested in the Julia code Honghu.jl.

Results

The adaptive scheme (ARSA) recovers the unmodified equations in cells more than a few photon mean free paths thick when gas dominates the heat capacity. In those opaque regions it is much more accurate than RSLA at the same time step, or matches RSLA accuracy with up to 100 times fewer steps. Gains are limited where radiation holds a large share of the heat capacity.
