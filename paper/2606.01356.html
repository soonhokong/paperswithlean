A Formally Verified Library of Mathematical Finance in Lean 4

Raphael Coelho

TL;DR
Builds a sorry-free Lean 4 library of mathematical finance on Mathlib and BrownianMotion, with axiom-audit gates and a faithfulness classification of every result.
Abstract
We describe a library of mathematical finance in the Lean 4 proof assistant, built on Mathlib and the BrownianMotion package. Its benchmark records 804 results from the literature, each with a Lean statement, a status, and a published account of what the formalization leaves out: 766 are proved in full, 18 restate an upstream theorem, and 20 are reduced cores that prove less than the result they are named after. The library is about 158,000 lines of Lean, contains no sorry, and a build-time audit pins every library constant that a benchmark proof cites to Lean's three standard axioms. The Itô integral is constructed rather than assumed. On it the library proves Itô's formula for C2 functions of Itô processes as an identity of processes, Lévy's characterisation, martingale representation, Girsanov's theorem for bounded predictable drifts, which yields a Brownian motion for the filtration when the driver's paths are almost surely continuous, and unique L2 solutions of Lipschitz stochastic differential equations on every horizon. These results reach the contracts a user prices. In a contract language that separates payoffs from models, the Black-Scholes call is replicated by its delta hedge up to every date before maturity, and every fixed-dated contract on the physical Black-Scholes asset has, under the Girsanov measure of a horizon containing its dates, its risk-neutral value. An audit of faithfulness makes the logical status of every claim checkable, and its review has caught what mechanical gates cannot see, including a convergence theorem whose hypothesis could never be satisfied. Nearly all of the mathematics is classical, and what a formal base over it produces is certified unification of known results; two contributed developments, by Robert Martin, address problems the library's own survey had listed as open. The contribution is methodological and infrastructural.
Problem

Mathematical finance has barely been formalized in proof assistants. Prior work covers narrow pieces such as the discrete CRR model, AMMs, or a single Itô-to-Black–Scholes derivation, and none has a way to state how faithful a formalization is to the mathematics it claims.

Approach

A Lean 4 library spanning eleven areas is built on Mathlib and the BrownianMotion package. It constructs a continuous L² Itô integral via the adapted Itô isometry and norm-preserving extension, and derives the risk-neutral measure through static and dynamic Girsanov changes of measure. Pricing results are organized around a linear no-arbitrage functional, the Garman normal form, and Brownian grounding bridges. A four-tier faithfulness classification (full, library_wrapper, reduced_core, placeholder) is enforced at build time by an AxiomAudit file that pins the #print axioms output of each audited result.

Results

The paper body reports 330 benchmark theorems (298 full, 18 library wrappers, 14 reduced cores) in about 52,300 sorry-free lines resting only on Lean's three standard axioms. The abstract of the latest version reports 804 results (766 proved in full) in about 158,000 lines, including Itô's formula, Lévy's characterisation, martingale representation, Girsanov's theorem, and delta-hedge replication of the Black–Scholes call.
