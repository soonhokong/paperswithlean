The maximum area of the convex hull of a polyhex

Pragyaan Gaur

math.CO Sep 23, 2026 · v2 math.MG
formalization mathlib combinatorics geometry
Read PDF arXiv abstract Code
TL;DR
The upper bound on the polyhex convex hull area is formalised in Lean 4 with Mathlib, with no unproved steps, and released on GitHub.
Abstract
A polyhex is a connected set of n cells of the regular hexagonal tiling, joined edge to edge, where each cell has area one. Kurz conjectured an upper bound for the area of the convex hull of a polyhex, after the corresponding problem for squares was solved. We prove that the convex hull of a polyhex with n cells has area at most one sixth of the ceiling of $n^2+14n/3$, and that for every n this value is attained by a path made of three straight runs of nearly equal length. This proves the conjecture of Kurz and improves his bound by 1/6 when n is divisible by 3. The proof bounds the hull of the cell centres and three of its widths with a spanning tree of the polyhex. The upper bound is formalised in the Lean 4 proof assistant with the Mathlib library. We also show that for every n the extremal polyhex is unique up to rotation, reflection and translation, which an exhaustive search had confirmed for at most 12 cells. The proof of uniqueness was generated by Claude at the request of Sascha Kurz, and it is also formalised in Lean.
Problem

The task is to find the maximum area of the convex hull of a polyhex with n unit-area hexagonal cells, settling a conjecture of Kurz. The analogous problem for polyominoes was solved earlier.

Approach

The hull is written as the Minkowski sum P+H, where P is the hull of the cell centres in axial coordinates and H is the unit hexagon. This gives an area identity in terms of three widths of P. The area and the widths of P are bounded using the edge-direction counts of a spanning tree of the polyhex. The upper bound is formalised in Lean 4/Mathlib using Lebesgue measure, with a single slicing lemma and a cell ordering in place of the spanning tree.

Results

M(n) = ⌈n²+14n/3⌉/6, attained by a three-run path called the washtub. This proves Kurz's conjecture and improves his bound by 1/6 when 3 divides n. The Lean proof uses only the standard axioms, and exhaustive search up to 12 cells confirms the bound and a unique free maximiser for each n.
