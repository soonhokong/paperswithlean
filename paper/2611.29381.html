Gaussian Belief Propagation and Periodic Jacobi Matrices on Trees: Spectral Curve, Antibound Poles, and Fixed Points

Yusuke Watanabe

TL;DR
Part of the scalar theory (sheet count, Perron pole continuation, existence of two positive fixed points, signed degree/parity) is formalized in Lean 4 with Mathlib.
Abstract
Diagonal Green functions of a periodic Jacobi operator on a universal covering tree satisfy the scalar Gaussian belief propagation covariance equations on the finite connected loopless base multigraph. At cycle rank at least two, the Green functions continue to simple antibound poles at both Perron values for arbitrary real potentials and nonzero real weights. This proves the conjecture of Christiansen, Simon and Zinchenko. For positive weights the pole result includes self-loops. On loopless graphs the lower pole is the walk-summability threshold. Each energy strictly between it and the spectral bottom has at least two fixed points with positive definite local beliefs. On a connected simple graph, some coefficients yield a compact component of positive solutions exactly when the graph contains two vertex-disjoint connected subgraphs of cycle rank at least two. The covariance curve is generically irreducible, with one sheet per edge subset with no vertex of degree one. For cycle rank at least two and generic real coefficients, the diagonal Green functions, infinite half-tree $m$-functions and the density-of-states Stieltjes transform all have this degree, answering a question of Avni, Breuer and Simon. Then all eigenvalues of the signed Jacobi matrices are antibound poles. Signed principal submatrices classify finite points with nonzero residue of $(\sum_i V_i) dz$, where $V_i$ are the vertex variances. We give a genus formula. Near zero potentials and unit weights on $K_4$, independently varying the coefficients leaves the cover spectrum an interval. This refutes Avni, Breuer and Simon's conjecture on the density of parameters with all gaps open. In block models, generic irreducibility and a uniqueness criterion extend, and at uniform dimension $p$ the degree can exceed $L^p$ for scalar degree $L$. Part of the scalar theory is formally verified in Lean 4 with Mathlib.
Problem

Diagonal Green functions of periodic Jacobi operators on universal covering trees are algebraic functions. Open conjectures and questions by Christiansen–Simon–Zinchenko and Avni–Breuer–Simon concern their antibound poles, number of sheets, and spectral gaps. These Green functions are linked to fixed points of scalar Gaussian belief propagation.

Approach

The Green functions are identified with solutions of the Gaussian belief propagation covariance equations on the finite base multigraph. The resulting spectral curve is studied algebraically, analyzing sheets at infinity, poles at eigenvalues of signed Jacobi matrices, monodromy and irreducibility, and real positive solutions. Part of the scalar theory is machine-checked in Lean 4 with Mathlib, with the source released on GitHub.

Results

At cycle rank at least two, the Green functions have simple antibound poles at both Perron values, proving the Christiansen–Simon–Zinchenko conjecture. For generic coefficients the curve is irreducible with one sheet per leafless edge subset, which answers Avni–Breuer–Simon's two-sheet question. A K4 example refutes their conjecture on the density of parameters with all gaps open. The paper also gives counts of positive fixed points and a genus formula.
