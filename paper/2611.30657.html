Computational Complexity and Energy Landscapes of Coulomb Crystals

Paco Navarro, Michael Emmerich

cs.CG Nov 23, 2026 · v1 cs.CC math-ph physics.atom-ph
formalization verification physics
Read PDF arXiv abstract Code
TL;DR
The logical skeleton of the NP-hardness reduction for pinned Coulomb crystals is machine-checked in Lean 4 as a conditional correctness theorem.
Abstract
Simulated annealing is widely used to search for ground states of trapped-ion Coulomb crystals and of charges on a sphere (the Thomson problem). Elementary nonuniform bounds clarify the role of the input encoding. For identical ions in a fixed trap, unary ion count and a binary energy threshold define a decision problem in P/poly; NP-hardness would imply a collapse of the polynomial hierarchy. The same holds on the unit sphere. With unary ion count and logarithmic precision parameter, approximate configuration search on the sphere and in a fixed positive-definite harmonic trap admits a solution function in FP/poly. These advice bounds provide no uniform search algorithm. In simulations of up to 100 ions, the number of distinct minima found and the time to reach the best-known minimum both grow approximately exponentially. When ions are pinned to prescribed candidate sites, choosing which sites to occupy to minimize the Coulomb energy is NP-hard in two and three dimensions; a finite-precision promise version is NP-complete. All ions are identical, the interaction is bare Coulomb repulsion, and the hardness is carried by the site positions alone. We extend a previous inverse-square reduction to the Coulomb interaction by deriving its angular law, including the quartic field term, and sharpening two estimates. The construction is an array of quadrupoles whose common monopole term cancels from energy differences between legal configurations. Its energy landscapes exhibit large barriers between nearly degenerate minima. Barrier trees resolve this structure, and simulated annealing becomes trapped above the ground state in the cube example. At laboratory parameters, with individually tunable well depths, the encoding survives numerically on a half-filled square lattice. For Doppler-cooled strontium ions, this requires stiff wells a few to tens of micrometers apart, as in electrode trap arrays.
Problem

Finding ground states of trapped-ion Coulomb crystals and of charges on a sphere (the Thomson problem) is often described as NP-hard, but the claim depends on how instances are encoded. The paper separates free crystals in a fixed trap from pinned crystals with prescribed candidate sites.

Approach

For free crystals, elementary nonuniform arguments place the decision problem in P/poly and approximate search in FP/poly, so NP-hardness under these encodings would collapse the polynomial hierarchy. For pinned crystals, an earlier inverse-square reduction from maximum independent set on planar cubic graphs is extended to the Coulomb interaction using quadrupole selector gadgets. The logical skeleton of this reduction is machine-checked in Lean 4, while the geometric estimates are proved by hand and certified with interval arithmetic. Simulated annealing runs and barrier trees are used to study the energy landscapes.

Results

Choosing occupied sites to minimize Coulomb energy is NP-hard in two and three dimensions, and a finite-precision promise version is NP-complete. In simulations of up to 100 ions, annealing effort grows roughly exponentially, and the hard instances have nearly degenerate minima separated by large barriers. With individually tunable well depths, the encoding survives at laboratory parameters for Doppler-cooled Sr+ ions.
