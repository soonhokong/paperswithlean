Quantitative homogenization for the critical long-range random conductance model

Ahmed Bou-Rabee, Paul Dario

Abstract
We consider the long-range random conductance model on $\mathbb{Z}^d$ at the critical exponent: the jump rate between sites $x$ and $y$ is $\mathbf{a}(\{x,y\}) |x-y|^{-(d+2)}$, where the conductances $\mathbf{a}(\{x,y\})$ are independent, uniformly elliptic and have a common mean. Below the critical exponent $(d+2)$ the walk converges to a stable process; above it, to Brownian motion with diffusive $\sqrt{t}$ scaling. At criticality the second moment of the jump kernel diverges logarithmically. We establish quantitative homogenization of the associated heat equation at the rate $1/\sqrt{|\ln \varepsilon|}$. As a consequence, we deduce annealed convergence of the random walk to Brownian motion under the anomalous $\sqrt{t \log t}$ scaling. Unlike in standard homogenization, the effective diffusivity is determined by the mean conductance alone, with no corrector contribution at leading order.
Problem

The long-range random conductance model on Z^d at the critical exponent d+2 has a jump kernel whose second moment diverges logarithmically. Its large-scale behavior sits between the stable and diffusive regimes and lacked a quantitative homogenization theory.

Approach

The heat equation's lattice operator is split into a deterministic averaged part and a random fluctuation part. The deterministic part is shown consistent with the continuum heat equation up to an error of order 1/kappa_epsilon, and the fluctuations are controlled via independence of the conductances. An annealed invariance principle follows from heat-kernel bounds, Aldous' criterion and convergence of finite-dimensional marginals. All labeled results were formalized in Lean 4 on top of Mathlib and a Lattice-Probability library, with the Lean code written by Claude under the authors' direction.

Results

Quantitative homogenization of the heat equation holds at rate 1/sqrt(|ln epsilon|). The random walk converges to Brownian motion under sqrt(t log t) scaling, with effective diffusivity determined by the mean conductance alone and no corrector contribution at leading order.
