TL;DR
Formalizes heights, Siegel's lemma, Roth's theorem and Schmidt's Subspace Theorem with applications in Lean 4 using Mathlib, sorry-free.
Abstract
A Lean 4 / Mathlib development of Schmidt's Subspace Theorem and the theory beneath it, following Bombieri–Gubler, Heights in Diophantine Geometry (Chapters 1–7), and Schmidt's route in general. Two libraries, sorry-free and within Lean's three standard axioms. ArithmeticHeights builds heights of points, polynomials, matrices and linear subspaces, Northcott and Kronecker, Minkowski's second theorem, Siegel's lemma and the Bombieri–Vaaler theorems. DiophantineApproximation builds on it Roth's theorem over a number field with several places (and Ridout, the p-adic form, targets at infinity, moving targets), the geometry of numbers of approximation domains, and the Subspace Theorem: parametric, with coefficients in K, with algebraic coefficients, for S-integral points, and in Vojta's general-position form. It then proves the applications — Schmidt's simultaneous approximation, a transcendence criterion, unit equations, decomposable-form, Thue–Mahler, hyperelliptic and norm-form equations, the Corvaja–Zannier gcd bound — and Evertse's quantitative steps: systems of inequalities, the gap principles, the count of small solutions, and the passage from height intervals to subspaces. Twenty-six headline statements are compared: seven forms of Siegel's lemma and Bombieri–Vaaler, seven of Roth's theorem, seven of the Subspace Theorem, and five of Evertse's counting of the subspaces.

Problem

Schmidt's Subspace Theorem and the height theory beneath it, as presented in Bombieri–Gubler Chapters 1–7, had not been formalized. Its many variants and Diophantine applications rest on a large body of prerequisite theory.

Approach

Two Lean 4 / Mathlib libraries were built, sorry-free and using only Lean's three standard axioms. ArithmeticHeights develops heights of points, polynomials, matrices and subspaces, Northcott and Kronecker, Minkowski's second theorem, Siegel's lemma and the Bombieri–Vaaler theorems. DiophantineApproximation builds on it with Roth's theorem over number fields and its variants, the geometry of numbers of approximation domains, and several forms of the Subspace Theorem. It then proves applications and Evertse's quantitative steps.

Results

The development covers the parametric, K-coefficient, algebraic-coefficient, S-integral and Vojta general-position forms of the Subspace Theorem. Applications include unit equations, Thue–Mahler, norm-form equations and the Corvaja–Zannier gcd bound. Twenty-six headline statements are compared: seven forms of Siegel/Bombieri–Vaaler, seven of Roth, seven of the Subspace Theorem, and five of Evertse's subspace counting.
