Distilling LLM Feedback for Lean Theorem Proving

Gaetan Narozniak, Gérard Biau, Rémi Munos, Ahmad Rammal, Pierre Marion

cs.AI May 29, 2026 · v2
theorem-proving-ml ai-agents
Read PDF arXiv abstract
TL;DR
Trains LLMs to write Lean 4 proofs with tool access to the Lean compiler and Mathlib search, evaluated on MiniF2F and LeanWorkbook.
Abstract
Post-training for reasoning models typically combines supervised fine-tuning with reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards, most commonly with GRPO. However, this algorithm suffers from sparse rewards, limited exploration, and mode collapse. Building upon recent works on self-distillation, we propose Feedback Distillation, a training method where the model is trained to match, at the token level, its own distribution conditioned on privileged feedback produced by a language model. Feedback Distillation offers token-level supervision and can inject external knowledge. Evaluating our method for Lean4 theorem-proving, we find that Feedback Distillation maintains greater diversity in generated trajectories than GRPO, yielding higher policy entropy and better pass@k scaling. The two methods are complementary: initializing GRPO from a Feedback Distillation checkpoint outperforms either method alone. All in all, our results suggest a promising avenue to improve post-training for complex reasoning.
Problem

GRPO-based reinforcement learning for reasoning models suffers from sparse trajectory-level rewards, limited exploration, and mode collapse. These problems are acute in Lean theorem proving, where the model must explore the large Mathlib library of lemmas.

Approach

Feedback Distillation trains a student model to match, token by token, the distribution of a teacher copy of itself. The teacher is conditioned on textual feedback about the student's attempt, written by an LLM: Claude, or a frozen copy of the model. The teacher is updated by EMA and the loss is a per-token KL divergence. Qwen3-8B and Qwen3.5-9B are trained on LeanWorkbook, using tools to write Lean files, compile them, and regex-search Mathlib.

Results

Feedback Distillation keeps higher policy entropy, scales better with pass@k, and discovers Mathlib lemmas faster than GRPO. Initializing GRPO from a Feedback Distillation checkpoint outperforms either method alone, including with self-feedback. Claude and ground-truth feedback beat raw Lean compiler output as feedback sources.
