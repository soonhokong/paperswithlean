Steelhead: Interleaving Partially Synchronous and Asynchronous Commit Rules on a Shared DAG

George Danezis, Zeno De Angeli, Philipp Jovanovic, Lefteris Kokoris-Kogias, Markus Legner, Alberto Sonnino

TL;DR
Safety and liveness of the Steelhead dual-mode DAG consensus protocol are mechanized in Lean 4.
Abstract
Dual-mode consensus protocols are fast when the network is partially synchronous and remain live under asynchrony. We introduce Steelhead, a dual-mode mechanism that composes a partially synchronous and an asynchronous commit rule over one DAG: every k-th round is decided by the asynchronous rule, whose leader a common coin reveals after the votes, and all other rounds by the partially synchronous rule. Every interval, validators replay the committed DAG under each candidate period, adopt the one with the fewest expected message delays, and fall back to k = 1 when the output stalls; the asynchronous rule applied to the coin rounds alone keeps the protocol live. Steelhead sends no message beyond the DAG's blocks, not even to agree on the period, and opens a coin only on the rounds that need a hidden leader. It is generic over pairs of DAG commit rules that share a committee; we instantiate it with Mysticeti and Mahi-Mahi at n >= 3f+1 and with the two variants of BlueBottle at n >= 5f+1. We prove it safe and live, and provide mechanized proofs in Lean 4. In simulation, Steelhead matches the partially synchronous protocol in a healthy network, stays close to the asynchronous one when network conditions stall the partially synchronous one, and adapts quickly in both directions.
Problem

Dual-mode BFT consensus protocols combine a fast partially synchronous commit rule with an asynchronous one that stays live, but existing designs pay costly overhead for switching between modes. The goal is to interleave both rules on a single DAG without a mode vote or extra messages.

Approach

Steelhead composes a partially synchronous and an asynchronous commit rule over one shared DAG, with every k-th round decided by the asynchronous rule (leader revealed by a common coin) and all others by the synchronous rule. Validators adapt the period k by replaying the committed DAG and picking the candidate with fewest expected message delays, falling back to k=1 when stalled. The design is generic over pairs of DAG commit rules sharing a committee, instantiated with Mysticeti/Mahi-Mahi and BlueBottle variants. Safety and liveness are proven and machine-checked in Lean 4.

Results

Steelhead is proven safe and live, with mechanized Lean 4 proofs (Lemma 1, Lemma 2, Theorem 0.B.1). In simulation it matches the partially synchronous protocol in healthy networks, stays close to the asynchronous one when conditions stall the synchronous path, and adapts promptly in both directions.
