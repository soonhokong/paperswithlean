Gaps in the Most Frequent Sizes of Integer Sumsets

Saad A. Awan, Steven J. Miller, Linh Nguyen, Steven Senger, Elvis Wong

TL;DR
The paper's results are formalized in Lean 4 with Mathlib, partly written by hand and partly generated by the Aristotle prover; one computation uses native_decide.
Abstract
Nathanson observed that, for four-element sets $A$ of integers, the most frequent sizes of the iterated sumset $hA$ are separated by triangular numbers. Senger proved this and suggested an analogue for larger sets. We establish this pattern for $k$-element subsets of $\{1,\ldots,q\}$, for every fixed $k\geq3$, where $h\geq2$ is either fixed or grows slower than $q^{1/(2k-1)}$. As $q\to\infty$, almost every such set has the largest possible $h$-fold sumset, and almost every exception has one of $h-1$ explicit smaller sizes. The differences between consecutive sizes among these $h$ values are consecutive $(k-2)$-dimensional simplex numbers.
Problem

Nathanson observed, and Senger proved, that for four-element integer sets the most frequent sizes of the h-fold sumset hA are separated by triangular numbers. Senger suggested an analogue for larger sets, which is the question addressed here.

Approach

Collisions in hA are encoded as primitive integer coefficient vectors with zero coordinate sum and weight at most h that are orthogonal to the set vector of A. Counting the lattice points on the corresponding hyperplanes and on their pairwise intersections bounds the number of sets of each sumset size. The results are formalized in Lean 4 with Mathlib. Part of the proofs were generated by Harmonic's Aristotle, and all proofs are checked by the Lean kernel except one finite computation done with native_decide.

Results

For fixed k≥3 and h fixed or growing slower than q^{1/(2k-1)}, almost every k-subset of [q] has the maximal h-fold sumset. Almost every exception has one of h-1 explicit smaller sizes, and consecutive gaps between these sizes are consecutive (k-2)-dimensional simplex numbers. The paper also generalizes several of Senger's results to arbitrary k≥3.
