Gaps in the Most Frequent Sizes of Integer Sumsets

Saad A. Awan, Steven J. Miller, Linh Nguyen, Steven Senger, Elvis Wong

Abstract
Nathanson observed that, for four-element sets $A$ of integers, the most frequent sizes of the iterated sumset $hA$ are separated by triangular numbers. Senger proved this and suggested an analogue for larger sets. We establish this pattern for $k$-element subsets of $\{1,\ldots,q\}$, for every fixed $k\geq3$, where $h\geq2$ is either fixed or grows slower than $q^{1/(2k-1)}$. As $q\to\infty$, almost every such set has the largest possible $h$-fold sumset, and almost every exception has one of $h-1$ explicit smaller sizes. The differences between consecutive sizes among these $h$ values are consecutive $(k-2)$-dimensional simplex numbers.
Problem

Nathanson observed, and Senger proved, that for four-element integer sets A the most frequent sizes of the iterated sumset hA are separated by triangular numbers. The task is to extend this pattern to k-element subsets of {1,...,q} for every k≥3.

Approach

Collisions in hA are encoded as primitive integer coefficient vectors d with coordinate sum zero, weight at most h, and d·A=0. The number of sets orthogonal to one such vector, or to two of them, is bounded by lattice-point counts. Sets orthogonal to exactly one vector of weight s are shown to have |hA| = M_{h,k} − M_{h−s,k}. The full development is formalized in Lean 4 with Mathlib, partly by hand and partly generated by the Aristotle prover.

Results

For fixed k≥3 and h fixed or growing slower than q^{1/(2k-1)}, almost every k-subset of [q] is a B_h-set. Almost every exception has one of h−1 explicit smaller sumset sizes, and the gaps between consecutive sizes are consecutive (k−2)-dimensional simplex numbers. All proofs are checked by the Lean kernel, except one finite computation that relies on native_decide.
