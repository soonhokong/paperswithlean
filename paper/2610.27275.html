On Maximum Clique and Maximum Consensus Formulations in Point Cloud Registration

Abolfazl Babanazari, Kaveh Fathian

cs.RO Oct 19, 2026 · v2 cs.CV
formalization geometry
Read PDF arXiv abstract
TL;DR
All proofs about maximum clique, maximum consensus, and group-k consistency bounds were formally verified in Lean, with the formalization publicly released.
Abstract
Maximum clique and maximum consensus are widely used formulations for robust point cloud registration. Maximum consensus seeks one transformation that aligns as many matches as possible, while maximum clique selects matches through pairwise distance tests. Maximum clique is often used as a substitute for or an approximation to maximum consensus, with empirical evidence suggesting a close relationship. However, how closely the two formulations agree is not fully understood. We study this relationship theoretically. We show that, at any fixed positive tolerance, a maximum clique can have arbitrarily large alignment error and be arbitrarily larger than a maximum consensus set. When rotation is fixed, we derive sharp error bounds and tests that exactly characterize maximum consensus for the translation subproblem. We also study group-$k$ consistency, which uses consistency of groups of $k$ matches, and determine the smallest group sizes that guarantee alignment error bounds independent of point cloud scale and geometry. These are $d+1$ for rotations and translations and $d+2$ when reflections are allowed, in dimensions $d\ge2$. Finally, our experiments on nearly 8,000 registration examples quantify the frequency of these limitations in practice and show how the derived bounds can be used to check whether a subset of matches can be aligned within a point tolerance.
Problem

Robust point cloud registration often uses maximum clique as a substitute for maximum consensus. How closely the two formulations agree, in selected-set size and in alignment error, has not been characterized theoretically.

Approach

The authors analyze pairwise and group-k consistency using classical geometry (Jung's theorem), near-isometry theory, and Procrustes analysis. They derive sharp error bounds for the translation-only subproblem and build counterexamples for full rigid registration. They find the smallest group sizes that guarantee alignment error bounds. All proofs are formally verified in Lean, and the formalization is released online.

Results

At any fixed positive tolerance, a maximum clique can have arbitrarily large alignment error and can be arbitrarily larger than a maximum consensus set. Group-(d+1) consistency suffices for proper rigid motions and group-(d+2) when reflections are allowed, giving bounds independent of point cloud scale and geometry. Experiments on nearly 8,000 registration examples measure how often these limitations occur in practice.
