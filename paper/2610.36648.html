An upper bound of 60 for the Ramsey number R(3,3,3,3)

Milos Tatarevic

TL;DR
The combinatorial reductions are formalized in Lean 4, with a final theorem whose only premise is the unsatisfiability of 56,723 SAT formulas emitted by Lean.
Abstract
We improve the upper bound on the classical Ramsey number $R(3,3,3,3)$ from 62 to 60. The proof is based on a global incidence count, classifications of small neighborhoods and exhaustive SAT computations. We formalize the mathematical reductions in Lean and refute every resulting formula with two SAT solvers.
Problem

The best known upper bound for the four-color Ramsey number R(3,3,3,3) was 62. The paper aims to lower it by showing that every four-coloring of the edges of K_61 contains a monochromatic triangle. The title and abstract state a bound of 60, while the stated main theorem concerns K_61, which gives R(3,3,3,3) ≤ 61.

Approach

A ticket double-counting argument over color-neighborhood degree profiles reduces the problem to finitely many cases. These rest on the classification of good three-colorings of K_15 and K_16 into two templates, U and T, and on marked-neighborhood constructions. The cases are encoded as SAT formulas and refuted with both Kissat and CaDiCaL. A Lean 4 formalization covers the degree profiles, the ticket counting, the template classification (via 55 CNF/LRAT certificate pairs), the covering arguments, the perfect-balance case and the encoding into SAT. Lean emits the formulas, and separate programs check that the solver inputs match them.

Results

All 56,723 formulas were found unsatisfiable by both solvers, taking about 677 CPU hours in total. The final Lean theorem, UniformK61.WholeProof.ramsey61, uses only the standard axioms plus the premise that these formulas are unsatisfiable. Lean does not check the SAT exclusions themselves.
