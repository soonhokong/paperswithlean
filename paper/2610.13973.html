Bijective Non-Contractive Maps of Hereditarily Precompact Quasi-Metric Spaces Need Not Be Isometries

Abstract
Zavarzina asked whether every bijective non-contractive self-map of a precompact quasi-metric space is an isometry. We show that it need not be, even when the space is hereditarily precompact. On the integers, take the distance of an irrational rotation and add a fixed toll $c>0$ to every step from a nonnegative integer down to a smaller one. The resulting space, the toll spiral, is a hereditarily precompact quasi-metric space, and the shift $n\mapsto n+1$ is a bijective non-contractive map that is not an isometry. The construction works over any metric space $Y$: for every bijective isometry $σ$ of $Y$ and every point whose $σ$-orbit is aperiodic and totally bounded, adding the same toll along the orbit gives such a counterexample, for instance over the $p$-adic odometer. The conjugate of the toll spiral is not even precompact, so the hypothesis on the conjugate in Zavarzina's positive result cannot be dropped, even for bijections. The toll spiral is also not expand-contract plastic, since the inverse shift is a non-expansive bijection that is not an isometry. For an arbitrary bijective non-contractive map $F$, the distance from $F^kx$ to $F^ky$ has a limit as $k\to-\infty$; $F$ is an isometry for this limit, and the excess $D_0$ of the distance over it is the defect. On a hereditarily precompact space, we prove a monochromy theorem: if a pair $(a,b)$ has positive defect $η$, then there are natural numbers $t_1<t_2<\cdots$ such that either $D_0(a,F^{-Δ}a)>η/4$ for every difference $Δ=t_j-t_i$ with $i<j$, or $D_0(F^{-Δ}b,b)>η/4$ for every such $Δ$. Three boundary results delimit the remaining question; the open case, that of separated spaces compact in one of their topologies, has since been settled by the author (arXiv:2610.14301). The construction, in general and for the circle, is machine-checked in Lean 4 using Mathlib, with no unproved steps.
Problem

Zavarzina asked whether every bijective non-contractive self-map of a precompact quasi-metric space must be an isometry. The question is the asymmetric analogue of expand-contract plasticity for totally bounded metric spaces.

Approach

On the integers, the construction takes the arc distance of an irrational rotation and adds a fixed toll c>0 to each descending step from a nonnegative integer. More generally, it adds a toll along an aperiodic, totally bounded orbit of a bijective isometry of any metric space. A structural theory follows: a limit quasi-pseudo-metric under which F is an isometry, a defect function, and a monochromy theorem on hereditarily precompact spaces. The Section 3 results are mechanized in Lean 4 over Mathlib in two layers: an abstract core over an equivariant injective orbit map, and a family layer derived from an isometric generator. A circle instance is also formalized.

Results

The toll spiral is hereditarily precompact, and the shift on it is a bijective non-contractive map that is not an isometry, so the question has a negative answer. The conjugate space is not precompact, which shows the conjugate hypothesis in Zavarzina's positive theorem is necessary. The space also fails expand-contract plasticity. The Lean artifact compiles sorry-free under a pinned toolchain, covering the quasi-metric axioms, non-contractiveness, both forms of hereditary precompactness, the general scheme and the concrete √2 instance.
