A fully Lean-verified quadratic lower bound on the border determinantal complexity of $x_1^n+\cdots+x_n^n$ over $\mathbb{C}$, including the symmetric and exact models

TL;DR
All four determinantal complexity lower bounds for the Fermat power sum are verified end-to-end in Lean 4 with Mathlib, using only the standard axioms.
Abstract
We present the first end-to-end Lean-verified superlinear lower bound on border determinantal complexity. Let $F_n=x_1^n+\cdots+x_n^n$. We prove, over $\mathbb{C}$, that the border determinantal complexity of $F_n$ is at least $(n-1)^2/(4e)$ and its symmetric border determinantal complexity is at least $(n-1)^2/(2e)$; the same bounds therefore hold for exact determinantal complexity. Together with known $O(n^2)$ constructions, both border quantities are $Θ(n^2)$. The proof compares one enumerative invariant on two sides of a degeneration. On the determinant side, the $(n-2)$nd multidegree of the multiplicity-one Gauss graph of any size-$m$ affine-linear determinant is bounded by an explicit multihomogeneous Bézout number; no smoothness, irreducibility, or squarefreeness is assumed. On the target side, a flat limit of these effective Gauss-graph cycles contains the conormal variety of the projective Fermat cone, whose relevant multidegree is the classical dual degree $n(n-1)^{n-2}$. Effectivity gives the inequality in the correct direction, and an $(n-1)$st root yields the quadratic bound. All four bounds are fully verified in Lean 4 with Mathlib. The Lean theorems assume only $n\ge 3$ and a literal determinantal representation or literal border membership, and depend on no axioms beyond Lean's foundational ones. The formal proof follows a localized version of the argument presented here: where the text compares global cycles, it compares local intersection lengths of finite slices of those cycles. Problem

Lower bounds on determinantal complexity for explicit polynomials have stayed close to linear. The goal is a quadratic lower bound on the border (and symmetric border) determinantal complexity of the power sum F_n = x_1^n + ... + x_n^n over C. Known constructions give O(n^2).

Approach

The proof compares the multidegree δ_{n-2} on two sides of a degeneration. On the determinant side, the multidegree of the multiplicity-one Gauss graph of any size-m affine-linear determinant is bounded by a multihomogeneous Bézout number, obtained via kernel lifts. On the target side, a flat limit of these effective Gauss-graph cycles contains the conormal variety of the Fermat cone, whose multidegree is the dual degree n(n-1)^{n-2}. The Lean formalization follows a localized version of the argument, comparing local intersection lengths of finite slices instead of global cycles.

Results

For n ≥ 3 the border determinantal complexity is at least (n-1)^2/(4e) and the symmetric border determinantal complexity is at least (n-1)^2/(2e). The same bounds hold for the exact models, so both border quantities are Θ(n^2). All bounds are fully verified in Lean 4 with Mathlib, depending only on propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound.
