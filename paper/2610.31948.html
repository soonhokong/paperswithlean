Sharp Bounds on the Winner's Bias under Independent and Nonnegatively Correlated Noise

Ivan Viakhirev, Kirill Borodin, Grach Mkrtchian

cs.LG Oct 22, 2026 · v1 math.ST

TL;DR
All theorems, corollaries and lemmas on sharp winner's-bias constants are machine-checked in Lean 4 with Mathlib, in about 7,900 lines with no sorry.
Abstract
Reporting the best of $M$ noisy, unbiased scores overstates the winner's value by up to $c\sqrt{V}$, where $V$ is the total noise variance and the sharp constant $c$ depends on how the noise is dependent. It was known only at the two extremes, arbitrary dependence ($\sqrt{(M-1)/M}$) and i.i.d. noise. We determine it for two classes in between: for independent, not identically distributed noise it is $\sqrt{(M-1)/(2M)}$, and for pairwise nonnegatively correlated noise it is $(M-1)/M$; for uncorrelated noise this answers the maximum version of an open question about the expected range. Explicit finite constructions attain both. As a corollary, the bias of the cross-validation estimator is bounded sharply within each class. All theorems are formally verified in Lean 4. Across 448 language-model and classifier selection pipelines, designs with per-candidate items realize a median 0.75 of the independent-class ceiling. At a fixed item budget, scoring all candidates on shared items is markedly less optimistic than giving each candidate its own items.
Problem

Reporting the best of M noisy, unbiased scores overstates the winner's value by up to c√V. The sharp constant c was known only for arbitrary dependence and for i.i.d. noise. The intermediate classes of independent non-identically distributed noise and pairwise nonnegatively correlated noise were open.

Approach

A centered master bound (Aven's bound applied to deviations from the mean, proved via Samuelson's inequality and Cauchy–Schwarz) isolates a covariance correction term. Explicit finite extremal constructions (a one-up law plus a common shock, and a cascade law) show the bounds are sharp. A transfer lemma carries the bounds to the cross-validation estimator. All results are formalized in Lean 4 with Mathlib (11 files, no axioms beyond the standard three), and an empirical study covers 448 language-model and classifier selection pipelines.

Results

The sharp constant is √((M-1)/(2M)) for independent noise and (M-1)/M for pairwise nonnegatively correlated noise. This settles van Hasselt's conjecture on cross-validation bias and the uncorrelated case of Papadatos's question. Per-candidate evaluation designs realize a median 0.75 of the independent-class ceiling, and shared-item designs are markedly less optimistic at a fixed item budget.
