The limit shape of uniform random antichains in $[0,1]^d$

Andrew Elvey Price, Minjae Park

Abstract
For each $d\ge2$, we study $n$ independent uniform points in $[0,1]^d$, conditioned to form an antichain: no point is strictly smaller than another in every coordinate. We prove that the point cloud converges in Hausdorff distance to the closure of the hypersurface \[\mathcal S_d=\left\{\mathbf x\in(0,1)^d: \sum_{i=1}^dΦ^{-1}(\mathbf x_i)=0\right\}\] in probability as $n\to\infty$, where $Φ$ is the standard normal cumulative distribution function. The proof also shows that the antichain probability $a_n$ satisfies $\log a_n=-\frac{n}{d-1}\log n+β_d n+o(n)$ for a finite constant $β_d$. We identify the limiting empirical distribution and hence the limiting permuton of uniform random $d$-dimensional permutations avoiding a single pattern of size $2$.
Problem

The paper studies n independent uniform points in [0,1]^d conditioned to form an antichain, where no point is coordinatewise strictly smaller than another. It asks for the limit shape of such random antichains and for refined asymptotics of the antichain probability a_n.

Approach

Coordinates are mapped through the inverse normal CDF, and each point is split into a floor position on the hyperplane orthogonal to (1,...,1) and a height. The antichain condition becomes a Lipschitz-type constraint on the heights. A simplex tiling of the floor hyperplane yields weighted volume estimates, with matching upper bounds via Poisson processes and lower bounds via boundary-matching constructions. The authors report that AI generated an accompanying Lean formalization (repository antichain-lean), whose main theorem statements they reviewed.

Results

The point cloud converges in Hausdorff distance, in probability, to the closure of the hypersurface where the sum of Φ^{-1}(x_i) equals zero. The antichain probability satisfies log a_n = -n/(d-1) log n + β_d n + o(n). The limiting empirical distribution is identified, giving the permuton limit of d-dimensional permutations avoiding a size-2 pattern, together with an enumeration asymptotic.
