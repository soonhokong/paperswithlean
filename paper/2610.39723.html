Countable Nondeterminism in Weighted Programming with an Application to Mechanized Foundations for Probabilistic Program Verification

Sebastian Körner, Oliver Emil Bøving, Christoph Matheja

cs.PL Oct 29, 2026 · v1 cs.LO

TL;DR
Mechanizes the extended weighted programming framework and its wp-soundness results, including a fixed point characterization of minimal expected rewards, in Lean.
Abstract
Weighted programming is a framework by Batz et al. for describing mathematical models or optimization problems as imperative programs parametrized over semirings and then analyzing them with program verification techniques like weakest precondition calculi. Applications include reasoning about expected outcomes of probabilistic programs and competitive analysis of online algorithms. We extend weighted programming with (1) countable nondeterminism and (2) user-defined additive rewards, where programs are parametrized over modules instead of semirings. The resulting framework enables reasoning about expected resource consumption of probabilistic programs and about optimal scores of combinatorial games. A technical challenge is that wp-calculi for weighted programs with (1) and (2) over arbitrary modules can be unsound, because it is well-known that continuity properties commonly used in program semantics break in the presence of countable nondeterminism. We study conditions under which a wp-style transformer for reasoning about weighted programs is sound with respect to an operational ground truth defined in terms of weighted decision processes (WDPs) - transition systems that generalize Markov decision processes by drawing transition weights and state rewards from a module. Based on those conditions, we show that a range of concrete instances of the weighted programming framework enjoy sound weakest precondition reasoning. We demonstrate the usefulness of our wp-semantics through case studies for different modules. We mechanized our extended weighted programming framework and our main soundness results in the interactive theorem prover Lean. Instantiating these results for probabilistic programming yields, to the best of our knowledge, the first mechanized proof of a fixed point characterization of minimal expected rewards in Markov decision processes with countable nondeterminism.
Problem

Weighted programming lacked support for countable nondeterminism and user-defined additive rewards. In the presence of countable nondeterminism, wp-calculi over arbitrary modules can be unsound because the usual continuity properties fail.

Approach

Weighted programs are parametrized over weight-reward modules rather than semirings. An operational ground truth is given by weighted decision processes (WDPs), which generalize MDPs. The authors identify conditions, such as well-behavedness of WDPs, under which the minimal weighted reward equals the least fixed point of the Bellman operator. The framework and the main soundness results are mechanized in Lean.

Results

Several concrete module instances (probabilistic, arctic, formal languages) are shown to have sound wp reasoning, with case studies on a delayed probabilistic loop, subtraction games, and message passing. The authors describe the result as the first mechanized proof of a fixed point characterization of minimal expected rewards in MDPs with countable nondeterminism.
