Finite-Time Singularity Formation for the Three-Dimensional Euler Equations

Levent Alpöge, Tristan Buckmaster, Matei P. Coiculescu

math.AP Nov 19, 2026 · v1
formalization analysis physics
Read PDF arXiv abstract Code
TL;DR
The proof of finite-time Euler blow-up is formalized in Lean, with the formalization in a public GitHub repository.
Abstract
We construct smooth periodic initial data for the three-dimensional incompressible Euler equations whose solution blows up in finite time. The velocity remains bounded, while the vorticity magnitude at the origin tends to infinity. Away from the origin, the velocity and its time derivative have smooth spatial limits at the singular time. The construction uses localized Craik–Criminale waves as building blocks of a multi-scale construction.
Problem

Whether smooth solutions of the three-dimensional incompressible Euler equations on the torus can develop a singularity in finite time.

Approach

Localized Craik–Criminale waves, which are exact plane-wave solutions over linear background flows, serve as building blocks. Waves are transported along the flow of a parent velocity field and corrected to stay divergence-free. Initial data are selected through an endpoint problem. An inductive multi-scale sequence of approximate solutions is built, with each wave amplified in the shear of its predecessor. The full proof is formalized in Lean.

Results

Smooth periodic initial data are constructed whose solution blows up in finite time. The velocity stays bounded while the vorticity at the origin tends to infinity. Away from the origin, the velocity and its time derivative have smooth limits at the singular time.
