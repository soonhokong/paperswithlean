Formalization, Transparency, and Collusion in Open-Source Game Theory

Colomban Duclaux, Riccardo Formenti, Pepijn Cobben, Terry Jingchen Zhang, Bernhard Schölkopf, Zhijing Jin

cs.GT Nov 15, 2026 · v1 cs.AI cs.MA econ.TH
formalization logic economics ai-agents
Read PDF arXiv abstract
TL;DR
Mechanizes a resource-bounded proof-based open-source game theory framework in Lean 4, building on the Foundation logic library, sorry-free and axiom-free.
Abstract
Cooperation among AI agents will undermine alignment protocols when it takes the form of collusion. Under these protocols, agents often have some information about one another, and how much they have may determine whether they collude. Open-source game theory studies the extreme case, in which each program reads its opponent's source code and acts based on what it can prove about it. We develop formal foundations for this theory: we specify a concrete proof system, resolve an open problem concerning DupocBot and CupodBot through a transposition argument, and mechanize the framework in Lean 4; to our knowledge, this is the first mechanization of resource-bounded open-source game theory. We then extend the framework to the more realistic setting of partial transparency, where each program receives only partial information about its opponent. In Prisoner's Dilemma experiments, where mutual cooperation models collusion, greater transparency increases both the cooperation sustainable in equilibrium and the cooperation selected by replicator dynamics. This suggests that controlling how much agents can know about one another matters for alignment protocols.
Problem

Open-source game theory studies programs that read their opponents' source code and act on what they can prove about them, a model relevant to collusion among AI agents. Its formal foundations were underspecified, and some outcomes, such as DupocBot versus CupodBot, were open problems.

Approach

The authors fix a concrete proof system S: Peano Arithmetic extended with action constants and an action axiom, with proof length measured in symbols. They introduce a transposition argument to settle the DupocBot/CupodBot problem. They mechanize the framework in Lean 4 on top of the Foundation library, using only Lean's standard axioms and no sorry. They then extend the theory to partial transparency and run Prisoner's Dilemma experiments over a library of programs, analyzing program equilibria and replicator dynamics.
