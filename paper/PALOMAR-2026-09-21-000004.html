Lower bounds for sets without perfect-power differences

Abstract
Let D_k(N) be the largest size of a subset of {1, ..., N} with no positive k-th-power difference. We prove D_k(N) >= c_k N^alpha_k for every N >= 1, with positive constants c_k and exponents 0.75806770413, 0.9142, and 0.95295 for squares, fourth powers, and sixth powers. The proofs use a general interval-moment criterion for every positive integer k. We also prove arithmetic lifting results, exact product and prime-depth laws for interval capacity, and existence of the binary square capacity limit. The square construction refines Naslund's published example while retaining its binary transition rules. All selected statements are proved in Lean.

Problem

D_k(N) denotes the largest subset of {1, ..., N} with no positive k-th-power difference. The goal is explicit polynomial lower bounds D_k(N) >= c_k N^alpha_k that hold for every N >= 1.

Approach

A general interval-moment criterion, valid for every positive integer k, yields lower bounds. Arithmetic lifting results and exact product and prime-depth laws for interval capacity support the constructions. The square case refines Naslund's published example while keeping its binary transition rules. The selected statements are proved in Lean.

Results

The exponents obtained are alpha = 0.75806770413 for squares, 0.9142 for fourth powers, and 0.95295 for sixth powers. The binary square capacity limit is shown to exist. The proofs were machine-checked in Lean.
