Metric Dimension is NP-complete on Graphs of Treewidth Two

Tristan Benoit

cs.CC Oct 15, 2026 · v2 cs.DM
formalization mathlib combinatorics ai-agents
Read PDF arXiv abstract
TL;DR
The main reduction results (connectivity, treewidth at most two, metric dimension values) are formalized in Lean 4 with Mathlib, produced by an LLM.
Abstract
The metric dimension of a connected graph is the least number of vertices whose distance vectors distinguish all vertices. We show that deciding whether the metric dimension of a graph is at most a given integer is NP-complete on graphs of treewidth at most two. As the problem is polynomial on trees, the graphs of treewidth one, this settles the treewidth threshold of its hardness, which was known to lie between two and twenty-four. The reduction is from satisfiability of formulas in which every literal occurs at most twice. To such a formula with $n$ clauses and $r$ variables it associates a graph $G_\varphi$ of treewidth at most two with $O((n+r)^2)$ vertices, whose metric dimension is $2(n+r)+5$ if the formula is satisfiable and $2(n+r)+6$ otherwise.
Problem

The complexity of Metric Dimension on graphs of bounded treewidth was open. Hardness was known to set in somewhere between treewidth two and twenty-four, and the problem is polynomial on trees.

Approach

A polynomial reduction from satisfiability of formulas in which every literal occurs at most twice. Each formula is mapped to a graph G_φ of treewidth at most two built from series-parallel templates, using distance certificates and forcing lemmas. An LLM carried out the research and also produced an independent Lean 4/Mathlib formalisation of the main results.

Results

Deciding whether metric dimension is at most k is NP-complete on graphs of treewidth two: md(G_φ) is 2(n+r)+5 if φ is satisfiable and 2(n+r)+6 otherwise. The Lean formalisation proves connectivity, the treewidth bound, the metric dimension values and the normalisation step with no unproved statements and only standard axioms.
