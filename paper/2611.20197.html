Integral zero-sum incidence identities and Davenport constants in rank three

Claudiu G. Pop, George C. Rad, George C. Ţurcaş

Abstract
We study the Davenport constant of finite abelian groups through signed identities among zero-sum subsequences. An integral incidence identity of degree $t$ for a sequence assigns integer weights to its nonempty zero-sum subsequences so that the weights add up to one, while those of the subsequences containing any given nonempty set of at most $t$ terms add up to zero. Over a finite abelian $p$-group $H$, the zero-sum subsequences of a sequence of any length have an indicator polynomial of degree at most $D(H)-1$, and this gives a transfer principle: if, for each $t\ge0$, every sequence of $L(t)$ terms over $Q$ admits such an identity of degree $t$, then $D(Q\oplus H)\le L(D(H)-1)$ for every finite abelian $p$-group $H$. For $m\in\{2,3,4\}$ we construct integral identities at length $L(t)=m(t+3)-2$ over $C_m^3$, and prove that these thresholds are sharp over fields of characteristic zero or characteristic not dividing $m$. For a prime $p\nmid m$ and $0\le a\le b\le c$, we prove that \[ D(C_{mp^a}\oplus C_{mp^b}\oplus C_{mp^c}) =m(p^a+p^b+p^c)-2. \] In particular $D(C_{15}^3)=43$ and $D(C_{20}^3)=58$, which are the two smallest cases of the conjecture $D(C_n^3)=3n-2$ left open in the literature. The proofs of the base cases for $m \in \{3,4\}$ are computer-assisted. All of the results in this paper have been formalised in Lean 4.
Problem

The Davenport constant D(G) of a finite abelian group is conjectured to equal n1+n2+n3-2 in rank three. Cases such as D(C_15^3) and D(C_20^3) were the smallest open instances for C_n^3.

Approach

The authors define integral incidence identities: integer weightings of zero-sum subsequences that cancel on all small term sets. They prove a transfer principle that bounds D(Q⊕H) for p-group kernels H, using a low-degree indicator polynomial built from nilpotence of the augmentation ideal. For quotients C_m^3 with m in {2,3,4}, they build identities from computer-found base certificates and three constructions, and prove sharpness via a binomial determinant. Everything, including the enumerations and the soundness of certificate checks, is formalized in Lean 4 with Mathlib, with LLM assistance.

Results

For a prime p not dividing m, with m in {2,3,4}, D(C_{mp^a}⊕C_{mp^b}⊕C_{mp^c}) = m(p^a+p^b+p^c)-2. This gives D(C_15^3)=43 and D(C_20^3)=58, and the identity-length thresholds are shown to be sharp.
