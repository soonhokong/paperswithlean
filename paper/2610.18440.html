A Central Limit Theorem for High-Temperature SYK Model: A case study on mathematical research using AI and Lean

Yoshinori Kamijima, Shuta Nakajima, Ryoichiro Noda, Kôhei Sasaya

TL;DR
The main CLT proofs for the SYK model are formalized in Lean 4, conditional on one annealed free-energy proposition, using AI-generated code reviewed by the authors.
Abstract
The Sachdev–Ye–Kitaev (SYK) model is a quantum many-body model of Majorana fermions with all-to-all random interactions. It has attracted wide interest in condensed matter and high-energy physics as a solvable model of non-Fermi liquids and holography. Fix an even integer $q\geq4$, and let $Z_N(β)$ be the partition function of the Gaussian $q$-body SYK model. We prove that for all sufficiently small inverse temperatures $β> 0$, there exists $ρ=ρ(β)>0$ such that \[ N^{q/2 -1}(\log Z_N(β)-\mathbb{E}[\log Z_N(β)]) \Longrightarrow \mathcal{N}(0,ρ^2). \] In addition to the mathematical results, we discuss how the proofs were developed and refined through interaction between human researchers and AI, and we describe the subsequent formalization of the main arguments in Lean 4. This work thus serves as a case study of a research workflow that integrates human insight, AI-assisted exploration, iterative proof refinement, and formal verification.
Problem

The work seeks a central limit theorem for the free energy of the Gaussian q-body Sachdev–Ye–Kitaev (SYK) model at high temperature. It also serves as a case study of a research workflow combining human researchers, AI, and Lean.

Approach

The authors first prove CLTs for the partition function using a polynomial (Hermite) chaos expansion. The second chaos gives the Gaussian limit, and a combinatorial Rademacher argument controls all higher-order chaoses. Proofs were developed and refined through dialogue with ChatGPT. AI then generated Lean 4 code formalizing the refined proofs, which the humans reviewed.

Results

For even q≥4 and sufficiently small β, N^{q/2-1}(log Z_N − E log Z_N) converges to a centered Gaussian with explicit variance (q!/2)β²(ℓ_q'(β))². The main results are formalized in Lean 4 conditional on Proposition 4.2, with code at github.com/TokyoProbabilityGroup/SYK_CLT.
