Quantum Subentropy: Subadditivity and Sharp Bounds on Discrimination Error

Pratik Patil

quant-ph Nov 23, 2026 · v1 cs.IT
formalization quantum
Read PDF arXiv abstract
TL;DR
All theoretical results on quantum subentropy are formalized in Lean 4, partly adapting code from the Omega Institute's Lean development.
Abstract
Subentropy gives a tight spectral lower bound on the accessible information of ensembles of pure states. We prove its subadditivity for arbitrary bipartite states in finite dimensions and derive a sharp relation between conditional subentropy and the minimum discrimination error. This relation strengthens a conjectured comparison with conditional min-entropy. Its inverse gives the strongest universal lower bound on the error determined only by conditional subentropy and the number of labels. Both main results follow from a comparison of conditional subentropy with the average subentropy of measurement posteriors, valid for mixed signal states and every finite measurement. We characterize all equality cases in these two results and bound posterior deviations near equality. We also determine the optimal Lipschitz constant in trace distance for conditional subentropy on classical-quantum states, including the exact constant when the quantum marginal is fixed. A determinant inequality strengthens subadditivity and proves strong subadditivity for classical distributions, while an exact counterexample shows that strong subadditivity fails for quantum states. Lean 4 proofs of all theoretical results are available at https://pratikpatil.io/lean/subentropy/
Problem

Subentropy lower-bounds the accessible information of pure-state ensembles. Two conjectures of Datta et al. remained open: subadditivity for arbitrary bipartite states, and a comparison between conditional subentropy and conditional min-entropy.

Approach

Subentropy is expressed through a Gaussian expectation and through an integral of reciprocal determinants. Conditional subentropy is compared with the average subentropy of measurement posteriors, using a Gram-matrix pinching functional that is shown to be convex. Determinant inequalities and maximal coupling give the continuity and strong-subadditivity results. All theoretical results are formalized in Lean 4.

Results

Subadditivity is proved for arbitrary finite-dimensional bipartite states, together with a sharp bound linking conditional subentropy to minimum discrimination error, with all equality cases characterized. The paper also gives optimal Lipschitz constants for conditional subentropy on classical-quantum states and a proof of classical strong subadditivity. An exact counterexample shows that quantum strong subadditivity fails.
