Imaginary quadratic fields of class number at most 2000

Iwao Kimura

TL;DR
The explicit Siegel-zero gap and the class-number discriminant bound are proved in Lean 4 with Mathlib and checked by the Lean kernel and nanoda.
Abstract
We determine all imaginary quadratic fields with class number at most 2000, unconditionally: there are 16,453,493 of them, and the largest $|d_K|$ among them is 1,062,350,083. To our knowledge, such a list was previously known only for class number at most 100. We make explicit a recent argument, released by OpenAI and not refereed, that excludes Landau-Siegel zeros: every real zero $β$ of $L(s,χ)$, for a primitive real character $χ$ of conductor $q\ge3$, satisfies $(1-β)\log q\ge5.8\cdot10^{-4}$. An elementary lower bound for $L(1,χ)$ then shows that a field with class number at most 2000 has $|d_K|<1.61\cdot10^{15}$. The fields with $6\cdot2^{28}\le|d_K|\le1.65\cdot10^{15}$ are excluded by Watkins' sieve, and those below are read from the class group tables of Mosunov and Jacobson. As a consequence, the lists of Cremona and Sutherland of imaginary quadratic orders with class number at most 1000, and the counts of Holmin, Jones, Kurlberg, McLeman and Petersen for odd class numbers up to 1999, previously known under the generalized Riemann hypothesis, hold unconditionally. The two steps, the bound for $β$ and the bound for $|d_K|$, are proved in Lean, building on the existing formalization of that argument, and the proofs are checked by two independent kernels. The contributions of AI systems to this work are stated in the paper.
Problem

Unconditional lists of imaginary quadratic fields by class number were previously known only up to class number 100. Larger lists, such as the Cremona–Sutherland list (h ≤ 1000) and the Holmin et al. counts for odd h, depended on GRH. A recent unrefereed OpenAI argument excluding Landau–Siegel zeros gives no explicit constant.

Approach

The OpenAI argument is made explicit, giving (1-β)log q ≥ 5.8·10^-4 for real zeros of real primitive L-functions. An elementary lower bound for L(1,χ) via the hyperbola method then bounds |d_K| for class number ≤ 2000. Both theorems are formalized in Lean 4 with Mathlib, reusing parts of the existing OpenAI formalization unmodified, and are checked by two independent kernels. Watkins' sieve excludes the intermediate discriminant range, and the Mosunov–Jacobson class group tables cover small discriminants.

Results

There are 16,453,493 imaginary quadratic fields with class number at most 2000, and the largest |d_K| among them is 1,062,350,083. This makes the GRH-conditional lists of Cremona–Sutherland and Holmin et al. unconditional in the ranges stated in the abstract.
