Proofs Without Nominals: Gödel's Ontological Argument, its Shallow Embedding, and the Open Questions of the Monatshefte Notes

Christoph Benzmüller

TL;DR
All theorems are verified in Isabelle/HOL and independently in a Lean 4 port, with a Lean detector for nominal witnesses and a kernel-checked countermodel.
Abstract
The shallow embedding of higher-order modal logic in classical higher-order logic, used in Benzmüller and Scott's Notes on Gödel's and Scott's variants of the ontological argument, reaches beyond the modal object language: its property quantifiers range over domains containing the nominals and satisfaction operators of hybrid logic. A proof using one proves a theorem of the embedding, not necessarily of the modal logic. That the framework affords this is not new. Whether a result is one of the modal logic can be settled by replaying it in an explicit calculus, one result at a time. More cheaply, and for all results at once, it can be approached by analysing what the embedding's proofs substitute. Every statement the Notes prove has a proof in the embedding substituting nothing outside the object language: 294 of them explicit proofs, machine-checked, none using a nominal. The Notes' own proofs substitute none either; what the check flags are terms a prover introduced. The three questions the Notes leave open are settled too, without nominals, but by the empty instance of the conjunction axiom, too cheaply to convince. As the Notes generalise it, to cover Gödel's "any number of summands", the axiom covers the empty conjunction and the singleton. This article restricts it to at least two different conjuncts, a non-degenerate reading of Gödel's footnote, and settles the questions again, without nominals. In effect the restriction excludes only the empty conjunction; the one-element case comes back with the other axioms. In one of the three variants the restriction refutes the possible existence of a God-like being; the answers return, with no side condition, once the Notes' non-emptiness clause is confined to Gödel's essence. Every theorem stated here is verified in Isabelle/HOL and independently in Lean 4; the countermodels are Nitpick's, checked by the build.
Problem

The shallow embedding of higher-order modal logic in HOL, used in Benzmüller and Scott's Notes on Gödel's ontological argument, lets property quantifiers range over hybrid-logic terms such as nominals. A proof in the embedding therefore need not be a proof in the modal logic itself. The Notes also leave three questions open.

Approach

A proof is defined as hybrid-free when every term it substitutes for a property or proposition variable belongs to the modal object language. Detectors in Isabelle/HOL and Lean 4 audit the substituted witnesses in the proofs. The open questions are settled again under a restricted conjunction axiom (Ax1GenTwo) that requires at least two different conjuncts. Countermodels come from Nitpick and are checked by the build, and one is written out and verified by Lean's kernel.

Results

The Isabelle audit certifies 403 of the Notes' 427 proofs directly. The other 24 are flagged only for prover-introduced terms, and hand-written replacement proofs are certified. No proof uses a nominal. The open questions are resolved hybrid-free. Under the two-conjunct axiom, one variant refutes the possible existence of a God-like being, and the answers return once the non-emptiness clause is confined to Gödel's essence.
