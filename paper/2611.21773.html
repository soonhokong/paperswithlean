Hidden convexity of the Eliashberg condensation energy: existence, uniqueness and continuity of the superconducting state

Davis Rens

TL;DR
The finite-dimensional algebraic and convex-analytic core (rearrangement identities, folding, strict convexity, finite-dimensional existence and uniqueness) is formalized in Lean 4 with Mathlib.
Abstract
Kiessling, Altshuler and Yuzbashyan conjectured the thermodynamic transition picture for the isotropic Eliashberg spin-chain functional with vanishing Coulomb pseudopotential: the normal state is the unique minimizer above and at a critical temperature, the superconducting minimizer below it is unique up to gauge, and the transition is continuous. We establish the nonlinear part of this picture. In the variables $ρ_n=\sin^2θ_n$, the condensation energy restricted to the first quadrant is strictly convex, and folding any admissible chain into that quadrant cannot increase its energy. For every positive, non-increasing interaction with power-law decay, hidden convexity and a compactness estimate give existence and uniqueness of the minimizer up to gauge. The normal state minimizes the energy exactly when its quadratic form is non-negative, including marginal stability. Among admissible chains, a positive solution of the nonlinear Eliashberg equations exists exactly when the normal state is linearly unstable and is then unique. For temperature-dependent families with pointwise continuity and locally uniform decay bounds, the minimizing density is continuous in a weighted $\ell^1$ norm. Together with uniqueness of the linear-stability temperature, these results prove the thermodynamic conjectures for the $γ$-model, Einstein phonons and general phonon spectra; the phonon applications use separately established spectral input. Finite-dimensional algebraic and convex-analytic components are machine-checked in Lean. The proofs were obtained independently of concurrent work that reaches overlapping conclusions.
Problem

Kiessling, Altshuler and Yuzbashyan conjectured a thermodynamic picture for the isotropic Eliashberg spin-chain functional with zero Coulomb pseudopotential. In this picture the normal state is the unique minimizer at and above a critical temperature, the superconducting minimizer below it is unique up to gauge, and the transition is continuous. Existence and uniqueness of minimizers had been assumed rather than proved.

Approach

In the variables ρ_n = sin²θ_n, the condensation energy restricted to the first quadrant is shown to be strictly convex (hidden convexity). Folding any chain into that quadrant does not increase its energy. Coercivity and compactness estimates give existence of a minimizer, and an identity decomposes the energy as the stability quadratic form plus non-negative terms. The finite-truncation core is machine-checked in Lean 4/Mathlib with no sorry, including the identities, folding inequality, strict convexity, and finite-dimensional existence and uniqueness up to gauge.

Results

A minimizer exists and is unique up to gauge for every positive, non-increasing, power-law-decaying interaction. The normal state is the minimizer exactly when its quadratic form is non-negative. A positive Eliashberg solution exists exactly under linear instability and is then unique, and the minimizing density depends continuously on temperature. Together these prove the KAY conjectures for the γ-model, Einstein phonons and general phonon spectra.
