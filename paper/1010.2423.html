$δ$-superderivations of simple finite-dimensional Jordan and Lie superalgebras

Ivan Kaygorodov

math.RA Oct 12, 2010 · v2 math.AC

Read PDF arXiv abstract

Abstract
We introduce the concept of a $δ$-superderivation of a superalgebra. $δ$-Derivations of Cartan-type Lie superalgebras are treated, as well as $δ$-superderivations of simple finite-dimensional Lie superalgebras and Jordan superalgebras over an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0. We give a complete description of $\{1}{2}$-derivations for Cartan-type Lie superalgebras. It is proved that nontrivial $δ$-(super)derivations are missing on the given classes of superalgebras, and as a consequence, $δ$-superderivations are shown to be trivial on simple finite-dimensional noncommutative Jordan superalgebras of degree at least 2 over an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0. Also we consider $δ$-derivations of unital flexible and semisimple finite-dimensional Jordan algebras over a field of characteristic not 2.
Problem

The paper studies δ-superderivations of superalgebras, a generalization of derivations. The goal is to classify δ-(super)derivations of simple finite-dimensional Lie and Jordan superalgebras, including Cartan-type Lie superalgebras, over algebraically closed fields of characteristic 0.

Approach

The authors introduce δ-superderivations and the supercentroid. They analyze δ-derivations of the Cartan-type superalgebras W(n), S(n), S̃(2n) and H(n) using root-space and grading arguments. Odd δ-superderivations are reduced to even ones via commutators with ad_x. Results for Jordan algebras over non-closed fields are obtained by extending scalars to the algebraic closure of the center.

Results

Simple finite-dimensional Lie and Jordan superalgebras in characteristic 0 have no nontrivial δ-superderivations, and the 1/2-derivations of Cartan-type Lie superalgebras are fully described. Semisimple finite-dimensional Jordan algebras in characteristic ≠ 2 have no nontrivial δ-derivations. Several lemmas and theorems were corrected relative to the published version, and the corrected version is stated to have been verified in Lean.
