Recognizing potentially planar degree sequences

Zhe Zhang, Renwen Tian

cs.CC Nov 24, 2026 · v1
formalization combinatorics
Read PDF arXiv abstract Code
TL;DR
A Lean 4 companion formalizes the paper's results, indexed by theorem number, with enumeration scripts; some classical theorems are taken as explicit hypotheses.
Abstract
Testing for a simple planar realization is strongly NP-complete even on graphic $n$-term degree lists of minimum degree at least three and sum $6n-12$. Two $13$-vertex triangulations have sorted degree lists whose midpoint is graphic and has no planar realization, ruling out a characterization by simultaneous affine inequalities in the sorted entries even at sum $66$. For arbitrary lists, an ordered interval rule and a finite difference in the degrees alone each give an exact criterion. On an $L$-bit input, a deterministic $O(L)+2^{O(\sqrt{n})}$-time search finds a realization or rules one out. At the first two even sums beyond the sparse bound of Bar-Noy et al., we classify all exceptions among positive graphic lists, giving linear-time recognition on a word RAM throughout the extended range. In particular, for $n\ge 8$, appending $n-6$ twos suffices to make every graphic $n$-term list of minimum degree at least three and sum $6n-12$ potentially planar.
Problem

Hakimi and Chvátal asked which graphic degree lists admit a simple planar realization. The paper studies the complexity of this recognition problem and looks for exact criteria.

Approach

The authors reduce from 3-Partition, using stacked triangulations in which triangular faces encode prescribed adjacencies. For general lists they give an ordered interval rule in Havel–Hakimi style, an arithmetic criterion based on sphere-embedding counts, and a weighted-separator search algorithm. For sparse sums they count the degree-two vertices needed via planar multigraphs on the core vertices. Results are formalized in Lean 4, with the canonical-ordering, planar separator and weighted matrix-tree theorems assumed as hypotheses.

Results

Planar Degree Realization is strongly NP-complete even for graphic lists with minimum degree at least three and sum 6n−12. A 13-vertex midpoint example rules out a characterization by simultaneous affine inequalities in the sorted degrees. An O(L)+2^{O(√n)} algorithm decides the problem. The paper also gives linear-time recognition at the first two even sums beyond the sparse bound of Bar-Noy et al. For n≥8, appending n−6 twos makes every such list planar.
