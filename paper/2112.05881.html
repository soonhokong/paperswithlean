Optimal local laws and CLT for the circular Riesz gas

Jeanne Boursier

TL;DR

Abstract
We study the circular Riesz gas, a one-dimensional system of particles with long-range power-law repulsion. We establish near-optimal rigidity estimates for the gaps at all scales. We then prove a quantitative central limit theorem for linear statistics, combining Stein's method with a mean-field transport. The main difficulty is to control the fluctuations of the local error terms left by the transport, which we do using the rigidity estimates, convexity and monotonicity. In particular, a comparison principle for the Helffer–Sjöstrand equation allows us to treat test functions with low regularity, including indicators of intervals.
Problem

The circular Riesz gas is a one-dimensional particle system on the circle with long-range power-law repulsion. The goals are optimal local laws (rigidity of gaps at all scales) and a quantitative central limit theorem for linear statistics, including low-regularity test functions such as indicators of intervals.

Approach

Rigidity is proved through a convexification procedure that compares the gas with a locally constrained Gibbs measure, combined with log-concavity of the gap distribution. The CLT combines Stein's method with a mean-field transport. Fluctuations of the local error terms are controlled using the rigidity estimates, convexity and monotonicity, plus a comparison principle for the Helffer–Sjöstrand equation. The main theorems and corollaries were formalized in Lean using Mathlib, with large language models assisting the formalization.

Results

The paper establishes near-optimal rigidity estimates for gaps at all scales, an optimal variance estimate for singular linear statistics, and a quantitative CLT valid for indicator test functions. Theorems 1–3 and Corollaries 1–2 are reported as formalized in Lean.
