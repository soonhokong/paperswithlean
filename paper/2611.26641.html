Improving Razmyslov's (1974) bound: the Nagata-Higman index is at most $n^2-2$ for $n\ge3$

Dave Goel

TL;DR
The Nagata-Higman bounds d(n) ≤ n²−1 and d(n) ≤ n²−2 were also proved in the Lean proof assistant.
Abstract
Let $d(n)$ be the least $m$ such that every associative (not necessarily unital) algebra over a field of characteristic zero satisfying the identity $x^n=0$ also satisfies $x_1x_2\cdots x_m=0$; this is the Nagata-Higman index. Razmyslov proved $d(n)\le n^2$ in 1974; Kuzmin proved $d(n)\ge n(n+1)/2$ and conjectured equality; this is the Kuzmin (1975) conjecture. We prove $d(n)\le n^2-2$ for $n\ge3$ (and, by a simpler case of the same method, $d(n)\le n^2-1$ for $n\ge2$); in particular $d(6)\le34$. To our knowledge, $n=6$ is the first case in which the new bound improves the known bounds, and this is the first improvement of Razmyslov's bound for general $n$. For $m=n^2-1$ and $m=n^2-2$, Formanek's criterion reduces $d(n)\le m$ to the statement that no nonzero linear combination of $(m+1)$-cycles lies in one particular isotypic block of the group algebra of $S_{m+1}$. For $m=n^2-1$ this statement follows from the branching rule; for $m=n^2-2$ we prove it using the Jucys-Murphy elements and a theorem of Frobenius on the characters of the alternating group. These bounds have also been proved in the Lean proof assistant.
Problem

The Nagata-Higman index d(n) is the least m such that every characteristic-zero associative algebra satisfying x^n=0 also satisfies x_1⋯x_m=0. Razmyslov's 1974 upper bound d(n) ≤ n² had not been improved for general n. Kuzmin conjectured d(n) = n(n+1)/2.

Approach

Formanek's criterion reduces d(n) ≤ m to showing that no nonzero linear combination of (m+1)-cycles lies in a particular isotypic block of the group algebra of S_{m+1}. For m=n²−1 this follows from the branching rule applied to the square partition. For m=n²−2 the proof uses the partition (n^{n-1},n−1), Jucys-Murphy elements, and a theorem of Frobenius on characters of the alternating group. The bounds were also proved in Lean.

Results

d(n) ≤ n²−1 for n ≥ 2 and d(n) ≤ n²−2 for n ≥ 3, giving d(6) ≤ 34, the first case that improves the known bounds. As a corollary, invariants of n×n matrices are generated in degree at most n²−2 for n ≥ 3.
