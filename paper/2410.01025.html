Dipole Formation in the Two-Component Plasma

Jeanne Boursier, Sylvia Serfaty

TL;DR

Abstract
We consider the two-dimensional two-component plasma, or Coulomb gas, consisting of $N$ positive and $N$ negative charges with logarithmic interaction. We introduce a suitable regularization of the interaction by smearing the charges over a small length scale $λ$, which allows us to give meaning to the system in the continuum at any temperature. We provide an expansion of the free energy for each inverse temperature $β\ge2$, as $N \to \infty$ and $λ\to 0$. Doing so allows us to show that, for $β\geq 2$, the charges (for the most part) pair into neutral dipoles of very small size as $λ\to 0$. This complements the prior work of Leblé, Zeitouni, and the second author, which proved that this does not happen for $β< 2$, thereby implying a transition at $β= 2$ from free charges to dipole pairs. Moreover, we obtain an estimate on the size of linear statistics. The description in terms of dipoles is made via a decomposition into nearest-neighbor graphs of the point configurations, à la Gunson-Panta. This is combined with new energy estimates obtained via an electric reformulation of the interaction energy and a ball-growth method, which are expressed in terms of the nearest-neighbor graph distances only. In this way, the model is compared to a reduced nearest-neighbor interaction model, by showing the relative smallness of the dipole-dipole interactions.
Problem

The paper studies the two-dimensional two-component plasma: N positive and N negative charges with logarithmic interaction, regularized by smearing the charges over a length scale λ. The aim is to understand the free energy and dipole pairing for β ≥ 2, complementing prior work showing that no pairing occurs for β < 2.

Approach

Point configurations are decomposed into nearest-neighbor graphs following Gunson–Panta. This is combined with energy estimates from an electric reformulation of the interaction and a ball-growth method, expressed only in terms of nearest-neighbor distances. The model is then compared to a reduced nearest-neighbor model by showing that dipole-dipole interactions are relatively small. Results
