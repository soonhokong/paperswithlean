Sharp Transitions of Copy Complexity in Local Certification of Typical Pure States

Yimeng Cao, Ge Bai

quant-ph Nov 25, 2026 · v1

TL;DR
Main theorems on certification copy complexity (Theorem 1, Corollary 2, Theorem 3) are formally verified in Lean 4 using Mathlib and Lean-QIT.
Abstract
We determine the asymptotic copy complexity of $n$-qudit state certification with tensor-product measurements. For given acceptance and rejection fidelities $a$ and $b$ ($b<a$), the optimal copy complexity is $Θ(n^r)$. For each qudit dimension $d$, $r$ is a piecewise constant function of $g=(a-b)/(1-b)$. This scaling holds for a family of sets of pure target states whose Haar measures tend to one as $n\to\infty$. We characterize the range of $g$ corresponding to each optimal exponent and provide an algorithm for computing its endpoints exactly. The optimal exponent grows logarithmically with $1/g$ as $g$ tends to zero. The upper bound is achieved by a nonadaptive protocol using only single-qudit projective measurements and classical postprocessing. The matching lower bound is established by constructing pairs of states that are difficult to distinguish under arbitrary tensor-product measurements. Our result can thus guide the design of certification tests when the number of available copies is limited.
Problem

The work determines the asymptotic number of copies needed to certify an n-qudit pure state using tensor-product measurements, given acceptance and rejection fidelity thresholds a > b. It asks how the optimal exponent depends on the normalized gap g = (a-b)/(1-b) for typical (Haar-random) targets.

Approach

Upper bounds come from a nonadaptive protocol using random single-qudit projective measurements and a classically computed complement score built from polynomial envelopes. Lower bounds come from pairs of hard-to-distinguish states, whose KL divergence is bounded under arbitrary product measurements, combined with a sequential-information argument. Step points are characterized via extremal duality and computed exactly with finite rational certificates. The main theorems are formally verified in Lean 4 with Mathlib and the Lean-QIT library.

Results

The optimal copy complexity is Θ(n^r log(1/δ)), where r is a piecewise constant function of g. The step points decay as Θ((d+1)^{-j}/√j), so r grows logarithmically in 1/g. Low-degree step points are given exactly, for example 1/(d+1) and 1/((d+1)^2+1).
