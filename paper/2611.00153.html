On Small Universal Reversible Turing Machines

T. Yokoyama

cs.FL Oct 30, 2026 · v2 cs.LO
formalization mathlib logic
Read PDF arXiv abstract Code
TL;DR
All definitions and theorems, including the URTM constructions, universality proofs, and nonexistence results, are formalized in Lean 4 with Mathlib, with kernel-checked computations.
Abstract
A reversible Turing machine (RTM) is a Turing machine in which every instantaneous description has at most one predecessor. Writing $\mathrm{URTM}(m,n)$ for a universal RTM with $m$ states and $n$ symbols, we construct $\mathrm{URTM}(3,600)$, $\mathrm{URTM}(4,38)$, $\mathrm{URTM}(5,36)$, $\mathrm{URTM}(6,28)$, $\mathrm{URTM}(7,26)$, $\mathrm{URTM}(8,21)$, $\mathrm{URTM}(9,8)$, $\mathrm{URTM}(10,7)$, and $\mathrm{URTM}(85,2)$; each of them has fewer states or fewer symbols than each of the nine URTMs in Morita's book (2017). Most of them come from improved versions of Morita's conversions to three states, to four states, and to two symbols; for example, the improved conversion to three states uses $O(mn^2)$ symbols instead of $O(m^2n^3)$. $\mathrm{URTM}(10,7)$ comes from merging two states of Morita's $\mathrm{URTM}(13,7)$, recording the difference in tags on the tape cells, and then merging symbols, three times; one more such step gives $\mathrm{URTM}(9,8)$. We also show that the halting problem of RTMs with at most two states is decidable for finite instantaneous descriptions, so that no RTM with at most two states is universal under any notion of universality that reduces the halting problem of Turing machines to it by a computable encoding into finite instantaneous descriptions; in particular, there is no URTM with at most two states in our formalization of Morita's definition. We also show that no three-state, two-symbol RTM is universal in that formalization.
Problem

The question is how small a universal reversible Turing machine (URTM) can be, measured by its numbers of states and symbols. A related open question from Morita is whether any two-state RTM is universal. Morita's book lists nine URTMs, three of which come from conversions acknowledged to be wasteful.

Approach

The authors improve Morita's conversions to three states, to four states, and to two symbols. The techniques are disjoint counter ranges by direction, reverse counting, block decomposition of state numbers, shared counters, and symbol merging via search. They formalize Morita's definition of universality using Mathlib's computable functions. Nonexistence is proved by showing that halting is decidable for two-state RTMs and for three-state two-symbol RTMs on finite instantaneous descriptions. All results are machine-checked in Lean 4 with no sorry and only the standard axioms.

Results

New URTMs include (3,600), (4,41), (5,37), (6,30), (7,28), (8,21), and (85,2); together with six of Morita's machines they give 13 non-dominated points. No URTM exists with at most two states, and no three-state two-symbol RTM is universal in the formalized definition. The formalized model also uncovered one discrepancy in a step count in Morita's test data.
