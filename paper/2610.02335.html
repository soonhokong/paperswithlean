A Lean 4 Framework for the Radii Polynomial Method

Fengyang Wang, Konstantin Mischaikow, Marcio Gameiro

math.DS Oct 1, 2026 · v2
formalization verification mathlib analysis
Read PDF arXiv abstract
TL;DR
Formalizes the radii polynomial method for computer-assisted proofs in Lean 4 with Mathlib, checking rational certificates via LeanCert and custom tactics.
Abstract
Computer-assisted proofs in dynamics establish results about nonlinear systems by rigorous numerical computation. Their correctness rests on a trusted base of interval-arithmetic libraries and analytic estimates checked by hand. We formalize in Lean 4 a framework for the radii polynomial method, which certifies an exact solution near a numerical approximation by verifying four norm bounds and the resulting polynomial inequality. Weighted coefficient algebras provide the common setting for polynomial equations and initial value problems in Taylor and Chebyshev series. Their universal properties construct the bounded operators and the evaluation maps, and the universal property of the free commutative algebra makes polynomial substitution commute with evaluation. Finite/tail reductions turn the four norm bounds into finite rational inequalities, which are checked in Lean. The radii theorem then yields an exact coefficient solution, and realization theorems carry it to a solution of the original equation. The worked examples are a square-root branch given by a convergent power series and polynomial initial value problems, among them the Lorenz system, for which the library proves existence, uniqueness within the trajectory ball, and analyticity of the function-level solution.
Problem

Computer-assisted proofs in dynamics rely on trusted interval-arithmetic libraries and hand-checked analytic estimates. The radii polynomial method, which certifies an exact solution near a numerical approximation, had no machine-checked foundation.

Approach

The radii polynomial theorem is proved in Lean 4 for maps between Banach spaces, using Mathlib's mean value and contraction mapping theorems. Weighted l^1 coefficient algebras over index monoids are constructed, and their universal properties give bounded operators, evaluation maps, and polynomial substitution compatible with evaluation. Finite/tail reductions turn the four norm bounds into finite rational inequalities, which are checked in Lean via LeanCert and custom tactics such as finsum_bound and finmatrix_bound. Realization theorems carry coefficient solutions back to the original equations in Taylor and Chebyshev bases.

Results

Worked examples include a square-root branch given by a convergent power series and polynomial IVPs, including the Lorenz system. For Lorenz, the library proves existence, uniqueness within the trajectory ball, and analyticity of the function-level solution. Some infrastructure, including a discrete-convolution API, was contributed to Mathlib.
