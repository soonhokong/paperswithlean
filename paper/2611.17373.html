Smale's mean value constant (1981) equals 4

Dave Goel

TL;DR
The main theorem, that Smale's mean value constant equals 4, is formalized in Lean 4, checked with the Lean comparator, and released on GitHub.
Abstract
For a complex polynomial $p$ of degree $d\ge 2$ with $p(0)=0$ and $p'(0)\ne 0$, let $S(p)$ be the minimum of $|p(c)/(c\,p'(0))|$ over the critical points $c$ of $p$, and let $K(d)$ be the supremum of $S(p)$ over all such $p$ of degree $d$. In 1981 Smale proved that $K(d)\le 4$ for every $d$, and asked whether $4$ can be replaced by $1$. We prove that Smale's mean value constant $\sup_dK(d)$ equals $4$ and that $K(d)\to4$ as $d\to\infty$, combining a reduction due to Jatar and Ng with an explicit construction. Each $K(d)$ is known to be below $4$, so the supremum is not attained. The main result has also been formalized in the Lean 4 proof assistant.
Problem

Smale (1981) proved that for complex polynomials with p(0)=0 and p'(0)≠0, the minimum over critical points c of |p(c)/(c p'(0))| is at most 4. He asked whether 4 can be replaced by 1. The problem is to determine the best constant K* = sup_d K(d).

Approach

The authors use a reduction due to Jatar and Ng, which turns lower bounds on K* into the construction of pairs of holomorphic functions on the disc via Hilbert's lemniscate theorem. They build an explicit function F as an entire function composed with a conformal map onto a half strip, and a univalent map W built from logarithmic perturbations of the upper half-plane. Comparing these with a scaled Koebe function gives lower bounds approaching 4. The main theorem is also formalized in Lean 4.

Results

K* = lim K(d) = 4, so the supremum of Smale's constant is 4 and is not attained, since each K(d) < 4. The proof is non-effective, and a Lean 4 proof of the main theorem is publicly available.
