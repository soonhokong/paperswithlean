Abstract
A Lean 4 and Mathlib proof that at least one of the 2-adic zeta values ζ₂(7), ζ₂(9), ζ₂(11) is irrational. The theorem has no hypotheses, and its proof uses only the axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. The values are defined through the Volkenborn integral: zeta2 s = J(s−1)/((s−1)·2^s), where J(s) is the 2-adic limit of the Riemann sums 2^(−N) Σ_{x<2^N} (x+1/2)^(−s), and the theorem also proves that these limits exist. By Lemma 2.8 of Lai, Sprang and Zudilin (IMRN 2026), zeta2 s is the Kubota–Leopoldt value ζ₂(s) = L₂(s, ω^(1−s)) for s ≥ 2. That identification is cited, not formalised; it only involves a non-zero rational factor, which does not affect irrationality. The result sharpens Theorem 1.1 (case s = 3) of L. Lai (Int. J. Number Theory 2025), which shows that at least one of ζ₂(7), ζ₂(9), ζ₂(11), ζ₂(13) is irrational. The proof uses linear forms S_n = −∫ R_n”'(t+1/2) dt in 1, ζ₂(7), ζ₂(9), ζ₂(11), with R_n(t) = 2^(16n)(2t+n)(t+1/2)_n^8/(t)_(n+1)^8. Their denominators are controlled by a one-power saving that comes from Andrews' multiple-series transformation (proved here in Lean) and by the prime number theorem (proved here from a vendored Lean proof of the Wiener–Ikehara theorem). The exact 2-adic valuation of S_n along n = 2^m − 1 shows that S_n ≠ 0, and Lai's irrationality criterion concludes. Problem

The goal is to show that at least one of the 2-adic zeta values ζ₂(7), ζ₂(9), ζ₂(11) is irrational. This sharpens Lai's earlier result, which needed ζ₂(13) as well.

Approach

The 2-adic zeta values are defined via the Volkenborn integral as 2-adic limits of Riemann sums, and the existence of these limits is proved. Linear forms S_n in 1, ζ₂(7), ζ₂(9), ζ₂(11) are built from a rational function R_n. Their denominators are bounded using Andrews' multiple-series transformation and the prime number theorem, both proved in Lean; the prime number theorem comes from a vendored proof of Wiener–Ikehara. Nonvanishing of S_n follows from computing its exact 2-adic valuation along n = 2^m − 1, and Lai's irrationality criterion finishes the argument.

Results

The theorem has no hypotheses and uses only the standard axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound.
