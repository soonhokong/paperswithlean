Abstract
A Lean 4 formalization of Guy Robin's 1984 equivalence between the Riemann hypothesis and the strict divisor-sum inequality sigma(n) < exp(gamma) n log(log n) for every integer n > 5040. The development also proves the restriction to colossally abundant integers, reconstructs the Nicolas–Landau oscillation argument used for the converse implication, and checks the finite ranges with exact Lean certificates. It also formalizes Lagarias's 2001 elementary harmonic-number criterion and its equivalence to the Riemann hypothesis. This is a formalization of equivalence criteria; it does not prove the Riemann hypothesis.

Problem

Robin (1984) showed that the Riemann hypothesis is equivalent to the inequality sigma(n) < exp(gamma) n log(log n) for all n > 5040. Lagarias (2001) gave a related elementary criterion based on harmonic numbers. The goal is to machine-check both equivalences.

Approach

A Lean 4 development proves the reduction of Robin's inequality to colossally abundant integers. It reconstructs the Nicolas–Landau oscillation argument needed for the converse direction. Finite ranges are handled with exact certificates checked in Lean. Lagarias's harmonic-number criterion and its equivalence to the Riemann hypothesis are also formalized.

Results

The development gives machine-checked proofs of Robin's and Lagarias's equivalence criteria for the Riemann hypothesis. It proves only the equivalences, not the Riemann hypothesis itself.
