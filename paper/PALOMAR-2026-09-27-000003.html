Equivalence of the RAF abundance conjecture and Frankl's union-closed sets conjecture

Abstract
In a catalytic reaction system with finitely many reactions, a RAF is a nonempty set of reactions whose reactants are produced from the food set by iterated closure under the selected reactions and whose reactions are each catalysed by a molecule of that closure. Steel (arXiv:2305.16714v4, Section 4) asked whether some reaction always lies in at least half of the RAFs. Call a reaction food-ready if all its reactants are food, and abundant if it lies in at least half of the sets in the family formed by the RAFs and the empty set. If some RAF U consists entirely of food-ready reactions, then some reaction of U is abundant, with no condition on the other reactions. If every reaction of the system, except at most two, is food-ready and the system has a RAF, then some reaction is abundant. Finally, the statement that every catalytic reaction system with finitely many reactions and a RAF has an abundant reaction is equivalent to Frankl's union-closed sets conjecture in the form: every finite union-closed family containing the empty set and a nonempty set has an element lying in at least half of its members.

Problem

Steel asked whether, in a finite catalytic reaction system, some reaction always lies in at least half of the RAFs (reflexively autocatalytic food-generated sets). The question is how this abundance question relates to Frankl's union-closed sets conjecture.

Approach

A reaction is called food-ready if all its reactants are food, and abundant if it lies in at least half of the sets in the family of RAFs together with the empty set. Special cases are handled by relating RAF families to union-closed families, and a general equivalence is established. The results are formalized in Lean and machine-checked in the Palomar registry.

Results

If some RAF consists entirely of food-ready reactions, then one of its reactions is abundant. If all but at most two reactions are food-ready and a RAF exists, then some reaction is abundant. The general abundance statement is equivalent to Frankl's union-closed sets conjecture.
