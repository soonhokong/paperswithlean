TL;DR
Provides Lean statements and machine-checked Lean proofs of irrationality criteria for reciprocal Mersenne subseries sum 1/(b^a - 1).
Abstract
For an infinite set A of positive integers and an integer base b at least 2, consider the reciprocal Mersenne subseries X_A(b) = sum over a in A of 1/(b^a - 1). For a finite nonempty set P of primes, write h_P(a) for the P-part of a. The principal theorem proves irrationality when sum over a in A of h_P(a)/(a*(b^h_P(a) - 1)) converges. The base-two condition implies irrationality at every integer base at least 2 for every infinite subset of the host support. This is a sufficient support criterion, not a solution of the universal Erdős problem. It includes reciprocal-summable supports; the paper credits Erdős for that earlier stated all-base criterion and makes no identification with his suggested weakenings. The five selected statements also give cofinally arbitrarily small positive weighted displacements, a strengthened positive divisor-cover criterion, and two common-average mixed-support extensions, one using an arbitrary positive weight budget. The proof averages displacements along multiples of a finite divisibility modulus, with another average controlling incomplete periods. Rationality would instead separate every positive displacement from zero. These extensions retain their explicit cover, summability, positivity and infinite-subset assumptions. Full support and all odd exponents are outside the weighted criterion; failure of the criterion is not a rationality assertion. The paper's achievement-set geometry, rational-membership tests and finite-denominator results are outside this registration. Problem

For an infinite set A of positive integers and base b ≥ 2, the question is when X_A(b) = Σ_{a∈A} 1/(b^a − 1) is irrational. This relates to an Erdős problem, which the entry does not resolve in general.

Approach

For a finite set P of primes, h_P(a) denotes the P-part of a. The main theorem proves irrationality when Σ h_P(a)/(a(b^{h_P(a)} − 1)) converges. The proof averages displacements along multiples of a finite divisibility modulus, with a second average controlling incomplete periods; rationality would force every positive displacement to be separated from zero.
