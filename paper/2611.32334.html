Hadwigerś Covering Conjecture in Three Dimensions

Dror Aiger, Sariel Har-Peled, Goran Žužić

TL;DR

Abstract
Let $K \subset \R^3$ be an arbitrary convex body, that is, a compact convex subset of $\R^3$ with nonempty interior. We prove unconditionally that $K$ can be covered by eight smaller positive homothetic copies of itself: there exist a scalar $λ\in (0, 1)$ and translation vectors $v_1, \dots, v_8 \in \R^3$ such that \[ K \subseteq \bigcup_{i=1}^{8} (λK + v_i). \] This resolves Hadwiger's covering conjecture in three dimensions. The proof works in Boltyanski's equivalent boundary illumination formulation and is completely self-contained. Parallelepipeds are covered directly by eight translates of $\frac{2}{3} K$. For any convex body $K$ that is not a parallelepiped, we place its centrally symmetric halved difference body $S = \frac{1}{2}(K - K)$ in John's position ($B^3 \subseteq S$) and show that the spherical average of its support function satisfies $8 \int_{S^2} h_S \, d\sigmatilde < 12$, with equality only for orthogonal cubes. Using outer polyhedral approximations together with Cauchy's planar perimeter formula, the full spherical measure of directions not parallel to any boundary segment of $K$, and lower semicontinuity of curve variation, we find a unit vector $w_* \in S^2$ whose shadow boundary $\Shad{K}{w_*}$ on $K$ itself is a continuous closed curve of $S$-length strictly less than $12$. Splitting $\Shad{K}{w_*}$ into six contiguous arcs of $S$-length strictly less than $2$ and applying Helly's theorem in $\R^3$ illuminates each arc with one direction, while $\pm w_*$ illuminate the remainder of $\partial K$. The proof was discovered using an experimental harness on top of Gemini 4 Carbon and formally verified in Lean 4.
Problem

Hadwiger's covering conjecture states that every convex body in R^d can be covered by 2^d smaller positive homothetic copies of itself. It was open for all d >= 3; the best previous bound for general bodies in R^3 was 14.

Approach

The proof uses Boltyanski's illumination formulation. Parallelepipeds are covered directly by eight translates of (2/3)K. For other bodies, the halved difference body is placed in John's position, and a sharp mean-width inequality, outer polyhedral approximation, and Cauchy's perimeter formula yield a direction whose shadow boundary has S-length below 12. That boundary is split into six arcs, each illuminated via Helly's theorem, with ±w illuminating the rest; the proof was found with an AI harness on Gemini and formalized in Lean 4 with Mathlib.

Results

Every convex body in R^3 can be covered by eight smaller homothets, which resolves the conjecture in three dimensions. The Lean formalization (34 files) was checked against a standalone challenge statement through kernel replay, with no sorry, only standard axioms, and supporting statement-faithfulness audits.
