A $(17/7-o(1))n$ Circuit Lower Bound for Binary-Encoded 3-SAT

Theodoros Yianni, Sahibzada Allahyar

cs.CC Nov 23, 2026 · v1 math.CO
formalization mathlib combinatorics logic
Read PDF arXiv abstract Code
TL;DR
Lean 4 with Mathlib formalizes the finite inequality and asymptotic 17/7 circuit lower bound for arbitrary acyclic binary circuits, archived on Zenodo.
Abstract
We strengthen the elementary $n-1$ circuit lower bound for a specified binary encoding of 3-SAT to $(17/7)n-O(n\log\log n/\log n)$ gates over the full two-input Boolean basis, with unrestricted depth and sharing. For each integer $w\ge5$, we encode 3-CNF formulas with exactly $m=2^w-1$ clauses over $m$ available variables using $n=3m(w+1)$ bits. The proof embeds Boolean tests in variable identifiers while retaining $n-o(n)$ free bits, and combines weighted gate elimination with a nonlinear-gate lower bound from the tests' full algebraic degree over $\mathbb F_2$. We provide Lean proofs of the finite inequality and the asymptotic bound for arbitrary acyclic binary circuits.
Problem

The known elementary circuit lower bound for a specified binary encoding of 3-SAT is n-1 gates. The goal is a stronger linear bound over the full two-input Boolean basis, measured against every bit of the formula description, with depth and sharing unrestricted.

Approach

3-CNF formulas with m=2^w-1 clauses over m variables are encoded in n=3m(w+1) bits. Helper clauses force selected variables false, which embeds Boolean tests in the variable identifiers while leaving n-o(n) bits free. Weighted gate elimination uses a potential function that drops by at least 14 per eliminated input. This is combined with a Schnorr-type count of nonlinear gates that follows from the tests' full algebraic degree over F_2. The finite bound and both asymptotic estimates are formalized in Lean 4.35.0 with Mathlib, using only the standard axioms.

Results

The 3-SAT function requires at least (17/7)n - O(n log log n / log n) gates over the full binary basis. A variant based on a weighted sum modulo a prime gives (17/7)n - O(n/sqrt(log n)). The Lean proofs are archived on Zenodo.
