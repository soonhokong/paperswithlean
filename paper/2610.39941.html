Block-exchange descent for insertion: commuting projections and minimum masks

Haobo Ma, Wenlin Zhang, Charles E. Hughes

cs.FL Oct 29, 2026 · v2 math.CO
formalization combinatorics mathlib
Read PDF arXiv abstract Code
TL;DR
The iteration-depth no-gap theorem for arbitrary languages over any alphabet type is formalized in Lean 4 with Mathlib, released as a pinned artifact.
Abstract
For fixed nonempty words $u,v$, the minimum insertion degree of a shuffle is the least number of inserted-source runs among its representations. We prove that, after a block exchange from a minimum mask of degree $r$, the minimum over masks with commuting source projections is exactly $r-1$; a larger decrease occurs precisely when a noncommuting mask has fewer runs. If either source is unary, every such exchange is exact. We prove an explicit repair for an infinite periodic degree-three family. Computer-assisted results establish first-mask descent through combined length seventeen over every alphabet, degree-three first-mask descent through length thirty, and a uniform repair of all degree-three failures through length thirty, including all further letter identifications. Counterexamples to arbitrary, first, and last minimum-mask choices explain the need for alternative masks. Finally, we prove that minimum iteration depths have no gaps for arbitrary languages. General block-exchange descent and Singleton No-Gap remain open.
Problem

For fixed nonempty words u, v, the minimum insertion degree of a shuffle is the least number of inserted-source runs over its representations. The paper asks whether a block exchange from a minimum mask always lowers the degree by exactly one, and whether degree spectra have no gaps.

Approach

Block exchanges are analyzed through commuting source projections, which shows the commuting-mask minimum after an exchange is exactly r-1. Exactness is proved for unary sources, and an explicit label-shift repair is given for a periodic degree-three family. Exhaustive symbolic search over letter-equality patterns, with checked certificates, supports the finite claims. The no-gap theorem for minimum iteration depths is formalized in Lean 4 with Mathlib.

Results

First-mask descent holds through combined length 17 over every alphabet, and degree-three first-mask descent and uniform repair hold through length 30. Counterexamples rule out arbitrary, first, and last minimum-mask choices. Minimum iteration depths have no gaps for arbitrary languages, while general block-exchange descent and Singleton No-Gap remain open.
