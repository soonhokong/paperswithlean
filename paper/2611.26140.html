Optimal Patrol Widths in the Infinite-Corridor Barrier-Guarding Game

Kevin M. Boergens

TL;DR
An accompanying Lean 4/Mathlib library formalizes the wall-budget algebra, phase identities, root bracket and excursion inequalities, and replays the rational surface and continuum certificates in the kernel.
Abstract
A guard of speed at most one and capture radius $r$ patrols an infinite corridor. An intruder knows the patrol and can follow any path of speed at most $q$ from one end to the other. We determine where the maximum width permitted by straight wall runners can be guarded against all such paths. Write $W_{\mathrm{opt}}(q)$ for the supremum over patrols with bounded longitudinal excursion and $F(q)=2r\sqrt{1+(3q^2+2q-1)^{-1}}$ for the wall ceiling on $1/3 < q < 1$. A four-leg bowtie attains $F(q)$ exactly for $q_a\le q < 1$, where $0.457892036651 < q_a < 0.457892036652$. For every $1/3 < q < q_a$ the supremum is strictly smaller. As $q$ approaches $q_a$ from below, $F(q)-W_{\mathrm{opt}}(q)$ is bounded above and below by positive constant multiples of $r(q_a-q)^2$. The lower estimate controls near-optimal patrols uniformly in their period. A rescaled bowtie gives the matching upper estimate. We also prove that periodic patrols give the same supremum as bounded-excursion patrols. For $q\ge1$ the one-way optimum is $r\sqrt{4+q^{-2}}$, a transverse sweep. This sweep is optimal against transit in both directions for $q\ge1/2$. At low speeds, $qW_{\mathrm{opt}}(q)/r$ tends to two, and an explicit ten-leg patrol guards width greater than $(2/q-1.235353)r$ throughout $0 < q\le1/40$. Rational spacetime and flux certificates give finite-speed bounds, including $6.944691333740r < W_{\mathrm{opt}}(1/4)\le8.10r$.
Problem

A unit-speed guard with capture radius r patrols an infinite corridor against an intruder of speed at most q who knows the patrol. The task is to find the maximal width W_opt(q) that a patrol with bounded longitudinal excursion can guard against every transit path.

Approach

A phase-cylinder speed budget gives a straight-wall ceiling F(q) for 1/3<q<1. Four-leg bowtie patrols are shown to capture all transits using two-level and obstacle-wavefront separator constructions. Periodic polygonal approximation, ordered wall-contact control, and midline-excursion bounds handle the remaining cases. Finite-speed bounds come from exact rational spacetime-surface and flux certificates, checked by a Python script and replayed in the Lean 4 kernel with Mathlib.

Results

The bowtie attains F(q) exactly for q_a≤q<1, with q_a≈0.457892036651. Below q_a the optimum is strictly smaller, with the gap of quadratic order in q_a−q. For q≥1 the optimum is r√(4+q^{-2}), and this transverse sweep is optimal for two-way transit when q≥1/2. Certified bounds include 6.9447r < W_opt(1/4) ≤ 8.10r.
