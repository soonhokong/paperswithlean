Abstract
A Lean 4 formalization, built on Mathlib, of the basic theory of locally convex topological vector spaces over the real or complex numbers. It covers barrelled, quasi-barrelled, bornological and ultrabornological spaces; the closed graph and open mapping theorems for barrelled spaces, and Pták's closed graph and open mapping theorems; duality theory with the bipolar, Alaoglu–Bourbaki, Mackey, Mackey–Arens, Banach–Dieudonné and Krein–Šmulian theorems and Grothendieck's completeness theorem; semi-reflexive, reflexive and Montel spaces with their permanence properties; completeness of strong duals; countable strict inductive limits, with the Dieudonné–Schwartz theorem; Milman's converse to the Krein–Milman theorem; and webbed spaces with De Wilde's closed graph and open mapping theorems. The Palomar entry compares 52 theorems from these areas; their statements and the notions they use are in Challenge.lean. The results are classical textbook and research results; the formalization proves them without sorry and without additional axioms.

Problem

Much of the classical theory of locally convex topological vector spaces over the real or complex numbers had not been formalized. This includes barrelled and bornological spaces, duality theory, and closed graph theorems.

Approach

A Lean 4 library built on Mathlib develops barrelled, quasi-barrelled, bornological, ultrabornological, semi-reflexive, reflexive, Montel and webbed spaces. It also covers countable strict inductive limits. It proves the main duality theorems: bipolar, Alaoglu–Bourbaki, Mackey–Arens, Banach–Dieudonné, Krein–Šmulian, and Grothendieck completeness. It proves closed graph and open mapping theorems in the barrelled, Pták and De Wilde settings. Statements of 52 compared theorems are collected in Challenge.lean.

Results

All 52 target theorems are proved without sorry and without additional axioms, and the Palomar registry machine-checked the proofs. The theorems include the Dieudonné–Schwartz theorem, Milman's converse to Krein–Milman, and completeness of strong duals.
