Beyond Brown's radial tests: admissibility, parabolicity, and a doubling-Poincaré prior that neither test decides

Abstract
For the estimation of a normal mean in $\mathbb{R}^m$ under quadratic loss, Brown (1971) proved that a generalized Bayes estimator $δ_F$ with bounded drift is admissible if and only if a diffusion attached to the marginal $f^*$ of the prior $F$ is recurrent, and gave two radial tests: a sphere-average test for recurrence and a ray test for transience. He observed that both tests can be silent. We read his theory as the potential theory of the weighted Euclidean space $(\mathbb{R}^m,f^*\,dx)$ and ask what the modern theory of recurrence on weighted manifolds adds inside the zone where neither test applies. Three results. (A) If $f^*\,dx$ is doubling and supports a scale-invariant Poincaré inequality, the sphere test is already complete: recurrence, Brown's sphere test and the ball-volume test are equivalent, so geometric criteria can add decidable cases only on the inadmissible side. (B) They do add one. An explicit prior on $\mathbb{R}^3$ whose marginal is small along a lattice of circles at dyadic scales with spacing equal to the square root of the scale is doubling and Poincaré, silences both of Brown's tests, and is transient by the ball-volume test; its generalized Bayes estimator is inadmissible. (C) Outside the doubling-Poincaré class the zone is two-sided: similar circles placed on one surface per scale give an admissible estimator, decided by a flux bound along a non-spherical exhaustion, and a logarithmic product prior gives an inadmissible one, decided by product heat kernels. A comparison lemma shows that any positive weight with Lipschitz logarithm is, up to constants, the marginal of a prior, so that explicit weights may be used throughout.
Problem

Brown (1971) showed that admissibility of generalized Bayes estimators of a normal mean is equivalent to recurrence of a diffusion attached to the prior's marginal. He gave a radial sphere test and a radial ray test, and either test can be silent. The question is what modern parabolicity theory on weighted manifolds can decide inside this undecided zone.

Approach

Brown's theory is read as potential theory on the weighted space (R^m, f* dx). Three tools are used: doubling-Poincaré (D+P) criteria, explicit priors built from lattices of circles with small marginal, and flux bounds along non-spherical exhaustions. A comparison lemma shows that any weight with Lipschitz logarithm is comparable to a prior marginal. Poincaré constants are checked numerically, and part of the mathematics is machine-checked in Lean 4/Mathlib, with cited results carried as explicit hypotheses.

Results

Under D+P, recurrence, Brown's sphere test and the ball-volume test are equivalent. An explicit D+P prior on R^3 silences both of Brown's tests yet is transient, so its estimator is inadmissible. Outside the D+P class, the undecided zone contains both admissible and inadmissible examples.
