The Fundamental Theorem of Asset Pricing, Formalized in Lean 4

Raphael Coelho

TL;DR
Formalizes the Fundamental Theorem of Asset Pricing in Lean 4 over Mathlib in finite-state and one-period settings, sorry-free with an axiom audit.
Abstract
The Fundamental Theorem of Asset Pricing states that a market is free of arbitrage exactly when it admits an equivalent martingale measure. We formalize it in Lean 4 over Mathlib in three settings: a finite-state market with one risky asset over a finite horizon (Harrison–Pliska), and one-period markets with one asset or finitely many assets on an arbitrary probability space. The finite case is the geometry of a separating hyperplane. In the one-period case the proof builds the equivalent martingale measure from a minimiser of the potential $θ\mapsto\E[\log(1+e^{\langleθ,Y\rangle})]$: absence of arbitrage makes the potential coercive off the redundant directions, its first-order condition is the martingale property, and the logistic weight of the minimiser, normalised, is the density; a bounded tempering removes integrability, so the return need only be measurable. The construction uses no Hahn–Banach theorem, no $L^0$-closedness argument, no measurable selection and no non-redundancy hypothesis. One predicate serves every time index, with one forward theorem. The same separation also yields the Artzner–Delbaen–Eber–Heath representation of coherent risk measures on a finite state space. We add the second theorem on finite spaces (complete if and only if the martingale measure is unique) and, on any probability space, the reduction of the Dalang–Morton–Willinger theorem to its conditional one-period form. That conditional step, which needs a measurable selection, is assumed, not proved, so the multi-period theorem remains open; in continuous time only the forward direction, for simple strategies, is proved. Every theorem discussed is sorry-free and uses only Lean's three standard axioms, enforced by the build.
Problem

The Fundamental Theorem of Asset Pricing says a market has no arbitrage exactly when it admits an equivalent martingale measure. It had not previously been machine-checked in any proof assistant.

Approach

The theorem is formalized in Lean 4 over Mathlib in three settings. The finite-state multi-period (Harrison–Pliska) case uses a separating-hyperplane lemma against the standard simplex. The scalar one-period case on an arbitrary probability space uses a tempering density and a two-region balancing density. The d-asset one-period case builds the martingale measure from the minimiser of a softplus potential, whose logistic weight serves as the density, avoiding Hahn–Banach, L^0-closedness and measurable selection.

Results

All three biconditionals are sorry-free and use only Lean's standard axioms, enforced by an axiom-audit gate in the build. The work also covers the second FTAP on finite spaces, the coherent risk measure representation, and the reduction of Dalang–Morton–Willinger to its conditional one-period step, which is assumed rather than proved. The general multi-period theorem therefore remains open.
