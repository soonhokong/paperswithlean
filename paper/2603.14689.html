Less Is Harder Than More: Proving Unnecessary Complexity

Tristan Simas

TL;DR
A Lean 4 artifact mechanically verifies the optimizer-quotient universal property, reduction-correctness lemmas, finite deciders, and witness/checking schemas underlying the complexity results.
Abstract
When can a sensor, feature, or API field be removed without changing a fixed system's decisions? We require the remaining inputs to recover its complete optimal-action set, including ties, at every admitted state. Admitting every input combination guarantees one smallest preserving set. Yet checking one deletion is coNP-complete when circuits give utilities on Boolean products, even with two actions and at most two optimal-action sets. Missing combinations permit alternative preserving sets, so an auditor must choose inputs within budget and check the chosen set. Requiring every declared reading to have a positive-probability state adds a support check. We classify choosing and checking exactly for circuit-defined Boolean models, with and without the support requirement. A unique winner after averaging hidden states can still change the original decisions. Certifying such winners requires weighted score comparisons: finding inputs within budget is $\mathsf{NP}^{(\mathsf{NP}^{\mathsf{PP}})}$-complete even with two actions and independent uniform inputs. Explicit tables admit logarithmic approximation of the minimum preserving input count; succinct Boolean products admit no polynomial-time $o(Λ/\logΛ)$ approximation in total encoded length $Λ$, unless $\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}$. Supplied decompositions and symmetry proofs can make audits polynomial-time; discovery and validation are additional work. For a frozen digit classifier, eleven blocks suffice on held-out rows but fifteen are needed on the full product; four conflicting pairs expose the gap. Building the required response from retained inputs establishes sufficiency, not minimality. Preserve the structure that makes the dependence checkable.
Problem

The question is when input coordinates of a decision problem can be removed without changing the optimal-action set at every state. The paper also asks how hard it is to certify such exact relevance across static, stochastic, and sequential models.

Approach

The optimizer quotient is defined as the coarsest abstraction through which the optimal-action map factors. Complexity classifications are then derived for sufficiency checking, minimum sufficient sets, and anchor sufficiency in each regime, along with ETH lower bounds, approximation bounds, and tractable structural subcases. A Lean 4 artifact (Mathlib-based) mechanizes the finite combinatorial core: universal property, reductions, deciders, and bridge lemmas. The oracle-class placements are proved on paper rather than in Lean.

Results

In the static regime, sufficiency and minimum sufficiency are coNP-complete and anchor sufficiency is Σ2P-complete. Stochastic decisiveness is PP-hard in the succinct model, and all sequential queries are PSPACE-complete. Tractable cases include low tensor rank, tree structure, bounded treewidth, and coordinate symmetry.
