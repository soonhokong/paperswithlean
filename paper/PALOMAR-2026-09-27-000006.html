TL;DR
Lean 4 formalization proving no group satisfies Péterfalvi's hypothesis (B) for p = 3, plus the SL(2, 2^q) example for p = 2.
Abstract
Glauberman and Norton (Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 119, 1993) end their paper on a combinatorial problem that arises in the proof of the Feit–Thompson odd order theorem with a question of Péterfalvi: can the hypothesis of their Proposition 9 be satisfied for p = 3? The same question is Problem 1 in Appendix C of Bender and Glauberman, Local Analysis for the Odd Order Theorem (1994). Let F be the field with p^q elements, P its additive group, U its group of units of norm one over the prime field, H the semidirect product P ⋊ U, and P₀ the image of the additive group of the prime field in H. Hypothesis (B) asks for an injective homomorphism σ from H into a group G, a finite abelian subgroup Q of G whose order is prime to p, and an element y of Q, such that σ(P₀) normalizes Q and σ(P₀)^y normalizes σ(U). Proposition 9 also assumes condition (A): q does not divide p − 1. This project proves in Lean 4 that the answer is negative: for every prime q with q ∤ 3 − 1, no group G, finite or infinite, satisfies hypothesis (B) with p = 3. It also proves Péterfalvi's example from the same paper, which shows that SL(2, 2^q) satisfies hypothesis (B) with p = 2, so the hypothesis is not empty. The proof is new. It turns a witness of hypothesis (B) into relations between three copies of the additive group of the field with 3^q elements, and it rules out these relations by arithmetic in that field.

Problem

Péterfalvi asked whether the hypothesis of Glauberman–Norton's Proposition 9, which comes from a combinatorial problem in the Feit–Thompson odd order theorem, can be satisfied for p = 3. The same question appears as Problem 1 in Bender–Glauberman's Local Analysis for the Odd Order Theorem.

Approach

Hypothesis (B) concerns the semidirect product H = P ⋊ U, where P is the additive group of the field with p^q elements and U is its group of norm-one units. It asks for an injective homomorphism of H into a group G together with an abelian p'-subgroup Q and an element y satisfying certain normalization conditions. A witness of hypothesis (B) is turned into relations among three copies of the additive group of the field with 3^q elements. These relations are then ruled out by arithmetic in that field, and everything is formalized in Lean 4.

Results

For every prime q with q ∤ 2, no group G, finite or infinite, satisfies hypothesis (B) with p = 3, which answers Péterfalvi's question negatively with a new proof. Péterfalvi's example is also formalized: SL(2, 2^q) satisfies hypothesis (B) with p = 2, so the hypothesis is not vacuous.
