Gaps between roots in ballistic deposition on a strip: linear variance, Gaussian limits and a Poisson regime

Mateo Matijasevick

math.PR Oct 15, 2026 · v2
formalization mathlib probability combinatorics
Read PDF arXiv abstract
TL;DR
The root criterion, the valley/gap combinatorial lemmas, the exact moment formulas and a comparison step are formalized and machine-checked in Lean 4 with Mathlib.
Abstract
Mansour, Rastegar and Roitershtein studied the roots of ballistic deposition on a cyclic strip of $K$ sites, namely the sites that receive a particle at height one, and the number $D_{i,K}$ of pairs of consecutive roots at distance $i+1$. They computed the first two moments of $D_{i,K}$ for $i\le7$. They conjectured that for every $i$ the mean and the variance are exactly linear in $K$, that $D_{i,K}$ satisfies a central limit theorem, and that the standardized vector $(D_{i,K})_{i\ge1}$ converges to a Gaussian process. We prove these conjectures. The key observation is that the roots are exactly the valleys of a uniformly random circular arrangement, so that $D_{i,K}$ counts occurrences of a family of consecutive patterns. We determine sizes from which the moments are exactly linear; the size for the mean is sharp for every $i$, and those for the second moments cannot be lowered in any case we checked. We give closed forms for the variance constants and for the limiting covariance; the mean constants agree with a known distribution of distances between surface peaks. The reduction and the exact moment formulas are formalized in Lean 4 with Mathlib. We also prove a Wasserstein bound of order $(\mathbb{E} D_{i,K})^{-1/2}$ that is uniform in $i$, and a local limit theorem. Finally we describe the limit completely when $i$ grows with $K$: it is degenerate, Poisson or Gaussian according as $\mathbb{E} D_{i,K}$ tends to zero, to a positive constant, or to infinity.
Problem

Mansour, Rastegar and Roitershtein conjectured that the number D_{i,K} of consecutive root pairs at distance i+1 in ballistic deposition on a cyclic strip has mean and variance exactly linear in K. They also conjectured that it satisfies a CLT and that the standardized vector converges to a Gaussian process.

Approach

Roots are shown to be exactly the valleys of a uniformly random circular arrangement, via Poissonization. D_{i,K} then counts consecutive patterns built from locally dependent indicators. Exact moments come from window-overlap arguments and closed-form constants from an excursion kernel for unimodal sequences. Normal and Poisson approximations use Stein's method and the Chen–Stein method, and the deterministic lemmas are formalized in Lean 4.

Results

All three conjectures are proved, with exact linearity thresholds K ≥ i+4 for the mean and K ≥ 2i+7 for the variance; the mean threshold is sharp. Further results are an explicit formula for a_i, a Wasserstein bound 133/sqrt(a_i K) uniform in i, and a local limit theorem. When i grows with K, the limit is degenerate, Poisson or Gaussian according to the limit of a_i K.
