Ordering error estimates under mesh-uniform regularity and optimal two-mesh design for the KdV equation with boundary dissipation

Kangsheng Liu

Abstract
We study Galerkin error estimates and optimal two-mesh design for the Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) equation on a bounded interval with boundary dissipation. The ordering error is introduced to measure how much the nonlinearity changes the discretization error. A weighted cubic Hermite Galerkin scheme has mesh-uniform regularity: the discrete semigroups of the linearized equation inherit Crandall-Pazy regularity and Kato smoothing, with bounds uniform in the mesh size $h$. On a small ball of the energy space, the discrete solution maps are also analytic in the initial datum, with bounds uniform in $h$. On this ball the ordering error is at most of order $h^{1/6}$ times the squared energy norm of the datum, for all $t\ge0$. For $u_0$ on this ball and in the domain $D(A)$ of the linearized generator, the bound is of order $h^2\|u_0\|_{H^3}^{3/2}\|u_0\|_{L^2}^{1/2}$. A two-mesh computation adds a nonlinear correction on a coarse mesh to a linear simulation on a fine mesh. Its error is the linear error on the fine mesh plus the ordering error on the coarse mesh. Based on these error estimates, we formulate a model of the optimal two-mesh design: minimize the cost subject to an accuracy constraint given by the error bounds. For data in a small ball of $L^2$, the problem is convex in logarithmic mesh sizes. When the relative error bound for the coarsest allowed mesh exceeds the prescribed tolerance $η$, the optimal pair and its KKT multipliers are unique. For smooth data the problem is convex in the mesh sizes, and the optimal mesh ratio is independent of the tolerance until the coarse mesh reaches its upper limit. In both cases we characterize the small-amplitude limit of the relative cost when the relative tolerance is proportional to the amplitude.
Problem

The paper studies Galerkin error estimates for the KdV equation on a bounded interval with boundary dissipation. It introduces the ordering error, which measures how much the nonlinearity changes the discretization error, that is, whether linearizing and discretizing commute. It also asks how to design a cheap two-mesh computation from these estimates.

Approach

A weighted cubic Hermite Galerkin scheme (the W-scheme) is shown to have mesh-uniform Crandall–Pazy regularity, Kato smoothing and analyticity of the discrete solution maps. These properties give product-form bounds on the ordering error that hold for all time. A two-mesh method adds a nonlinear correction computed on a coarse mesh to a linear simulation on a fine mesh. Mesh selection is posed as a cost minimization subject to the accuracy constraint given by the error bounds, and the key theorems were checked in Lean 4 under certain premises.

Results

On a small ball of the energy space, the ordering error is bounded by O(h^{1/6}) times the squared datum norm for all t≥0, and by O(h^2) for smooth data in D(A). The two-mesh design problem is convex in logarithmic mesh sizes (small L^2 data) or in mesh sizes (smooth data), and the optimal pair and its KKT multipliers are unique under a stated condition. Numerical experiments illustrate the designs.
