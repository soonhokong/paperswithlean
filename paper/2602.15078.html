Computer Science as Infrastructure: the Spine of the Lean Computer Science Library (CSLib)

Christopher Henson, Fabrizio Montesi

cs.LO Feb 16, 2026 · v2 cs.PL

Read PDF arXiv abstract Code
TL;DR
Presents CSLib, a Lean 4 library of formalised computer science built on Mathlib, with semantic interfaces, grind-based automation, linters, and CI.
Abstract
Following in the footsteps of the success of Mathlib – the centralised library of formalised mathematics in Lean – CSLib is a rapidly-growing centralised library of formalised computer science and software. In this paper, we present its founding technical principles, operation, abstractions, and semantic framework. We contribute reusable semantic interfaces (reduction and labelled transition systems), proof automation, CI/testing support for maintaining automation and compatibility with Mathlib, and the first substantial developments of languages and models.
Problem

Mathlib provides a centralised library of formalised mathematics in Lean, but Lean has had no comparable unified library for computer science. Such a library needs shared abstractions for operational semantics and must stay compatible with Mathlib.

Approach

CSLib bundles reduction systems and labelled transition systems as Lean structures. From these it derives standard notions such as multistep reduction, reachability, and confluence, and it provides notation attributes that work with the calc tactic. Proof automation built on Lean's grind tactic is treated as a design constraint on interfaces. The library reuses Mathlib's linters and CI and adds stricter namespacing linters and weekly expensive checks.

Results

Developments include bisimilarity, similarity, and trace equivalence with up-to techniques, CCS, NFA-to-DFA subset construction, and lambda-calculi with polymorphism and subtyping. Declarations that adopted grind after their initial commit saved an average of 7.1 LoC per theorem, with 15 to 39 LoC saved on bisimilarity proofs. The System F formalization is about 45% smaller than the Rocq development it was modelled on.
