Lean-Verified No-Go Theorems for $R$-Symmetries and Modular Symmetries in 4D GUTs

Mu-Chun Chen, Xiang-Gan Liu, Michael Ratz, Alexander Stewart

hep-ph Nov 16, 2026 · v1 hep-th
formalization mathlib physics algebra
Read PDF arXiv abstract
TL;DR
Formalizes in Lean 4 on top of Mathlib a no-go theorem on R-symmetries in supersymmetric SU(5) GUTs, closing a gap in the published proof.
Abstract
$R$-symmetries are instrumental in supersymmetric model building. However, a no-go theorem restricts us from using them in a large class of four-dimensional (4D) grand unified theories (GUTs). Specifically, it has been shown that a supersymmetric 4D GUT based on SU(5), with finitely many chiral multiplets, cannot be broken spontaneously to the exact minimal supersymmetric standard model (MSSM) while preserving a discrete $\mathbb{Z}_M^R$, with $M \geq 3$, or continuous U(1) $R$-symmetry. We use Lean 4 to formalize this proof. In the process, we identify and close a gap in the general case of the published argument. Further, we discuss the implications for GUT models with modular symmetries. We point out that modular symmetries are inevitably $R$-symmetries in a supersymmetric framework that also describes gravity. At certain critical points in moduli space, discrete $R$-symmetries are linearly realized. In the context of 4D GUTs, the no-go theorem then implies that exotic states become massless at these points, provided the GUT-breaking vacuum preserves these symmetries. In the vicinity of these critical points, these exotics are parametrically light, which may spoil gauge coupling unification.
Problem

A published no-go theorem states that a supersymmetric 4D SU(5) GUT with finitely many chiral multiplets cannot break to the exact MSSM while preserving a Z_M^R (M>=3) or U(1)_R symmetry. Its proof had not been machine-checked, and its consequences for modular symmetries were not known.

Approach

The authors build a more general version of the theorem using index relations for the fermion mass map on Weyl-related roots, and formalize it in Lean 4 on top of Mathlib. They work directly in Lean rather than PhysLib, and credit Claude with help generating the Lean code. The general result is applied to SU(5) and to larger gauge groups containing SU(5). They also argue that modular symmetries are R-symmetries in supergravity.

Results

The Lean formalization yields a shorter proof and closes a gap in the general case of the published argument. Consequences: GUT models with modular symmetries have exotic states that become massless at critical points in moduli space, which may spoil gauge coupling unification. Higher-dimensional GUTs evade the theorem.
