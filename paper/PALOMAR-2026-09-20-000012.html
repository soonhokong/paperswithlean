Abstract
Conditioned on the presence of a run contained in a finite prefix, the first such run has a mixture law combining a microscopic border contribution with a uniform bulk location, geometric excess and fair sign. The results retain the marked record, describe both location scales in finite and infinite phase regimes, and determine the sign bias. Under compatible affine conditions on small primes, the border probability is exactly 2^(-d), where d is the row-space deficit. Affine localization and sign bias use the rank information budget without future neutrality; the full marked-clock limit also requires eventual neutrality for each fixed finite future prefix. Approximation uses the prime number theorem, the Poisson process estimate of Arratia, Goldstein and Gordon, and the stated Laishram–Shorey, Shorey and Nicolas–Robin estimates. The exact affine border identity needs none of these literature hypotheses.

Problem

The work concerns the law of the first run contained in a finite prefix, conditioned on such a run existing. It asks how this law splits into a microscopic border part and a bulk part, and what affine conditions on small primes imply for it.

Approach

The first-run law is written as a mixture of a microscopic border contribution and a uniform bulk location with geometric excess and fair sign. Location scales are analyzed in finite and infinite phase regimes. Under compatible affine conditions on small primes, a rank information budget is used, with eventual neutrality added for the full marked-clock limit. The approximation steps rely on the prime number theorem, the Arratia–Goldstein–Gordon Poisson estimate, and stated Laishram–Shorey, Shorey and Nicolas–Robin estimates as hypotheses. The Lean proofs were machine-checked by the Palomar registry.

Results

Under compatible affine conditions, the border probability is exactly 2^(-d), where d is the row-space deficit. This exact identity does not use the literature hypotheses. Affine localization and the sign bias are determined without assuming future neutrality.
