Linear forms in logarithms of rational numbers: an ineffective measure with the best possible exponent

Gregory V. Chudnovsky

TL;DR
The main theorem and corollary on linear forms in logarithms are fully verified in Lean 4 with Mathlib, with sources in ancillary files and declaration names cited throughout.
Abstract
Let $a_1,\dots,a_n$ be positive rational numbers without a nontrivial multiplicative relation. For every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a constant $C>0$, not effective, such that $|h_0+h_1\log a_1+\dots+h_n\log a_n| \ge C\max(1,|h_0|,\dots,|h_n|)^{-n-\varepsilon}$ for all integers $h_0,\dots,h_n$, not all zero. The exponent $n$ is best possible. For $n=1$ this is the known fact that the irrationality exponent of $\log a$ is $2$; for $h_0=0$ it gives the exponent $n$ for the linear forms $h_1\log a_1+\dots+h_n\log a_n$. The proof is an induction on $n$, starting from $n=1$. The step from $n-1$ to $n$ logarithms uses an interpolation determinant on $\mathbb{G}_m^n\times\mathbb{G}_a$ at the multiples of a rational point, in the manner of arXiv:2610.28079; its geometric part, a curve inequality, is proved by a second induction, on the number of torus coordinates, with the two-torus inequality of arXiv:2610.28079 as the base. The method is the interpolation and determinant method of OpenAI's preprint on the irrationality exponent of $π$; the case $n=1$ was obtained earlier by Liu, Jiang and Zhang (arXiv:2610.10192). The theorem and its corollary are verified in Lean 4 with Mathlib; the sources are in the ancillary files, on top of the Lean supplement of arXiv:2610.28079. This paper follows the formal proof: every definition, constant and intermediate statement is given in the form in which it is formalized, with the name of the Lean declaration.
Problem

For positive rationals a_1,...,a_n with no multiplicative relation, the paper seeks a lower bound for |h_0 + h_1 log a_1 + ... + h_n log a_n| with the best possible exponent n+ε. The case n=1 is the known fact that log a has irrationality exponent 2.

Approach

The proof is an induction on n. Each step uses an interpolation determinant on G_m^n × G_a at multiples of a rational point, following the interpolation and determinant method of OpenAI's preprint on the irrationality exponent of π. The geometric part, a curve inequality, is proved by a second induction on the number of torus coordinates. The whole argument is formalized in Lean 4 with Mathlib, building on an earlier Lean supplement, and the paper's text mirrors the formal declarations.

Results

An ineffective measure with the optimal exponent n+ε is proved for all n ≥ 1, together with the corollary for homogeneous forms (h_0 = 0). Both are machine-checked in Lean, as measureC_all and linear_forms_in_logarithms.
