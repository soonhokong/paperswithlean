Unary maps are restrictions of linear maps and $k$-ary maps are restrictions of homogeneous maps

Abstract
We study maps on a finite set $S$ under renaming elements of $S$ and extending $S$. We show that every unary map is linear and every $k$-ary map is homogeneous with respect to such renamings and embeddings.
Problem

The goal is the simplest functional description of an arbitrary map f on a finite set S, such as a cellular automaton rule, when S may be renamed and embedded into a commutative ring. Earlier work showed that unary maps are restrictions of linear maps over some Z/m, and that binary maps are not always represented by low-degree polynomials.

Approach

A refined lemma gives Sidon maps into Z/n satisfying φ(f(x)) = dφ(x), built using weights 2^i and adjugates of tI−A. These are lifted to multiplicative embeddings into F_p^× with distinct ratios. Explicit homogeneous polynomials are then built from products of linear forms. Lower bounds come from a rank criterion over prime fields applied to the shift, equality and inversion indicator maps.

Results

Every map S^k→S is represented by a homogeneous polynomial over some prime field F_p. Every binary map on an n-element set is n-separable over Z/m, while the shift and equality maps are not (n−2)-separable and not of total degree ≤ n−2. All results are formalized in Lean 4 with Mathlib.
