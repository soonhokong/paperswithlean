Superlinear complexity of the $(3/2)^n$ steering word

Ralf Stephan

Abstract
Write $(3/2)^n = m_n + \varepsilon_n$ with $m_n$ the nearest integer and $\varepsilon_n\in[-\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$, and let $T=(t_n)$, $t_n=2m_{n+1}-3m_n$, be the resulting steering word: the step-by-step record of the map $x\mapsto\tfrac32 x$ on the orbit of 1, coded by nearest-integer rounding. We prove that the subword complexity $p_T(k)$ of $T$ is superlinear, $p_T(k)/k\to\infty$. A pigeonhole argument reduces this to exponential pair repulsion of the orbit modulo one, which we establish by two applications of the $p$-adic Subspace Theorem: once in three variables, which handles all gaps between the two orbit points at once, and once in two variables (Ridout's theorem) for the single degenerate family of constant gap. The argument is completely formalized in Lean 4.
Problem

The steering word T records the nearest-integer rounding steps of the map x ↦ (3/2)x on the orbit of 1. The goal is to show that its subword complexity p_T(k) is superlinear, meaning p_T(k)/k → ∞.

Approach

An exact repetition identity shows that a repeated factor forces a Diophantine inequality between orbit points. A pigeonhole argument then reduces superlinearity to exponential pair repulsion of (3/2)^n modulo one. That repulsion is established with the p-adic Subspace Theorem, applied once in three variables and once via Ridout's theorem for the constant-gap family. The entire argument is formalized in Lean 4 with the subspace theorem as the single external axiom, with assistance from Claude Code.

Results

p_T is proved superlinear, along with the unconditional results that T is not eventually periodic and that p_T(k) ≥ (41/24)k − 3. The Lean formalization is available at github.com/rwst/Superlinear-Complexity.
