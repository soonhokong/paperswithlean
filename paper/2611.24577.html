Stafford's conjecture on cyclicity of torsion modules

Christopher Albert, Johanna Moser, Maximilian Philipp

math.RA Nov 18, 2026 · v1

TL;DR
Proof of Stafford's conjecture, including Gabber's involutivity theorem and further generalizations, is fully formalized in Lean 4 with archived sources.
Abstract
We prove that over a Weyl algebra over a field of characteristic zero $A_n(k)$, every nonzero $d\in A_n(k)$ admits $F,R,S\in A_n(k)$ such that $1=dR+FdS$, a conjecture Stafford proposed in 1978. As a consequence, every finitely generated torsion right $A_n(k)$-module is cyclic. The proof maps $d$, by a symplectic change of variables, to a normal form $\tilde d$ and shows that the cyclic module $Q=A_n(k)/(\tilde dA_n(k)+x_1^N\tilde dA_n(k))$ vanishes. We first show that right multiplication by $x_1$ is surjective on $Q$. A comparison of lengths of localized modules over the associated graded ring then gives $\operatorname{gr}Q=x_1\operatorname{gr}Q$, so that the characteristic variety of $Q$ is disjoint from the hyperplane $\{x_1=0\}$. Finally, Gabber's involutivity theorem together with an argument about conormal directions at infinity from algebraic geometry shows that such a characteristic variety must be empty and therefore, $Q = 0$.
Problem

Stafford's 1978 conjecture asks whether every nonzero element d of the Weyl algebra A_n(k) over a characteristic-zero field admits F,R,S with 1=dR+FdS. A positive answer implies that every finitely generated torsion right A_n(k)-module is cyclic.

Approach

A symplectic linear change of variables sends d to a monic normal form, and the goal becomes showing that the cyclic module Q=A_n/(dA_n+x_1^N dA_n) vanishes. Right multiplication by x_1 is shown to be surjective on Q, and length comparisons of localized graded modules give gr Q = x_1 gr Q. Gabber's involutivity theorem and an algebraic-geometry argument about conormal directions at infinity then force the characteristic variety, and hence Q, to be empty. The whole argument is formalized in Lean 4, including Gabber's theorem, using only the standard axioms, and the main theorem is registered in Palomar.

Results

Stafford's conjecture is proved, with an explicit choice of F, so finitely generated torsion modules over A_n(k) are cyclic. Lean also proves extensions with no printed proof: a left-handed version, versions for differential operators on smooth affine domains, and a version for simple filtered algebras.
