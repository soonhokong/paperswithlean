TL;DR
Lean proofs of Hausdorff dimension lower bounds and positive-measure results for (n,k)-Besicovitch sets, including exact bounds on the constant p_c.
Abstract
Lean proofs of Hausdorff dimension at least n-(n-k)/p_c^k for all (n,k)-Besicovitch sets with 1<=k<=n, and positive Lebesgue measure for Lebesgue measurable such sets when p_c^(k-1)+k>n. Here a Besicovitch set contains a translated unit k-disk in every k-dimensional direction, and p_c is the unique root in (2,3) of p^3-2p^2-2p+2=0. Building on Bourgain's arithmetic-combinatorial approach, Nets Hawk Katz and Terence Tao's New bounds for Kakeya problems (2002; preprint 2001) established the Minkowski-dimension bound 1+(n-1)/beta_c, where beta_c=p_c/(p_c-1)=1.67513... satisfies beta_c^3-4*beta_c+2=0. This is exactly n-(n-1)/p_c, so the numerical exponent itself is not new. They also proved the distinct Hausdorff bound (2-sqrt(2))*(n-4)+3. A Minkowski lower bound alone does not imply the same Hausdorff lower bound: Hausdorff dimension is at most Minkowski dimension. Richard Oberlin's subsequent X-ray work (2006 preprint; published 2010) gave the Hausdorff bound n-(n-k)/(1+sqrt(2))^k and positive measure when (1+sqrt(2))^(k-1)+k>n. Later polynomial-partitioning work by Hickman, Rogers, and Zhang improved Kakeya bounds in many dimensions while retaining the large-dimension leading coefficient 2-sqrt(2). The claimed advance here is a continuous projection estimate with joint measurable parameter selection, developed from the supplied September 2026 manuscript. Its mixed-norm X-ray and plate maximal consequences, together with a countable-cover argument, give the Katz-Tao numerical bound for Hausdorff dimension and replace 1+sqrt(2)=2.41421... by p_c=2.48119... in Oberlin's displayed dimension and positive-measure ranges. For k=1 the resulting Hausdorff bound is approximately 0.596968*n+0.403032, with a larger leading coefficient than 2-sqrt(2)=0.585786.... This is an improvement in sufficiently high dimensions, not a claim to the best bound in every dimension. The formalized hypotheses require only unit disks, and the Hausdorff theorem requires neither measurability nor boundedness of the set. Both geometric theorems and the exact bounds 2.481<p_c<2.482 are proved with only the standard Lean axioms. The comparisons above identify the improvement over the cited results; independent mathematical review and confirmation of novelty against the full literature remain pending. Problem

Lower bounds on the Hausdorff dimension of (n,k)-Besicovitch sets. Katz-Tao gave the Minkowski bound n-(n-1)/p_c, and Oberlin gave the Hausdorff bound n-(n-k)/(1+sqrt(2))^k. A Minkowski lower bound does not imply the same Hausdorff lower bound.

Approach

A continuous projection estimate with joint measurable parameter selection is developed from a supplied manuscript. It yields mixed-norm X-ray and plate maximal estimates. A countable-cover argument then transfers these to Hausdorff dimension. Everything is formalized in Lean using only the standard axioms.

Results

Hausdorff dimension at least n-(n-k)/p_c^k for all (n,k)-Besicovitch sets, and positive measure for measurable ones when p_c^(k-1)+k>n; this replaces 1+sqrt(2) with p_c≈2.4812. The bounds 2.481<p_c<2.482 are also proved. Independent mathematical review, novelty confirmation, and final sandboxed and independent-kernel verification are pending.
