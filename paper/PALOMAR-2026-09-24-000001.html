The Favard-length decay exponent of the four-corner Cantor set is at least 1/4

TL;DR
Proves in Lean that the Favard-length decay exponent of the four-corner Cantor set is at least 1/4, with the classical upper bound 1.
Abstract
For the four-corner Cantor set approximants K_n = C_n × C_n, where C_n is the union of the 2^n intervals of length 4^{-n} with left endpoints sum_{j<=n} w_j 4^{-j}, w_j in {0,3}, let α_Fav be the supremum of the a >= 0 for which the Favard length Fav(K_n) = (1/π) ∫_0^π |π_θ(K_n)| dθ is at most C n^{-a} for all n >= 1. This project proves in Lean that every a < 1/4 is admissible, so 1/4 <= α_Fav, together with the classical bound α_Fav <= 1. The previous best lower bounds were 1/6 (Nazarov-Peres-Volberg, 2010) and 1/5 (Marshall, arXiv:2509.02882 v2, 2026, whose v1 claim of 1/4 was withdrawn). The new ingredient is a joint negative-moment estimate for the low-frequency cosine product. The argument comes from an unpublished AI-assisted manuscript, and this formalization is its first complete proof.

Problem

The Favard length of the four-corner Cantor set approximants K_n decays like n^{-a}, and the optimal exponent α_Fav is unknown. The previous best lower bounds were 1/6 (Nazarov-Peres-Volberg) and 1/5 (Marshall), after a claimed 1/4 was withdrawn.

Approach

The argument introduces a joint negative-moment estimate for the low-frequency cosine product appearing in the Fourier analysis of the projections. It comes from an unpublished AI-assisted manuscript and was formalized completely in Lean. The classical upper bound α_Fav <= 1 is proved alongside it.

Results

Every a < 1/4 is shown to be admissible, giving 1/4 <= α_Fav <= 1 with machine-checked proofs. The formalization is the first complete proof of the argument.
