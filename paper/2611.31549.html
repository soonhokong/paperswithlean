A Hereditarily Atomic Domain without ACCP

Matthew Toeniskoetter

math.AC Nov 23, 2026 · v1
formalization algebra
Read PDF arXiv abstract Code
TL;DR
The main theorems (a hereditarily atomic domain failing ACCP) and several variations are formalized in a companion Lean 4 library on GitHub.
Abstract
An integral domain for which every subring is atomic is said to be hereditarily atomic. Every hereditarily atomic cancellative commutative monoid satisfies the ascending chain condition on principal ideals (ACCP), but it remained an open question whether every hereditarily atomic domain satisfies ACCP. We construct a hereditarily atomic domain which does not satisfy ACCP, answering this question in the negative. This domain is an overring of the ring of integer-valued polynomials $Int(\mathbb{Z})$, and its subrings that fail ACCP are precisely those of finite additive index.
Problem

An open question asked whether every hereditarily atomic integral domain, meaning one whose every subring is atomic, satisfies the ascending chain condition on principal ideals (ACCP). The analogous statement was already known for cancellative commutative monoids.

Approach

The authors build an overring D of Int(Z) inside Q(t). They impose p-adic integrality at two transcendental points over each prime and allow poles at carefully constructed irreducible polynomials F_i, chosen via congruences and Eisenstein's criterion. Prescribed valuation profiles produce an infinite strictly ascending chain of principal ideals. Atomicity of every subring is proved through valuation-based divisibility tests. The main theorems and variations are formalized in Lean 4.

Results

D is hereditarily atomic but fails ACCP, which answers the question negatively in characteristic zero. The subrings failing ACCP are exactly those of finite additive index, Z + nD. D is almost Dedekind, and its ideal theory is described.
