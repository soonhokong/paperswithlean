Exact Compilation and Resource Laws for Hidden Integer Feedback

Ganxuan Yang, Siqi Wen, Shuyan Yu, Jidong Yuan

cs.CR Oct 19, 2026 · v2
verification formalization mathlib cryptography
Read PDF arXiv abstract
TL;DR
Verifies the discrete-Gaussian kernel, floor arithmetic, hidden-rounding mixture, transcript semantics, and first-order privacy analysis in Lean 4 with pinned Mathlib.
Abstract
Hidden rounding carries dependencies through integer memory. We study finite-memory feedback with predictable inputs that differ by one on at most $C$ releases and with every output public. A shared preparation constrains future branches until a sequence of common-input releases synchronizes the conditional registers. Preserving those constraints yields exact finite-law compilation, polynomial in support width for fixed memory and participation budgets, with constructive discrete-Gaussian transfer and calibration. A commitment hierarchy quantifies the loss from releasing dependencies early and becomes exact at depth $mC$ for memory $m$. Strict examples include three levels with one participation and a discrete-Gaussian calibration gap exceeding 0.23. Charging actual preparation time gives a privacy resource law with a square-root remainder and a leading coefficient computable in polynomial bit cost at fixed parameters. Lean refinements cover the first-order privacy analysis and binary value algorithm; separate lemmas check finite-memory algebra and paths. Certificates establish multi-memory calibration and resource effects; workload comparisons include a finite-memory prefix-error lower bound.
Problem

Hidden randomized rounding in integer feedback mechanisms carries dependencies across successive private releases. Standard privacy analyses can lose these dependencies, so the exact privacy loss under bounded signed-unit participation and fully public outputs is hard to compute.

Approach

Preparations are retained until a common-input echo synchronizes the conditional states, which gives an exact finite-law compiler with support exponent 3C-2. A commitment hierarchy releases dependencies earlier and quantifies the resulting calibration loss. A KL-rate analysis under physical-time constraints gives a resource frontier. The kernel and transcript semantics, the first-order privacy analysis and the binary value algorithm are verified in Lean 4.19.0 with Mathlib, alongside independently checked finite certificates.

Results

The compiler returns tight exact intervals for the privacy profile, e.g. Delta_3*(2) is determined to within 2.4e-14. It runs 3.46x faster than root enumeration at B=97 and 25.4x faster at B=257 (single trial). Strict hierarchy gaps are shown, and the participation rate satisfies epsilon_C*(delta) = C a_* + O(sqrt C).
