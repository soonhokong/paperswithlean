Referential Regimes: Transformation-Based Identity Profiles for Neutral Substrates

Denise M. Case

TL;DR

Abstract
Data systems operating under persistent legal, institutional, and analytic disagreement require shared reference without presupposing agreement about interpretation. This paper investigates identity preservation under transformation, building on the conditional neutrality result of Neutral Substrates (arXiv:2601.14271). We pair candidate identity bases with families of record transformations and classify each family as preserving, breaking, neutral, or inapplicable for a basis. Using invariant-defined identity bases and explicit effect models, we derive and certify six classification cells in Lean 4 for scope decomposition, rule refinement, and forking. The derivations show how classifications depend on field granularity, admissible transformation steps, and explicit continuity warrants. We also characterize the identity relations generated by invariant-preserving steps. Two bases generate equal relations exactly when they agree on the preservation of every admissible step in a shared comparison domain, and a single separating step witnesses a difference. The formalization makes identity claims auditable, provides testable conditions for systems that transform records, and exposes the assumptions behind each classification. The results are conditional on the declared bases, models, and warrants, whose adequacy for particular referents requires independent justification.
Problem

Data systems that operate under persistent interpretive disagreement need shared reference without assuming agreement on interpretation. It is unclear how declared identity bases behave when records are transformed by operations such as forking, refinement, or decomposition.

Approach

Candidate identity bases for reference kinds (obligation-bearers, rules, occurrences, scopes, records, plain referents) are paired with families of record transformations. Each pairing is classified as preserving, breaking, neutral, or inapplicable. Invariant-defined bases and explicit effect models are formalized in a companion Lean 4 development, which derives a classification matrix and certifies selected cells. Identity relations generated by invariant-preserving steps are also characterized.

Results

Six cells are certified in Lean 4: SCOPE-E/S under aggregation-decomposition, RULE-C/S under refinement, and LOC/OBJ under branch/fork with continuity warrants. The Lean development also proves that the supporting fields are minimal. Two bases generate equal relations exactly when they agree on the preservation of every admissible step in a shared comparison domain, and a single separating step witnesses a difference.
