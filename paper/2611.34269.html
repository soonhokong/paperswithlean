Degree-respecting labellings with ordered neighbour sums

Toshio Ishikawa, Tomoki Uda

math.CO Nov 25, 2026 · v1
formalization combinatorics
Read PDF arXiv abstract Code
TL;DR
The proofs of the main theorem and corollary on degree-respecting graph labellings are checked in Lean 4.
Abstract
In an exhaustive enumeration of hydrocarbons with only single bonds, carbon skeletons are generated as adjacency matrices and pruned by two rules: a vertex of larger degree receives a smaller label, and among vertices of equal degree, a vertex whose adjacency row is the larger binary integer receives a smaller label. The pruning is sound only if every skeleton admits a labelling obeying both rules. We prove that such a labelling exists for every finite simple graph, connected or not and with no bound on the degrees, and that the same holds when the weights $2^i$ of the labels $i$ are replaced by any strictly increasing real weights. The proof takes, among the labellings that order vertices by non-increasing degree, one minimising the sum over edges of the products of the endpoint weights.
Problem

An exhaustive enumeration of saturated hydrocarbons prunes candidate adjacency matrices by two rules. Vertices of larger degree get smaller labels, and among vertices of equal degree, a larger row integer gets a smaller label. The pruning is sound only if every carbon skeleton admits a labelling satisfying both rules.

Approach

The authors prove a general theorem: every finite simple graph admits such a labelling, and the result holds for any strictly increasing real weights in place of 2^i. Among labellings that order vertices by non-increasing degree, they choose one minimising an edge potential, the sum over edges of products of endpoint weights. A swap argument on equal-degree vertices then yields the required neighbour-sum inequality. The proofs are checked in Lean 4.

Results

The labelling exists for all finite simple graphs, connected or not, with no degree bound, so the enumeration's pruning is sound. The paper also discusses non-uniqueness and counts the matrices remaining after each rule for skeletons with 2 to 7 carbons.
