Exit-Pair Rigidity, Neutral Restart Laws, and Inverse Reconstruction in One Dimension

Tony Newton, Juan Antonio Vega Coso

math.PR Nov 20, 2026 · v1 math.SP
formalization mathlib probability
Read PDF arXiv abstract Code
TL;DR
Selected results on the exit-pair renewal identities and the discrete analogue are formalized in a Lean 4/Mathlib project (LeanPaperB1), archived on Zenodo.
Abstract
For an absorbed one-dimensional diffusion, we characterize two-point restart laws preserving the exit-side probability for every independent renewal clock. Spectral identities establish rigidity under spatial accumulation, while explicit nonsymmetric potentials admit neutral pairs. We distinguish midpoint Dirichlet isospectrality from Weyl reflection rigidity and prove a second-order high-energy asymptotic under local continuity, giving two necessary asymmetry conditions for neutral pairs. Two complete exit profiles at distinct reset rates identify diffusivity and drift; a finite birth-death analogue admits exact Jacobi criteria and reconstruction from four channel functions.
Problem

For an absorbed one-dimensional diffusion, the paper asks which two-point restart laws keep the exit-side probability unchanged under every independent renewal clock. It also asks what the joint law of exit time and exit side (the exit pair) determines about the diffusion's coefficients.

Approach

Spectral identities for Dirichlet characteristic functions and high-energy asymptotics of the exit channels are used to derive rigidity results. Explicit nonsymmetric potentials are constructed that admit neutral pairs, and midpoint Dirichlet isospectrality is distinguished from Weyl rigidity. A Schwarzian-derivative identity gives two-rate reconstruction of diffusivity and drift, and a finite birth-death analogue is treated with Jacobi criteria. Selected results are formalized in Lean 4/Mathlib, and C++ programs provide numerical experiments.

Results

Neutrality is rigid under spatial accumulation, but individual neutral pairs exist for nonsymmetric potentials, and a second-order asymptotic gives two necessary asymmetry conditions for such pairs. Two complete exit profiles at distinct reset rates identify diffusivity and drift. Numerically, the direct reconstruction formula is highly sensitive to noise, while a finite-dimensional least-squares fit tolerates about 100 times more noise.
