The rank of $3\times 3$ matrix multiplication over $\mathbb{F}_2$ is 23

Tejasvi Singh Tomar

TL;DR
Search certificates proving the rank bound are checked in Lean 4 by checkers with Lean-proved soundness, building on Mathlib and the Rudich–Rousseau formalization.
Abstract
The rank of the tensor of $3\times 3$ matrix multiplication over the field with two elements is at most $23$ by Laderman's algorithm, and Rudich and Rousseau recently proved that it is at least $22$. We prove that it equals $23$. Hence Laderman's algorithm uses the fewest multiplications among all bilinear algorithms over $\mathbb{F}_2$ and among all bilinear algorithms with integer coefficients. The proof uses the substitution method in the form developed by D'Ambrosio, Wang and Yang et al., which bounds the number of first factors of a decomposition that lie in a given subspace of the space of first factors. We sharpen these bounds for $111$ of Wang's $496$ classes of subspaces, constrain the ranks of the first factors through a $27\times 27$ flattening of the tensor, and list, up to symmetry, all sets of $22$ first factors that satisfy these constraints. An exact completion search shows that none of them is the set of first factors of a decomposition. The computation emits certificates, which are checked in the Lean 4 proof assistant. In one formal proof the largest checks are evaluated as compiled code, and in a second one the kernel evaluates every check, so that its final theorem depends only on the three standard axioms of Lean. The Lean proofs build on the formalization of Rudich and Rousseau and import their table of bounds and their reduction for block algorithms.
Problem

Laderman's algorithm gives rank at most 23 for the 3x3 matrix multiplication tensor over F_2, and Rudich and Rousseau proved the rank is at least 22. The open question was whether 22 multiplications suffice.

Approach

The substitution method of D'Ambrosio, Wang and Yang et al. is sharpened for 111 of Wang's 496 subspace classes. Ranks of first factors are constrained through a 27x27 split flattening, and all admissible sets of 22 first factors are enumerated up to symmetry. An exhaustive completion search then refutes each set. The searches emit certificates, which are verified in Lean 4 by checkers whose soundness is proved in Lean. One formal proof evaluates the largest checks as compiled code; a second has the kernel evaluate every check, so it relies only on Lean's three standard axioms.

Results

The rank of 3x3 matrix multiplication over F_2 is exactly 23, and this equality is a theorem in Lean using Mathlib's ZMod 2. Consequently any bilinear algorithm over F_2, or with integer coefficients, for 3x3 matrix multiplication needs at least 23 multiplications.
